### 1.2 Immediate Pericope and Syntactic Observations
- **Pericope Setting (2 Kings 2:15):** Four verses prior, the sons of the prophets "who were at Jericho" observe that the spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha, establishing Jericho as the immediate geographic and prophetic-succession context.
- **Idiomatic Evaluation (2 Kings 2:19):** Clause analysis identifies the deferential idiom `אֲדֹנִי רֹאֶה` (`H7200 + H0113`), coupled with the qualitative polarity contrast `טוֹב` (good, token 10) vs. `רָעִים` (bad, token 15).
- **Intra-Passage Sound and Root Patterns (2 Kings 2:21–22):** Surface consonantal scan identifies `יְהוָה` (`H3068`) ↔ `יִהְיֶה` (`H1961`, shared consonants `יהה`, `2:21`), `לַמַּיִם` (`H4325`) ↔ `מָוֶת` (`H4194`, edit distance 1, `2:21`), `עוֹד` (`H5750`, `2:21`) ↔ `עַד` (`H5704`, `2:22`, shared `עד`), and cognate figure `כִּדְבַר` (`H1697`) ↔ `דִּבֵּר` (`H1696`, `2:22`). These are surface phonetic/cognate cues, not independent historical etymologies.

## 2. Unexpected Lexical and Morphological Discoveries

### 2.1 The Hapax Bowl (`צְלֹחִית`, `H6746`), Its Root (`צָלַח`, `H6743`), and Its 2 Kings Bookend (`צַלַּחַת`, `H6747`)
1. **Unique Occurrence & Derivation:** `צְלֹחִית` (`H6746`, token position 4 in 2 Kings 2:20) is a biblical **hapax legomenon** (`total_verses: 1`). Documented lexical formation links it to the root **`צָלַח` (`H6743`, "to rush / break out / prosper")** (`p_null = 3.8e-7`, 21.33 bits).
2. **Internal 2 Kings Bookend (`צַלַּחַת`, `H6747`):** The cognate dish/bowl noun `צַלַּחַת` (`H6747`, 3 verses total; 2 surfaced: 2 Kings 21:13 and Proverbs 26:15) creates an unexpected structural bookend across 2 Kings:
   - **2 Kings 2:20 (start of Elisha’s ministry):** A *new jar* (`צְלֹחִית חֲדָשָׁה`) holds salt to *heal* Jericho’s waters and end child-bereavement (`מְשַׁכָּלֶת`).
   - **2 Kings 21:13 (Manasseh’s judgment):** YHWH declares of Jerusalem, `"I will wipe Jerusalem as a man wipes a dish [הַצַּלַּחַת], wiping it and turning it upside down"` — the vessel of blessing inverted into a wiped, overturned dish of exile.
3. **Greek Vessel Bridge (`ὑδρίσκη` / `ὑδρία`, `G5201`):** While Rahlfs 1935 renders `צְלֹחִית` as `ὑδρίσκην`, the LXX lexicon index maps `H6746` to `G5201` **`ὑδρία`** (water-jar). In the entire NA28 New Testament, `G5201` occurs in **only 3 verses, all in the Gospel of John**:
   - **John 2:6–7:** Six stone water jars (`λίθιναι ὑδρίαι ἕξ`) set for Jewish purification are filled with water (`ὕδατος`) and transformed into wine.
   - **John 4:28:** The Samaritan woman leaves her water jar (`ὑδρίαν`) at the well to announce Jesus to her city (`πόλιν`).
   *(Distinction: This is a cross-testament Greek lexical-motif correspondence rather than a proven direct literary quotation of 2 Kings 2:20.)*

### 2.2 The Dual Semantics of `מוֹצָא` (`H4161`: "Spring of Water" and "Utterance of Lips")
Syntactic construct analysis of `מוֹצָא` (`H4161`, derived from `יָצָא` `H3318`, "to go out", 26 verses total) documents **two high-significance construct partners**:
1. **`מוֹצָא` + `מַיִם` (`H4325`, water — 6 occurrences total, `p_null = 1.55e-6`, 19.30 bits):**
   - **2 Kings 2:21** (`מוֹצָא הַמַּיִם`, Jericho’s spring).
   - **2 Chronicles 32:30** (`מוֹצָא מֵימֵי גִיחוֹן הָעֶלְיוֹן`, Hezekiah stops the upper spring of Gihon and channels it to David’s city — followed immediately in the same verse by `וַיַּצְלַח`, root `צלח`).
   - **Psalm 107:33 & 107:35** (`וּמֹצָאֵי מַיִם` turned to thirsty ground, and dry land turned `לְמֹצָאֵי מָיִם`).
   - **Isaiah 41:18** (`לְמוֹצָאֵי מָיִם`, dry land turned into springs of water).
   - **Isaiah 58:11** (`וּכְמוֹצָא מַיִם אֲשֶׁר לֹא יְכַזְּבוּ מֵימָיו`, "like a spring of water whose waters do not fail").
2. **`מוֹצָא` + `שָׂפָה` (`H8193`, lips — 4 occurrences, `p_null = 2.91e-6`, 18.39 bits):** e.g., **Numbers 30:12**, **Deuteronomy 23:23**, and **Jeremiah 17:16** (`מוֹצָא שְׂפָתַי`, "that which came out of my lips"), alongside **Deuteronomy 8:3** (`מוֹצָא פִי יְהוָה`, what proceeds from YHWH’s mouth).
- **Structural Synthesis:** `2 Kings 2:21–22` pairs the healing of the **`מוֹצָא הַמַּיִם`** (water-outlet) with the efficacy of **`דְּבַר אֱלִישָׁע אֲשֶׁר דִּבֵּר`** (prophetic speech). This dual lexical field (*spring-source* / *mouth-utterance*) directly anticipates **James 3:11–12**, where human speech from the mouth is tested against whether a single spring (`πηγή`) can produce sweet and salt water.

### 2.3 Salt’s Lexical Siblings (`מֶלַח` `H4417`, `מָלַח` `H4414`, `מְלֵחָה` `H4420`) and Homograph Controls
- **Primary Noun (`מֶלַח`, `H4417`, 24 verses):** Rendered in LXX primarily by `ἅλς` (`G0251`, 20×), `ἁλυκός` (`G0252`, 5×), and `ἅλας` (`G0217`, 3×).
- **Verbal Relative (`מָלַח`, `H4414`, 4 verses):**
  1. **Leviticus 2:13:** `"Every grain offering you shall season with salt [בַּמֶּלַח תִּמְלָח]... salt of the covenant of your God."`
  2. **Ezekiel 16:4:** Newborn Jerusalem was neither washed in water nor **`rubbed with salt [וְהָמְלֵחַ לֹא הֻמְלַחַתְּ]`** — an unexpected **neonatal ritual** linking *water + salt + newborn survival* that directly illuminates Elisha’s pairing of salt, water, and the cessation of miscarriage/child-loss (`מְשַׁכָּלֶת`).
  3. **Isaiah 51:6:** Contrasting homograph sense ("dissipate/vanish like smoke," `נִמְלָחוּ`).
  4. **Book 2 (Exodus) occurrence** (1 verse in book distribution, distinct from `H4417`).
- **Derivative Noun (`מְלֵחָה`, `H4420`, "salt-waste", 3 verses):** **Job 39:6** (wild donkey’s dwelling), **Psalm 107:34** (fruitful land turned `לִמְלֵחָה` because of the wickedness `מֵרָעַת` of its inhabitants — framed symmetrically between `מֹצָאֵי מַיִם` in vv. 33 and 35!), and **Jeremiah 17:6** (the cursed person "will not see when good comes [`וְלֹא יִרְאֶה כִּי יָבוֹא טוֹב`]... inhabiting an uninhabited salt land [`אֶרֶץ מְלֵחָה`]," lexically inverting 2 Kings 2:19’s `טוֹב ... רֹאֶה` and located in the very chapter where Jeremiah cites `מוֹצָא שְׂפָתַי` in 17:16).
- **Proper-Name & Aramaic Homograph Controls:** `עִיר הַמֶּלַח` ("City of Salt," `H5898`, Joshua 15:62, paired with En Gedi!) and Aramaic `מְלַח` (`H4415`/`H4416`) are kept distinct from common mineral salt.

## 3. Physical Properties and the Hydrological Paradox

From sense-gated real-world ontology (`H4417`):
- **Mineral Identity & Function:** Halite salt (`domain: minerals`); `preserves_and_seasons: true`; OEWN hypernym `flavorer / flavoring`; symbolic covenant emblem: `incorruptibility`.
- **The Paradox (Physical Analogy vs. Prophetic Sign):**
  - Physically and across OT curse texts, **salt induces sterility and ruins soil/water**: **Judges 9:45** (Abimelech sows Shechem with salt `מֶלַח`); **Deuteronomy 29:23 [MT 29:22]** ("sulfur, salt, and burning... not sown, doesn’t produce," like Sodom and Gomorrah); **Zephaniah 2:9** ("salt pits and a perpetual desolation"); **Genesis 19:26** (Lot’s wife); **Psalm 107:34** & **Jeremiah 17:6** (`מְלֵחָה`); and **James 3:12** (`οὔτε ἁλυκὸν γλυκὺ ποιῆσαι ὕδωρ`, a salt spring cannot physically yield fresh/sweet water).
  - Geographically, Jericho sits just north of the **Salt Sea (`יָם הַמֶּלַח`)**, where Jordan’s waters end (**Joshua 3:16**; **Joshua 18:19**).
  - **Theological Inversion:** Salt preserves food (Job 6:6) and seals covenants (Lev 2:13; Num 18:19; 2 Chr 13:5), yet ruins drinking water. Throwing salt into a spring to *heal* it and *end* barrenness/death (`מָוֶת וּמְשַׁכָּלֶת`) therefore functions as a **counter-physical prophetic sign**, explicitly grounded by the text not in halite chemistry but in YHWH’s word (`כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה רִפִּאתִי לַמַּיִם הָאֵלֶּה ... כִּדְבַר אֱלִישָׁע`).

## 4. Intermediate Canonical Bridges

### 4.1 Torah Antecedents: Marah (`Exodus 15:23–26`) and Covenant Fertility (`Exodus 23:26`)
1. **Marah (`Exodus 15:23–26`):** `intersect_words(H7993, H4325)` finds **only 8 verses** in the Hebrew Bible combining `שָׁלַךְ` (throw) and `מַיִם` (waters), placing **Exodus 15:25** and **2 Kings 2:21** in an exclusive lexical set:
   - Unusable waters (`מָרִים` bitter, LXX `πικρός`, Ex 15:23 vs. `רָעִים` bad, LXX `πονηρά`, 2 Kgs 2:19).
   - Mediated throwing (`וַיַּשְׁלֵךְ` `H7993`): Moses throws wood (`עֵץ`, LXX `ἐνέβαλεν ... ξύλον`, Ex 15:25); Elisha throws salt (`מֶלַח`, LXX `ἔρριψεν ... ἅλα`, 2 Kgs 2:21).
   - Waters transformed (`וַיִּמְתְּקוּ`, LXX **`ἐγλυκάνθη`** from `γλυκαίνω`, Ex 15:25 vs. `וַיֵּרָפוּ`, LXX `ἰάθησαν`, 2 Kgs 2:22).
   - Divine self-revelation as Healer: **`אֲנִי יְהוָה רֹפְאֶךָ` (`H7495`, LXX `κύριος ὁ ἰώμενός σε`, Ex 15:26)** ↔ **`כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה רִפִּאתִי לַמַּיִם הָאֵלֶּה` (`H7495`, LXX `ἴαμαι`, 2 Kgs 2:21)**.
   - *Negative Control:* Clause-sequence alignment (`sequence_parallels`) between 2 Kings 2:19 and Exodus 15:22–26 gives `p_null = 0.6462` (`evidence_bits = 0`), showing that the connection is **lexical-thematic and action-structural**, not a verbatim syntactic template copy.
2. **Book of the Covenant (`Exodus 23:26`):** Among the 21 verses containing `שָׁכֹל` (`H7921`), Exodus 23:26 promises covenant obedience will ensure `"no one will miscarry [מְשַׁכֵּלָה] or be barren in your land."` Elisha’s miracle restores this Torah covenant state to Jericho.
3. **Covenant of Salt (`Leviticus 2:13`; `Numbers 18:19`; `2 Chronicles 13:5`):** Salt embodies permanent, incorruptible covenant fidelity (`בְּרִית מֶלַח עוֹלָם`), matching the permanence of Jericho’s healing (`עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה`, 2 Kgs 2:22).

### 4.2 The Pivotal Prophetic Bridge: `Ezekiel 43:24` → `Ezekiel 36:12–14` → `Ezekiel 47:8–12`
Single-hop graph traversal (`follow_connectionchain`) and multi-lemma intersections reveal **Ezekiel’s temple vision** as the decisive canonical bridge between Elisha’s Jericho and the New Creation:

1. **`Ezekiel 43:24` — The Altar Consecration Link (`H4417` as Direct Object of `H7993`):**
   - Syntactic tree verification proves that across the **entire Hebrew Bible**, `מֶלַח` (`H4417`) serves as the direct grammatical object of `שָׁלַךְ` (`H7993`, to throw) **only twice** (`p_null = 1.16e-7` in word-level dependency; `3.28e-5` in chain traversal):
     - **2 Kings 2:21:** `וַיַּשְׁלֶךְ שָׁם מֶלַח` (Elisha throws salt into the spring).
     - **Ezekiel 43:24:** `וְהִשְׁלִיכוּ הַכֹּהֲנִים עֲלֵיהֶם מֶלַח` ("and the priests shall throw salt upon them [the burnt offerings]") to consecrate the restored temple altar!
2. **`Ezekiel 36:12–14` — Reversal of Land Bereavement (`H7921`):**
   - Following earlier judgment (`Ezekiel 5:17; 14:15` where wild beasts bereave `שִׁכֵּל` the land), YHWH pledges to Israel’s mountains: `"you will never again bereave them of their children [לֹא תוֹסִף עוֹד לְשַׁכְּלָם]... neither bereave your nation any more [לֹא תְשַׁכְּלִי עוֹד]"` (cf. **Malachi 3:11**, `לֹא תְשַׁכֵּל לָכֶם הַגֶּפֶן`, and **Hosea 9:12, 14**).
3. **`Ezekiel 47:8–12` — Sanctuary River Healing the Salt Sea:**
   - **Exclusive Intersections:**
     - `מַיִם` (`H4325`) + `רָפָא` (`H7495`, "waters + heal") occurs in **only 4 verses in the OT**: **2 Kings 2:21**, **2 Kings 2:22**, **Jeremiah 15:18** (incurable wound / deceitful waters, near Jer 15:7 `שִׁכַּלְתִּי`), and **Ezekiel 47:9**!
     - `מֶלַח` (`H4417`) + `רָפָא` (`H7495`, "salt + heal") occurs in **only 2 verses in the OT**: **2 Kings 2:21** and **Ezekiel 47:11**!
   - **Shared Demonstrative & Movement Vocabulary:** Both **2 Kings 2:21** and **Ezekiel 47:8–9** use `יָצָא` (`H3318`, going/flowing out) and the exact demonstrative phrase **"these waters"** (`לַמַּיִם הָאֵלֶּה` / `הַמַּיִם הָאֵלֶּה`). Ezekiel 47:10 names **En Gedi (`H5872`)** — paired in Joshua 15:62 with the **City of Salt (`עִיר הַמֶּלַח`)**.
   - **The Dialectic Reversal (47:11):** In 2 Kings 2:21, *salt heals the waters*; in Ezekiel 47:8–11, *waters flowing out from the salt-consecrated altar (43:24)* heal the Salt Sea (`וְנִרְפְּאוּ` / `וְיֵרָפְאוּ`), **except** its swamps and marshes `"will not be healed; they will be given up to salt"` (`וְלֹא יֵרָפְאוּ לְמֶלַח נִתָּנוּ`) — preserving salt as an enduring witness/boundary beside healed life.
   - **LXX Shift in Ezekiel 47:8–12:** Whereas 2 Kings 2:21–22 (and Exod 15:26) LXX uses `ἰάομαι` for `רָפָא`, Ezekiel 47:8, 9, 11 LXX shifts `רָפָא` to **`ὑγιάζω`** (`p_g_given_h = 0.0758`, `g_idf = 11.58`), translates the hapax **`תְּרוּפָה` (`H8644`, healing, 47:12)** as **`ὑγίεια`**, and adds the LXX expansion **`καινότητος`** ("of newness", 47:12).

## 5. Canonical Macro-Patterns (OT → NT)

### 5.1 From Healed Spring and Temple River to New Creation (`Ezekiel 47:8–12` → `Revelation 22:1–2`)
- Tri-textual analysis (`evidence_bits = 16`) confirms **Revelation 22:1–2** as a composite NT splice drawing on **Ezekiel 47:1, 8, 12**, **Genesis 2:9–10; 3:22**, **Psalm 1:3**, **Zechariah 14:8**, and **Joel 3:18**:
  - Ezekiel 47:12’s sanctuary river, monthly fruit trees, and leaves `לִתְרוּפָה` (`H8644`, hapax) culminate in Revelation 22:2’s river from the throne of God and the Lamb and leaves **`εἰς θεραπείαν τῶν ἐθνῶν`** (`G2322`, "for the healing of the nations").
  - The trajectory moves across four canonical stages: **Marah** (Exod 15:23–26: wood thrown into bitter water → YHWH heals Israel) → **Jericho** (2 Kgs 2:19–22: salt in a new jar thrown into the spring → death & barrenness ended) → **Zion/Temple** (Ezek 43:24; 47:8–12: salt thrown on altar → river heals the Salt Sea, leaves for healing) → **New Jerusalem** (Rev 22:1–2: crystal river + tree of life for healing of all nations).

### 5.2 From Spring/Mouth (`מוֹצָא`) and Sweet/Salt Water to NT Ethics of Speech and Community
- **James 3:11–12 (`tri_textual_diff`, `evidence_bits = 16`, editorial xref `p_null = 0.001`):**
  - Tri-textual alignment links James 3:11–12 to **Isaiah 5:20** (`τὸ γλυκὺ καὶ τὸ πικρόν`), **Deuteronomy 8:8**, and **2 Kings 2:20–21** (with **Exodus 15:23, 25** and **Ezekiel 47:8–9** among top candidate sources).
  - James 3:12 uses the NT hapax **`ἁλυκός` (`G0252`)** — the exact LXX adjective for Hebrew `מֶלַח` (5×) — paired with **`γλυκύ`** (same root as Exodus 15:25 LXX `ἐγλυκάνθη`!): `"neither can a salt [spring] produce sweet/fresh water."` What is physically impossible in nature (James 3:12) is precisely what God accomplished in Jericho (2 Kgs 2:21) and what the tongue must embody (James 3:9–12).
- **Synoptic & Pauline Salt Theology (`G0217` `ἅλας`, 4 verses; `G0233` `ἁλίζω`, 2 verses):**
  - **Matthew 5:13** (disciples as salt; unsalty salt thrown out `βληθὲν ἔξω`), **Mark 9:49–50** (NA28: `"For everyone will be salted with fire [πυρὶ ἁλισθήσεται]... Have salt in yourselves and be at peace with one another"` — uniting sacrificial salt, purification, and communal peace), **Luke 14:34**, and **Colossians 4:6** (`ὁ λόγος ὑμῶν ... ἅλατι ἠρτυμένος`, speech seasoned with salt).
