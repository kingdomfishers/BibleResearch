# Lexical Wordplay, Proper-Name Plays, and Sonic/Semantic Contrasts in Micah 1:10–15 and Philemon 1:8–21

## Epistemic Framework & Methodology

This study evaluates lexical wordplay, onomastic (proper-name) plays, and sonic or semantic contrasts in **Micah 1:10–15** and **Philemon 1:8–21** using primary original-language tokens (Hebrew Masoretic Text and Greek New Testament), Septuagint (LXX) alignment data, documented root/lexical records, and computational wordplay/consonantal scans. Throughout the analysis, five evidentiary layers are kept strictly separate:

1. **Attested Textual Evidence**: Exact Hebrew/Greek surface forms, Strong's numbers, lemmas, 1-based token positions, and LXX Rahlfs concordance alignments.
2. **Documented Lexical / Etymological Relationships**: Shared morphological roots and lexical-derivation anchors attested in standard lexica.
3. **Deliberate Rhetorical & Literary Paronomasia**: Contextually anchored sound-plays, syntactic talion/polyptoton, and antonymic or synonymous name plays (even where historical roots differ).
4. **Surface Consonantal / Phonetic Resemblance (Negative Controls)**: Coincidental letter/sound overlaps across unrelated roots that do not establish etymology or intentional wordplay.
5. **Interpretive Hypotheses**: Theological or rhetorical syntheses inferred from the verified textual patterns.

### 2. Town-by-Town Verification: Established Root vs. Paronomasia vs. LXX Witness

#### A. Established Root / Lexical-Derivation Wordplays

1. **Beth-le-aphrah (`H1036`, בֵּית לְעַפְרָה) ↔ Dust (`H6083`, עָפָר) — Micah 1:10**
   - *Attested & Lexical Evidence*: `get_Word` confirms documented root anchors for `H1036` as `בֵּית` (`H1004`, *p_null* = 6.23e-9) and `עָפָר` (`H6083`, *p_null* = 1.4e-8). `wordplay_scan` scores the onomastic root match at 0.95, flags `עפר` consonantal overlap (score 0.867), and identifies clause polyptoton (`R:c->Np`, score 0.86) alongside the adjacent syntactic talion `בָּכוֹ אַל־תִּבְכּוּ` (`H1058`, infinitive absolute + jussive, score 0.86).
   - *LXX Witness*: `lxx_concordance` shows the Greek translator rendered the place name semantically rather than transliterating it: `ἐξ οἴκου ... κατὰ γέλωτα` (`γέλως`, "laughter/derision"), alongside `γῆ` (`H6083`) and `καταπάσσω` (`H6428`, "sprinkle/scatter [upon oneself]").
   - *Assessment*: **Verified shared-root etymological wordplay** ("In House-of-Dust, in dust roll yourself!"). Also echoes `בֵּית` (`H1004`) from Micah 1:5 (`wordplay_scan` score 0.90).

2. **Shaphir (`H8208`, שָׁפִיר) ↔ Nakedness & Shame (`H6181`, עֶרְיָה; `H1322`, בֹשֶׁת) — Micah 1:11**
   - *Attested & Lexical Evidence*: `get_Word` documents `H8208` as derived from root `שָׁפַר` (`H8231`, "to polish / be fair", *p_null* = 7.45e-15; cognate `שַׁפִּיר` `H8209`, "fair", *p_null* = 2.82e-14). `wordplay_scan` flags `שָׁפִיר` (`H8208`) ↔ `בֹשֶׁת` (`H1322`) as an **etymological antonym** (score 0.92).
   - *LXX Witness*: LXX translates `שָׁפִיר` adverbially/semantically as `καλῶς` ("fairly/well", stability 0.603), while `עִבְרִי` (`H5674`) and `בֹשֶׁת` (`H1322`) are absent (`mt_minus`), and `עֶרְיָה` (`H6181`) maps via backoff to `πόλις` ("dwelling fairly her cities").
   - *Assessment*: **Verified root-based semantic inversion** ("Fair-Town, pass into exile in nakedness and shame").

3. **Maroth (`H4796`, מָרוֹת) ↔ Good (`H2896`, טוֹב) & Calamity (`H7451`, רָע) — Micah 1:12**
   - *Attested & Lexical Evidence*: `get_Word` documents `H4796` (`מָרוֹת`) as derived from root `מַר` (`H4751`, "bitter", *p_null* = 7.77e-10). In v. 12, `יוֹשֶׁבֶת מָרוֹת` ("inhabitant of Bitterness") writhes anxiously (`חָלָה`, `H2342`) for `טוֹב` ("good", `H2896`), only to receive `רָע` ("calamity/evil", `H7451`) descending from `יְהוָה` (`H3068`).
   - *LXX Witness*: `lxx_concordance` shows `מָרוֹת` (`H4796`) translated semantically as `ὀδύνη` ("pain/anguish", stability 0.936).
   - *Assessment*: **Verified root-based ironic contrast** (bitterness longing for good, receiving calamity).

4. **Moresheth-gath (`H4182`, מוֹרֶשֶׁת גַּת) ↔ Dispossessor (`H3423`, הַיֹּרֵשׁ) & Parting Gifts (`H7964`, שִׁלּוּחִים) — Micah 1:14–15**
   - *Attested & Lexical Evidence*: `wordplay_scan` (`H4182` ↔ `H3423`) confirms `proven_common_root: true` (`יָרַשׁ`, "to possess/inherit/dispossess"). `get_Connection` also links `H4182` morphologically to `גַּת` (`H1661`, *p_null* = 3.08e-7). In v. 14, `שִׁלּוּחִים` (`H7964`, dowry / parting gifts) pairs with "Possession-of-Gath", preparing v. 15's `הַיֹּרֵשׁ` ("the dispossessor", `H3423`).
   - *LXX Witness*: `lxx_concordance` aligns `מוֹרֶשֶׁת` to `κληρονομία` ("inheritance", stability 0.513) + `Γεθ` (stability 0.768), and `הַיֹּרֵשׁ` (`H3423`) to `κληρονόμος` ("heir", `lexical_theological_shift`, *p* = 0.0123).
   - *Assessment*: **Verified shared-root wordplay** bridging vv. 14 and 15 (and *interpretively*, sounding like Micah's own hometown name from Micah 1:1, *Moresheth*, given away with bridal parting gifts).

5. **Achzib (`H0392`, אַכְזִיב) ↔ Deceptive Thing (`H0391`, אַכְזָב) — Micah 1:14**
   - *Attested & Lexical Evidence*: `get_Word` documents `H0392` (`אַכְזִיב`) as derived from root anchor `אַכְזָב` (`H0391`, "deceptive [brook/wadi]", *p_null* = 1.13e-13). The clause `בָּתֵּי אַכְזִיב לְאַכְזָב` places the proper name and its cognate noun side by side with a minimal vocalic shift (`-îb` → `-āb`).
   - *LXX Witness*: `lxx_concordance` shows LXX translated **both** terms semantically: `אַכְזִיב` → `μάταιος` ("vain", stability 0.669) and `לְאַכְזָב` → `εἰς κενά` (`κενός` "empty", plus `γίνομαι`), erasing the toponym while preserving the semantic condemnation.
   - *Assessment*: **Verified cognate root wordplay** (explicit morphological pairing).

#### B. Deliberate Sonic / Contextual Paronomasia Without Shared Historical Root (or With Divergent Lexical Root Records)

1. **Gath (`H1661`, גַּת) ↔ Tell (`H5046`, נָגַד, *taggîdû*) — Micah 1:10**
   - *Lexical Verification*: `wordplay_scan` (`H1661` ↔ `H5046`) confirms `proven_common_root: false`, `formal_score: 0` (`גַּת` derives from `H1660`, "wine press"; `תַּגִּידוּ` derives from `H5046`, "to tell").
   - *Sonic & Allusive Basis*: `בְּגַת אַל־תַּגִּידוּ` creates crisp consonantal chiasm/alliteration (`g-t` / `t-g-d`) while echoing 2 Samuel 1:20 (reciprocally linked via editorial cross-reference, *p_null* = 3.02e-8).
   - *LXX Witness*: `ἐν Γεθ`, while `תַּגִּידוּ` (`H5046`) is `mt_minus` in LXX, replaced by `μεγαλύνεσθε` (`μεγαλύνω`, possibly reflecting a `תגדילו` / `תגידו` reading or shift).
   - *Assessment*: **Established literary allusion + sonic play**, *not* shared etymology.

2. **Zaanan (`H6630`, צַאֲנָן) ↔ Come Forth (`H3318`, יָצָא, *yāṣəʾâ*) — Micah 1:11**
   - *Lexical Nuance*: `get_Connection` documents STEPBible lexical derivation linking `צַאֲנָן` (`H6630`) to `צֹאן` (`H6629`, "flock") and `צְנָן` (`H6799`, "Zenan"). Simultaneously, `wordplay_scan` flags `צַאֲנָן` (`H6630`) ↔ `יֹצֵא` (`H3318` in Micah 1:3, score 0.87) under `etymological_root_match`, and `get_Connection` confirms direct textual convergence in Micah 1:11 (`לֹא יָצְאָה יוֹשֶׁבֶת צַאֲנָן`, *p_null* = 3.99e-8).
   - *Assessment*: **Deliberate prophetic paronomasia** exploiting the shared `צ-א` consonants between `יָצָא` (*yā-ṣāʾ*) and `צַאֲנָן` (*ṣa-ʾă-nān*)—even though the toponym's documented lexical cognates are `צֹאן`/`צְנָן`—creating the irony that "Going-Out-Town" does not go out. (LXX transliterates `Σεννααν` + `ἐξέρχομαι`).

3. **Beth-ezel (`H1018`, בֵּית הָאֵצֶל) ↔ Standing / Support (`H5979`, עֶמְדָּתוֹ) — Micah 1:11**
   - *Lexical & LXX Verification*: `H1018` ("house of nearness/side") contrasts contextually with `יִקַּח מִכֶּם עֶמְדָּתוֹ` ("its lament will take from you its standing/support"). Although `lxx_concordance` marks `H1018` as `mt_minus`, it records LXX's `ἐχόμενον αὐτῆς` (`ἔχω`, which translates `H0681` `אֵצֶל` "beside" 20× in the LXX!), showing the Greek translator rendered `בֵּית הָאֵצֶל` semantically as "the house next to her" and mapped `עֶמְדָּתוֹ` to `πληγή` / `ὀδύνη`.
   - *Assessment*: **Contextual/semantic contrast** ("House-of-Proximity/Neighbor" cannot lend support).

4. **Lachish (`H3923`, לָכִישׁ) ↔ Swift Steed (`H7409`, רֶכֶשׁ, *lārekeš*) — Micah 1:13**
   - *Lexical Verification*: `wordplay_scan` (`H3923` ↔ `H7409`) confirms `proven_common_root: false` (`H7409` derives from `H7408` `רָכַשׁ`, "to gather"; `H3923` `לָכִישׁ` is unrelated). However, `wordplay_scan` detects `edit_distance_1`, `phonetic_distance: 0`, `shared_consonants: "כש"` (formal score 0.542). With the prefixed preposition `לְ`, `לָרֶכֶשׁ` (*lā-re-keš*) and `לָכִישׁ` (*lā-kîš*) share an identical `lā- ... -k-š` frame.
   - *LXX Witness*: Transliterates `Λαχις` (stability 0.9995), aligns `לָרֶכֶשׁ` to `ἱππεύω` ("to ride"), and shifts `רֵאשִׁית` (`H7225`, "beginning") to `ἀρχηγός` ("leader/originator", *p* = 0.0208).
   - *Assessment*: **Pure sonic paronomasia** (assonance/alliteration across unrelated roots).

5. **Mareshah (`H4762`, מָרֵשָׁה) ↔ Dispossessor (`H3423`, הַיֹּרֵשׁ) — Micah 1:15**
   - *Lexical Verification*: `get_Word` documents `H4762`'s lexical root anchor as `מְרַאֲשָׁוֹת` (`H4761`, "head/top", *p_null* = 5.4e-10), and `wordplay_scan` (`H4762` ↔ `H3423`) confirms `proven_common_root: false`, `shared_consonants: "רש"`, `edit_distance: 1` (formal score 0.537).
   - *LXX Witness*: `lxx_concordance` aligns `מָרֵשָׁה` to both `Λαχις` (stability 0.844) and `κληρονομία` ("inheritance", stability 0.619), demonstrating that even ancient Greek translators perceived the `יָרַשׁ` ("inherit/possess") wordplay in `מָרֵשָׁה`.
   - *Assessment*: **Prophetic sonic/folk-etymological paronomasia** (`ר-ש` overlap echoing v. 14's `מוֹרֶשֶׁת` `H4182`), distinct from the lexical anchor `מְרַאֲשָׁוֹת` (`H4761`).

6. **Adullam (`H5725`, עֲדֻלָּם) ↔ Unto (`H5704`, עַד) — Micah 1:15**
   - *Lexical Verification*: `wordplay_scan` (`H5725` ↔ `H5704`) confirms `proven_common_root: false`, `attested_textual_wordplay: false`, `wordplay_candidate: true`, `shared_consonants: "עד"` (formal score 0.675). Paired in `עַד עֲדֻלָּם` (*ʿad ʿădullām*) alongside the internal verb shift `אָבִי` ("I will bring", `H0935` Hiphil) ↔ `יָבֹא` ("will come", `H0935` Qal).
   - *Assessment*: **Sonic alliteration (`ʿad ʿădullām`) + polyptoton on `בּוֹא`**, not a shared root between `עַד` and `עֲדֻלָּם`.

### 2. Verified Proper-Name Plays, Root Families, and Contrasts

#### A. The `Ὀνήσιμος` Onomastic Hub (vv. 10–11, 20)

1. **`Ὀνήσιμον` (`G3682`, v. 10) ↔ `ὀναίμην` (`G3685`, *ὀνίνημι*, v. 20) — Established Shared Root**
   - *Attested & Lexical Evidence*: `get_Word` documents `Ὀνήσιμος` (`G3682`) as sharing the root `ὄνησις` (`G8518`, "profitable", *p_null* = 1.04e-22). `wordplay_scan` (`onomastic_valence`) directly matches `Ὀνήσιμον` (`G3682`, v. 10) and `ὀναίμην` (`G3685`, aorist middle optative of `ὀνίνημι`, "may I have profit/benefit/joy", v. 20) via `etymological_root_match` (score 0.85). *(Tool observation: `get_Word` reports `etymology.status: "unavailable"` for `G3685`, even though `wordplay_scan` and `get_Connection` link `G3682` ↔ `G3685` as a root pair.)*
   - *Rhetorical Function*: Paul delays naming Onesimus until the final word of v. 10 (`Ὀνήσιμον`, "Useful/Beneficial"), and in the climactic plea of v. 20 plays directly on the verb from the same root: `ἐγώ σου ὀναίμην ἐν Κυρίῳ` ("let *me* have Onesimus-benefit from *you* in the Lord").

2. **`Ὀνήσιμον` (`G3682`, v. 10) ↔ `ἄχρηστον` (`G0890`) / `εὔχρηστον` (`G2173`, v. 11) — Semantic Name Play + Prefix Contrast**
   - *Attested & Lexical Evidence*: `wordplay_scan` (`onomastic_valence`) identifies `ἄχρηστον` (`G0890`, "useless") as `etymological_antonym` of `Ὀνήσιμον` (score 0.908) and `εὔχρηστον` (`G2173`, "useful/helpful") as `etymological_synonym` (score 0.868). Internally, v. 11 pairs `ἄχρηστον` ↔ `εὔχρηστον` (`shared_consonants: "χρηστ"`, formal score 0.742; `get_Connection` *p_null* = 4.62e-20), contrasting the privative prefix `ἀ-` with the meliorative prefix `εὐ-` across the temporal hinge `ποτέ` ("once") ↔ `νυνὶ δὲ` ("but now").
   - *Distinction on `Χριστός` (`G5547`) vs. `-χρηστος`*: `Χριστός` (`G5547`, vv. 8, 9, 20; rendering `H4899` `מָשִׁיחַ`, "anointed") and `χρηστός` ("useful/good", underlying `G0890`/`G2173`) belong to **unrelated etymological roots** (`χρίω` vs. `χράομαι`). *Interpretive hypothesis*: In Koine Greek pronunciation (with `η`/`ι` itacism), `ἄχρηστον` / `εὔχρηστον` would also carry an audible resonance with `Χριστῷ` (v. 8) / `Χριστοῦ` (v. 9) / `Χριστῷ` (v. 20), reinforcing that Onesimus becomes "useful" precisely "in Christ" (cf. v. 16 `ἐν Κυρίῳ`, v. 20 `ἐν Χριστῷ`).

3. **`ναί` (`G3483`) ↔ `ὀναίμην` (`G3685`) in v. 20 — Sonic Assonance Without Shared Root**
   - *Attested Evidence*: `wordplay_scan` flags `ναί` (`G3483`, pos 1) ↔ `ὀναίμην` (`G3685`, pos 5) via `shared_consonants: "ναι"` (score 0.627). While etymologically unrelated (affirmative particle `ναί` vs. verb `ὀνίνημι`), placing `ναί` and `ὀναίμην` at the opening of v. 20 creates an audible `nai ... o-nai-mēn` sound echo.

#### B. Verified Shared-Root Pairs & Semantic/Grammatical Clusters in Philemon 1:8–21

1. **`δέσμιος` (`G1198`, v. 9) ↔ `δεσμοῖς` (`G1199`, vv. 10, 13)**
   - `wordplay_scan` flags `consonantal_exact` (`δεσμ`, score 1.0). Both share the `δεσμ-` ("bind") root: Paul, himself a "prisoner" (`δέσμιος Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ`, v. 9), begot Onesimus "in my chains" (`ἐν τοῖς δεσμοῖς`, v. 10) and wished to keep him to serve "in the chains of the gospel" (`ἐν τοῖς δεσμοῖς τοῦ εὐαγγελίου`, v. 13).
2. **`ὀφείλει` (`G3784`, v. 18) ↔ `προσοφείλεις` (`G4359`, v. 19)**
   - `wordplay_scan` confirms shared root `οφειλ` (score 0.64). Paul pairs the simplex verb ("if he *owes* you anything") with the compound ("not to mention that you *owe* me *in addition* your own self"), embedded in a commercial-legal lexical cluster: `ἐλλόγα` (`G1677`, "charge to my account", v. 18), `ἀποτίσω` (`G0661`, "I will repay", v. 19, NT hapax; LXX equivalent of `H7999` `שָׁלֵם`), and `ἀπέχῃς` (`G0568`, "have/receive in full", v. 15).
3. **`ἔχων` (v. 8) / `κατέχειν` (v. 13) / `ἀπέχῃς` (v. 15) / `ἔχεις` (v. 17)**
   - Simplex and compound verbs built on `ἔχω` (`G2192`, "to have/hold") trace the narrative arc of possession and partnership: Paul *has* boldness (v. 8), wanted to *retain* (`κατέχειν`, `G2722`, v. 13) Onesimus, reframes Onesimus's absence so Philemon may *possess/receive in full* (`ἀπέχῃς`, `G0568`, v. 15), and appeals to Philemon's *holding/counting* (`ἔχεις`, v. 17) Paul as a partner (`κοινωνόν`, `G2844`).
4. **`μάλιστα` (`G3122`, v. 16) ↔ `μᾶλλον` (`G3123`, vv. 9, 16)**
   - `wordplay_scan` flags `μαλ` (score 0.667): true morphological superlative/comparative pair ("especially (`μάλιστα`) to me, how much more (`μᾶλλον`) to you", v. 16).
5. **`σπλάγχνα` (`G4698`, vv. 12, 20) & `ἀναπαύω` (`G0373`, v. 20)**
   - `get_Connection` confirms high-significance collocation between `ἀναπαύω` and `σπλάγχνα` (*p_null* = 9.18e-9, shared across Phlm 1:7, 20). Sending back Onesimus—who is literally identified as `τὰ ἐμὰ σπλάγχνα` ("my very heart", v. 12)—frames the closing imperative `ἀνάπαυσόν μου τὰ σπλάγχνα ἐν Χριστῷ` ("refresh my heart in Christ", v. 20).

#### C. Semantic & Rhetorical Contrasts in Philemon 1:8–21

- **Command vs. Appeal (vv. 8–9)**: `πολλὴν ... παρρησίαν ... ἐπιτάσσειν` ("great boldness ... to command") vs. `διὰ τὴν ἀγάπην μᾶλλον παρακαλῶ` ("for love's sake I rather appeal").
- **Compulsion vs. Free Will (v. 14)**: `κατὰ ἀνάγκην` (`G0318`, "according to necessity") vs. `κατὰ ἑκούσιον` (`G1595`, "according to free will"), contrasting with `χωρὶς δὲ τῆς σῆς γνώμης` ("without your consent").
- **Brief Separation vs. Eternal Reception (v. 15)**: `ἐχωρίσθη` (`G5563`, "was separated", passive voice) `πρὸς ὥραν` (`G5610`, "for an hour/short time") vs. `αἰώνιον` (`G0166`, "eternally") `αὐτὸν ἀπέχῃς` ("you might receive him back in full").
- **Slave vs. Beloved Brother (v. 16)**: `οὐκέτι ὡς δοῦλον` ("no longer as a slave") vs. `ὑπὲρ δοῦλον ἀδελφὸν ἀγαπητόν` ("above a slave, a beloved brother"), spanning both `ἐν σαρκὶ` ("in the flesh") and `ἐν Κυρίῳ` ("in the Lord").
- **Reciprocal Pronoun Framing (vv. 11, 18–20)**: `σοι ... καὶ σοὶ καὶ ἐμοὶ` (v. 11) → `τοῦτο ἐμοὶ ἐλλόγα` (v. 18) → `ἐγὼ ἀποτίσω ... σεαυτόν μοι προσοφείλεις` (v. 19) → `ἐγώ σου ὀναίμην ... μου τὰ σπλάγχνα` (v. 20).

#### D. Sonic Play vs. Surface Resemblance (Negative Controls in Philemon 1:8–21)

1. **`παρρησίαν` (`G3954`, v. 8) ↔ `παρακαλῶ` (`G3870`, v. 9) — Attested Alliteration, Unrelated Roots**
   - *Tool & Lexical Verification*: `get_Passage` (`r13:wp:G3954:G3870`, *p_null* = 0.000508 / 4.41e-5) and `wordplay_scan` (`attested_textual_wordplay: true`, `proven_common_root: false`, `shared_consonants: "παρ"`, score 0.609) flag this adjacent-verse pair.
   - *Distinction*: They have **distinct roots** (`παρρησία` < *pan + rhēsis*, "all-speech/boldness"; `παρακαλέω` < *para + kaleō*, "call alongside/appeal"). Yet the audible `παρ-` alliteration across vv. 8–9 (extended by `Παῦλος πρεσβύτης` in v. 9 and `παρακαλῶ ... περὶ` in v. 10) sharpens the rhetorical pivot from apostolic command to loving appeal.
2. **`Παῦλος` (`G3972`, vv. 9, 19) ↔ `ἀνάπαυσόν` (`G0373`, v. 20) — Surface Resemblance Only**
   - *Tool & Lexical Verification*: `wordplay_scan` flags `shared_consonants: "παυ"` (score 0.627), with `proven_common_root: false`, `attested_textual_wordplay: false`, `wordplay_candidate: false`.
   - *Distinction*: `Παῦλος` is a Latin cognomen (*Paulus*, "little/small"), whereas `ἀναπαύω` derives from Greek `παύω` ("stop/rest"). Their shared `παυ` sequence is a surface phonetic overlap rather than an etymological link.

   - *Tool & Lexical Verification*: Flagged by `wordplay_scan` (`shared_consonants: "χει"`, score 0.707). This is a purely coincidental orthographic overlap between an inflected ending (`ἔχ-εις`) and a distinct noun stem (`χειρ-`, "hand").

## Part III: Comparative Synthesis (Micah 1:10–15 vs. Philemon 1:8–21)

| Analytical Dimension | Micah 1:10–15 | Philemon 1:8–21 |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Device** | Series of 10–11 Shephelah town names transformed into judgment oracles via root punning and acoustic mimicry. | Delayed reveal of a single personal name (`Ὀνήσιμος`) unpacked through antonym/synonym contrast and cognate verb echo. |
| **Verified Root Wordplay** | `בֵּית לְעַפְרָה`↔`עָפָר`; `שָׁפִיר`↔`שַׁפַּר` (vs. `בֹשֶׁת`); `מָרוֹת`↔`מַר` (vs. `טוֹב`/`רָע`); `מוֹרֶשֶׁת`↔`יָרַשׁ`; `אַכְזִיב`↔`אַכְזָב`. | `Ὀνήσιμος`↔`ὀναίμην` (`ὄνησις`); `δέσμιος`↔`δεσμοῖς`; `ὀφείλει`↔`προσοφείλεις`; `μάλιστα`↔`μᾶλλον`. |
| **Non-Etymological Sonic Paronomasia** | `גַּת`↔`תַּגִּידוּ`; `צַאֲנָן`↔`יָצָא`; `לָכִישׁ`↔`לָרֶכֶשׁ`; `מָרֵשָׁה`↔`הַיֹּרֵשׁ`; `עַד`↔`עֲדֻלָּם`. | `παρρησίαν`↔`παρακαλῶ`; `ναί`↔`ὀναίμην`; (and `ἄχρηστον`/`εὔχρηστον` as semantic-etymological equivalents of `Ὀνήσιμος`). |
| **Direction of Irony (Interpretive Hypothesis)** | **Subversive reversal**: positive/protective town names ("Fair", "Going-Out", "Inheritance", "Spring") are turned into their opposites (shame, entrapment, dispossession, deception). | **Redemptive restoration**: an ironically named slave ("Useful") who became "useless" (`ἄχρηστον`) is restored to live up to his name (`εὔχρηστον`, `ὀναίμην`) as a beloved brother. |
| **LXX Preservation Effect** | `lxx_concordance` shows LXX translated several town names semantically (`γέλως`, `καλῶς`, `ὀδύνη`, `κληρονομία`, `μάταιος`), preserving meaning while losing Hebrew sonic puns. | Written directly in Greek, Philemon fuses sound, morphology, and commercial/familial semantics natively without translation loss. |
