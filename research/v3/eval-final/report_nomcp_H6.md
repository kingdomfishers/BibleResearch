# Intertextual and Text-Critical Investigation of Zechariah 11:4–17, Jeremiah 18–19, Jeremiah 32, and Matthew 27:3–10

## 1. Scope and Epistemic Framework

This study examines the prophetic sign-act of the shepherd, the two staffs (**Favor** and **Union**), the **thirty pieces of silver**, and the **potter/foundry/treasury** motif in **Zechariah 11:4–17**, alongside the potter and broken-jar sign-acts in **Jeremiah 18–19**, the field-redemption sign-act in **Jeremiah 32** (LXX **Jeremiah 39**), and the fulfillment citation and narrative of Judas's thirty silver coins and the potter's field in **Matthew 27:3–10**.

Throughout this analysis, claims are categorized into four distinct epistemic tiers:
1. **Attested Textual Evidence**: Direct readings in the Masoretic Text (MT), Septuagint (LXX), Syriac Peshitta, Targum Jonathan, and New Testament Greek manuscript tradition.
2. **Linguistic and Text-Critical Shifts**: Observable orthographic, morphological, syntactic, and lexical transformations across Hebrew, Aramaic/Syriac, and Greek witnesses.
3. **Structural and Rhetorical Parallels**: Shared genre conventions (prophetic *Zeichenhandlung* / sign-acts), dramatis personae, spatial settings, and motif clusters across the four textual units.
4. **Interpretive Hypotheses**: Historical, redactional, and compositional reconstructions explaining how these traditions converged in Second Temple Judaism and Matthean reception.

## 2. Zechariah 11:4–17: Textual Profile and MT/LXX Divergences

### 2.1 Literary Structure of the Sign-Act
Zechariah 11:4–17 concludes the first oracle (*maśśāʾ*, Zech 9:1–11:17) of Deutero-Zechariah and stands in thematic continuity with 11:1–3 (judgment on the shepherds/cedars) and 13:7–9 (striking the shepherd, scattering the sheep). Structurally, 11:4–17 unfolds as a paired prophetic sign-act:

1. **Commission and Indictment (11:4–6)**: The prophet is commanded to "shepherd the flock doomed to slaughter" (*ṣōʾn hahărēgâ*, v. 4; LXX *ta probata tēs sphagēs*), whose buyers, sellers, and shepherds show no pity (*lōʾ yaḥmōlû*). YHWH declares judgment in the form of internal fragmentation—delivering humanity each into the hand of his neighbor (*rēʿēhû*) and his king (*malkô*, v. 6).
2. **The Good Shepherd and the Two Staffs (11:7–14)**:
   - Taking two staffs (*šənê maqlôt*, v. 7), the prophet names one **נֹעַם** (*nōʿam*, "Favor / Pleasantness / Grace") and the other **חֹבְלִים** (*ḥōḇəlîm*, "Union / Binders / Bands").
   - After cutting off "the three shepherds in one month" (v. 8)—an unidentifiable historical-literary crux within the surviving text—mutual alienation ensues between shepherd and flock.
   - The prophet breaks **Favor** (*nōʿam*, v. 10) to symbolize annulling "my covenant which I had made with all the peoples" (*bərîtî ʾăšer kārattî ʾet-kol-hāʿammîm*).
   - Demanding his wages (*śəkārî*, v. 12), he is weighed out **thirty pieces of silver** (*šəlōšîm kāsep*)—the statutory compensation value of a gored slave in Exod 21:32 (*šəlōšîm šəqālîm kesep*).
   - At YHWH's command (v. 13), the silver is cast into the house of YHWH to the *yôṣēr* (potter / founder; see §2.2).
   - The prophet breaks **Union** (*ḥōḇəlîm*, v. 14) to dissolve "the brotherhood between Judah and Israel" (*hāʾaḥăwâ bên yəhûdâ ûḇên yiśrāʾēl*).
3. **Counter-Commission: The Worthless Shepherd (11:15–17)**: The prophet is instructed to take the gear of a "foolish shepherd" (*rōʿeh ʾĕwîlî*, v. 15; LXX *poimenos apeirou*, "inexperienced/unskilled shepherd"), culminating in a woe oracle against the "worthless shepherd" (*rōʿî hāʾĕlîl*, v. 17) whose arm and right eye will be struck.

### 2.3 The Zech 11:13 Crux: *yôṣēr* ("Potter"), *ʾôṣār* ("Treasury"), or *chōneutērion* ("Foundry")?
Three primary explanations account for the divergence in Zech 11:13 (classified here as **interpretive hypotheses** grounded in the attested textual variants above):
1. **Graphic Confusion Hypothesis (*yôṣēr* ↔ *ʾôṣār*)**: In square-script or late Hebrew orthography, **הַיּוֹצֵר** (*hywṣr*) and **הָאוֹצָר** (*hʾwṣr*) differ primarily by *yôd* vs. *ʾālep* (plus *wāw* placement). Because v. 13 specifies throwing the silver "into the house of YHWH" (*bêt YHWH*), many modern textual critics reconstruct an original *ʾel-hāʾôṣār* ("into the treasury," supported by the Peshitta's *bêt gazzā* and Targum's "treasurer"), viewing MT *hāyôṣēr* as an early graphic corruption or an intentional wordplay.
2. **Metallurgical / Temple-Foundry Hypothesis**: Other scholars (notably C. C. Torrey and subsequent commentators) argue that *yôṣēr* (from *yṣr*, "to form, cast, fashion," cf. Isa 44:9–12 of metalworkers) could denote a temple **metal-founder/caster** who melted down votive silver in the temple precinct into bullion (cf. 2 Kgs 12:10–13; 22:4–9; Herodotus *Hist.* 3.96). On this view, LXX *to chōneutērion* ("the smelting furnace / foundry") is not a guess from a different Hebrew word, but a contextual rendering of *yôṣēr* in a temple-metallurgy sense, which also explains LXX's *dokimos / edokimasthēn* ("tested/assayed") earlier in the verse.
3. **Pottery / Jeremianic Resonance Hypothesis**: Even if *hāyôṣēr* entered MT via textual error or retained a specialized craft sense, the vocalized MT unambiguously reads **"the potter"** (*hāyôṣēr*), the standard Hebrew term used throughout **Jeremiah 18–19**. As a consequence, Greek and Hebrew readers who read MT literally—including the Matthean tradition—encountered "potter" directly in Zech 11:13.

## 3. Jeremiah 18–19 and Jeremiah 32 (LXX 39): Textual Basis for the Composite Tradition

### 3.1 Jeremiah 18:1–12: The Potter's House (*bêt hayyôṣēr* / *ho oikos tou kerameōs*)
- **Attested Evidence**: Jeremiah is commanded to go down to the **potter's house** (MT 18:2 **בֵּית הַיּוֹצֵר**; LXX 18:2 **εἰς τὸν οἶκον τοῦ κεραμέως**). Watching the potter (*hayyôṣēr* / *ho kerameus*) rework a marred clay vessel (*kəlî*) on the wheel (*ʾobnayim*), the prophet receives an oracle of YHWH's sovereign power over nations to destroy or build depending on repentance (18:6–10).
- **Textual Relationship**: LXX Jer 18 closely follows the Hebrew and consistently translates *yôṣēr* with **κεραμεύς** (*kerameus*), the exact Greek noun used in Matt 27:7 and 27:10.

### 3.2 Jeremiah 19:1–15: The Broken Flask, Elders/Priests, Innocent Blood, and Burial
Jeremiah 19 exhibits dense lexical and structural overlap with both Zechariah 11 and Matthew 27:
- **Dramatis Personae (19:1)**: Jeremiah is told to buy a potter's earthenware flask (*baqbuq yôṣēr ḥāreś*; LXX *bikon peplasmenon ostrakinon*) and take **elders of the people and elders of the priests** (*ziqnê hāʿām wəziqnê hakkōhănîm*; LXX *apo tōn presbyterōn tou laou kai apo tōn presbyterōn tōn hiereōn*). Compare the repeated Matthean pairing of "chief priests and elders" (*hoi archiereis kai hoi presbyteroi*, Matt 27:1, 3, 6, 12).
- **Spatial Setting (19:2, 14)**: Jeremiah goes to the **Valley of Ben-Hinnom** (*gêʾ ben-hinnōm*) at the **Potsherd Gate** (*šaʿar haḥarsît*; LXX *pylē Charsith*), and afterward returns to stand in the court of the **house of YHWH** (*bəḥăṣar bêt-YHWH*, 19:14).
- **Innocent Blood and Slaughter (19:4, 6)**: Condemnation falls on Judah because they "filled this place with the **blood of innocents**" (MT 19:4 **דַּם נְקִיִּם**, *dam nəqiyyîm*; LXX *haimatos athōōn*), so Topheth/Ben-Hinnom will be renamed the **Valley of Slaughter** (MT 19:6 **גֵּיא הַהֲרֵגָה**, *gêʾ hahărēgâ*; LXX *pharax tēs polyandrias* / cf. 7:32 *pharax tēs sphagēs*). Notably, the Hebrew noun *hărēgâ* ("slaughter") recurs in Zech 11:4, 7 (*ṣōʾn hahărēgâ*).
- **Shattering and Burial (19:10–11)**: Shattering the **potter's vessel** (*kəlî hayyôṣēr*) beyond repair (*lōʾ-yûkal ləhērāpēh ʿôd*), Jeremiah announces that Judah will be buried in Topheth **"because there will be no [other] place to bury"** (MT 19:11 *mēʾên māqôm liqbôr*; LXX *dia to mē hyparchein topon tou thapsai*). This links **potter**, **innocent blood**, and **burial place** within a single prophetic judgment oracle.

### 3.3 Jeremiah 32 (LXX 39): Purchase of the Field (*śādeh* / *agros*) with Weighed Silver
In MT Jeremiah 32 (= LXX Jeremiah 39, owing to the mid-book placement of the Oracles Against the Nations in LXX), Jeremiah, confined in the court of the guard during Babylon's siege, exercises the right of redemption (*mišpaṭ haggəʾullâ*, 32:7–8) to purchase a **field** in Anathoth from his cousin Hanamel:
- **Lexical Core (Jer 32:9 / LXX 39:9)**:
  - MT: **וָאֶקְנֶה אֶת־הַשָּׂדֶה ... וָאֶשְׁקֳלָה־לוֹ אֶת־הַכֶּסֶף שִׁבְעָה שְׁקָלִים וַעֲשָׂרָה הַכָּסֶף** ("And I bought the field... and I weighed out to him the silver: seventeen shekels of silver").
  - LXX: **καὶ ἐκτησάμην τὸν ἀγρὸν ... καὶ ἔστησα αὐτῷ ἑπτὰ σίκλους καὶ δέκα ἀργυρίου** ("and I acquired the field [*agron*]... and weighed out [*estēsa*] to him seven shekels and ten of silver").
- **Legal Documentation and Earthenware Archive (32:10–15)**: Silver is weighed on scales (*bammōʾzənāyim*), deeds are signed before witnesses, and the deeds are placed in an **earthenware vessel** (*bikəlî-ḥāreś*, v. 14; LXX *eis angeion ostrakinon*) as a pledge of future restoration ("houses and **fields** and vineyards shall again be bought in this land," v. 15).
- **Structural Contrast**: Whereas Jer 32 uses the purchase of a **field** (*śādeh* / *agros*) with **weighed silver** (*šql* / *histēmi*) and an **earthenware jar** as a sign-act of **redemptive hope**, Jer 19 uses a **potter's vessel** and **innocent blood** as a sign-act of **catastrophic burial**. Matthew 27:3–10 draws vocabulary from both streams while inverting Jer 32's hopeful purchase into a cursed "Field of Blood."

## 4. Matthew 27:3–10: Narrative Integration, Textual Shifts, and Manuscript Evidence

### 4.1 Narrative Architecture (Matt 27:3–8)
1. **v. 3**: Judas, seeing Jesus condemned, feels remorse (*metamelētheis*) and returns the **thirty pieces of silver** (*ta triakonta argyria*, resuming 26:15 *estēsan autō triakonta argyria*) to the **chief priests and elders**.
2. **v. 4**: Confession: "I have sinned by betraying **innocent blood**" (*haima athōon* in א, Bᶜ, A, C, W, etc.; notable variant *haima dikaion* "righteous blood" in B*, L, some Old Latin/Syriac witnesses, likely assimilated to 27:19, 24).
3. **v. 5**: Judas throws the coins **into the sanctuary** (*rhipsas ta argyria eis ton naon*) and hangs himself (structurally paralleling Ahithophel in 2 Sam 17:23; distinct from the independent tradition in Acts 1:18–19, where Judas himself acquires a field with the wages of unrighteousness and dies by falling).
4. **v. 6**: The chief priests refuse to deposit the coins in the **temple treasury** (*eis ton korbanan*, Aramaic *qorbānā*), because it is **blood money / price of blood** (*timē haimatos*).
5. **vv. 7–8**: After consultation, they buy the **potter's field** (*ēgorasan ex autōn ton agron tou kerameōs*) "as a burial place for strangers" (*eis taphēn tois xenois*), giving rise to the etiology **"Field of Blood"** (*Agros Haimatos*, cf. Acts 1:19 *Hakeldamach* / *Chōrion Haimatos*).

### 4.3 Textual History of the "Jeremiah" Attribution in Matt 27:9
- **Dominant Attested Reading**: Virtually all major early Greek witnesses (א, A, B, C, W, Δ, Θ, most minuscules, Vulgate, Bohairic) read **διὰ Ἰερεμίου τοῦ προφήτου** ("through Jeremiah the prophet").
- **Secondary Harmonizing Variants**:
  - **Omission of the name**: A handful of witnesses (e.g., Φ, minuscule 33/157-related or *f*¹, some Old Latin MSS, Syriac Peshitta) read simply "through the prophet" (*dia tou prophētou*), removing the perceived error.
  - **Substitution of "Zechariah"**: A very few late/marginal witnesses (e.g., minuscule 22, Syriac Harklean margin, Arabic/Persian versions) substitute "Zechariah."
- **Text-Critical Verdict**: By the principle of *lectio difficilior potior*, **Ἰερεμίου** ("Jeremiah") is indisputably the earliest recoverable text of Matthew 27:9; the omission and "Zechariah" readings are transparent scribal corrections.

### 4.4 Interpretive Hypotheses for Matthew's Attribution and Textual Synthesis
1. **Conflated Citation (*Gezerah Shawah*) Under the Principal Prophet**: In Second Temple and early Jewish exegesis, quotations from two or more prophetic texts linked by shared catchwords (*yôṣēr* / *kerameus*, "silver," "innocent blood") were routinely fused and attributed to the major or framing prophet. An exact NT parallel occurs in **Mark 1:2–3**, where Exod 23:20 + Mal 3:1 + Isa 40:3 are introduced under the single heading "as it is written in Isaiah the prophet." In Matt 27:3–10, **Jeremiah 18–19** and **Jeremiah 32** supply the **elders/priests**, the **potter**, the **field** (*agros*) bought with weighed silver, **innocent blood**, and **burial**, while **Zechariah 11:12–13** supplies the **thirty pieces of silver**, the **valuation/price**, and throwing the coins into the **house of the Lord**.
2. **Narrative Mirroring of the Zech 11:13 Textual Tension**: Remarkably, Matt 27:5–7 dramatizes *both* competing senses of Zech 11:13:
   - Judas throws the silver into the temple (*naos*, v. 5 — matching *bêt YHWH*).
   - The chief priests consider placing it into the **treasury** (*korbanas*, v. 6 — echoing the *ʾôṣār* / Peshitta *bêt gazzā* reading!), explicitly reject that option as unlawful blood money, and instead buy the **potter's field** (*agros tou kerameōs*, v. 7 — activating MT's *yôṣēr* alongside Jeremiah 18–19 and 32).
   Whether Matthew knew both exegetical traditions (*ʾôṣār* vs. *yôṣēr*) or arrived at the contrast via halakhic reflection on Deut 23:18 and temple purity, the narrative accounts for why the coins do *not* stay in the temple treasury.
3. **Canonical Scroll-Order Hypothesis**: In the Talmudic order of the Latter Prophets (*b. Bava Batra* 14b), Jeremiah precedes Ezekiel, Isaiah, and the Twelve, leading some interpreters to suggest "Jeremiah" could serve as a collective scroll title for the Prophets. However, because Matthew cites Isaiah and Zechariah (21:4–5; 26:31) elsewhere without calling them "Jeremiah," the **composite-citation hypothesis** (Jeremiah 18–19 + 32 as the thematic anchor fused with Zechariah 11) remains far more persuasive than scroll-order nomenclature alone.
4. **Patristic Apocryphon Hypothesis**: Jerome (*Comm. Matt.* on 27:9) reports seeing an apocryphal Hebrew book of Jeremiah among the Nazarenes containing this exact wording, while Augustine (*Cons.* 3.7.29–30) treats it as a providential slip of memory linking Jeremiah and Zechariah. Most modern scholars view any purported apocryphal Jeremiah text containing Matt 27:9–10 verbatim as likely influenced by Matthew rather than serving as Matthew's source, given how tightly Matt 27:9–10 tracks the Hebrew syntax of Zech 11:12–13 combined with Jeremianic elements.

| **Theological Trajectory** | Covenant with peoples annulled; brotherhood of Judah & Israel shattered; rise of worthless shepherd | Warning of irreversible shattering due to innocent blood, contingent on repentance (ch. 18) vs. irrevocable doom (ch. 19) | Token of **future restoration** beyond exile: fields will again be bought | Inversion of Jer 32's hopeful purchase into Jer 19's **judgment/burial** motif via Zech 11's rejected shepherd-price |

### Key Analytical Takeaways
1. **Zech 11:13 is the hinge of two distinct textual trajectories**: A **metallurgical/treasury trajectory** (LXX *chōneutērion* + *dokimos*; Peshitta *bêt gazzā* = *ʾôṣār*) and a **potter trajectory** (MT *hāyôṣēr*).
2. **Matthew 27:9–10 is not a pure LXX quotation**: While sharing LXX's *estēsan* (26:15 // LXX Zech 11:12) and Jeremianic *kerameus* / *agros* vocabulary, Matt 27:9–10 reflects direct engagement with a **Hebrew-aligned Zechariah 11:13** (*yôṣēr* as potter; *yqr* as *timē*/price rather than LXX *dokimos*), reshaped to fit third-person narrative subjects (*they* took, *they* gave).
3. **Matthew's "Jeremiah" citation operates via midrashic conflation**: Zechariah 11 provides the numeric price (30 silver pieces), the act of casting silver into the sanctuary, and the *yôṣēr* catchword; **Jeremiah 18–19** provides the potter, the elders and priests, innocent blood, slaughter, and burial space; and **Jeremiah 32 (LXX 39)** provides the legal purchase of a *field* (*agros*) with weighed silver, whose hopeful symbolism Matthew dramatically inverts.

## 6. Methodological Scope and Limitations

- This study was conducted strictly from standard critical editions and established philological knowledge of the Masoretic Text, Septuagint, Peshitta, Targum Jonathan, and New Testament Greek manuscript tradition without automated corpus search tools or database lookups.
- Exact exhaustive statistical counts of every occurrence of roots such as *yṣr*, *ʾṣr*, *ḥbl*, *šql*, or *kerameus* across the entire biblical corpus are not claimed here; only directly relevant occurrences in the target passages and their immediate lexical parallels are cited.
- Where textual reconstructions remain debated in scholarship (e.g., whether *hāyôṣēr* or *hāʾôṣār* stood in the earliest Hebrew text of Zech 11:13, or the precise identity of the "three shepherds" in Zech 11:8), the competing hypotheses are presented explicitly alongside the attested witness evidence rather than resolved dogmatically.
