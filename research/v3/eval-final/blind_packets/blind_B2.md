### B. Linguistic, Grammatical, and Lexical Relationships

1. **`מִכְשׁוֹל` (H4383) & `מוֹקֵשׁ` (H4170) → `πρόσκομμα` (G4348) & `σκάνδαλον` (G4625):**
   - research engine `research (lxx translations)` confirms that in the BCV/Swete LXX corpus, `מִכְשׁוֹל` (H4383) is attested with `πρόσκομμα` (8× in Swete), `προσκοπή` (8×), and `σκάνδαλον` (3× in BCV/Swete, e.g., Lev 19:14, Ps 118[119]:165), while `σκάνδαλον` (G4625) renders `מוֹקֵשׁ` (H4170, 5×) and `מִכְשׁוֹל` (H4383, 3×).
   - Crucially, in **Isa 8:14**, Swete LXX uses `λίθου προσκόμματι` for `אֶבֶן נֶגֶף` (actually aligning `πρόσκομμα` with `נֶגֶף` [H5063], even though `נֶגֶף` elsewhere means "plague/blow," e.g., `πληγή` in Exod 12:13) and uses `πέτρας πτώματι` for `צוּר מִכְשׁוֹל`. Both **Rom 9:33** and **1 Pet 2:8** retain `λίθος/λίθον προσκόμματος` from the Isa 8:14 Greek tradition, yet replace `πτώματι` with `πέτρα(ν) σκανδάλου`—a more idiomatic Greek rendering of `צוּר מִכְשׁוֹל` (and echoing `מוֹקֵשׁ` later in Isa 8:14).
2. **`חוּשׁ` (H2363A, "to hasten") → `καταισχύνω` (G2617):**
   - research engine `research (lxx translations)` shows `H2363A` (`חוּשׁ`, "to hasten"; distinct from homograph `H2363B` "to enjoy") is rendered in Swete LXX by `προστρέχω` (7×), `κατασπεύδω` (2×), `σπεύδω` (1×), and `ταχύνω` (1×)—**never** `καταισχύνω`. Conversely, query module lexicon data for `G2617` (`καταισχύνω`) links it primarily to `בּוֹשׁ` (H0954, 6×) and `קָלוֹן`/`קָלָה` (H7036/H7034). Thus, LXX Isa 28:16's `οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ` is an interpretive translation (or reflects a `יבוש`-type reading/semantic extension from "panic/flee in haste" to "be put to shame"), and **Rom 9:33, Rom 10:11, and 1 Pet 2:6 all depend on the LXX** against the MT here.
3. **`אָמַן` (H0539) & `אֱמוּנָה` (H0530) → `πιστεύω` (G4100) & `πίστις` (G4102):**
   - research engine `lxx` bridges show `H0539` (`אָמַן`, Hiphil `הַמַּאֲמִין` in Isa 28:16) corresponds to `πιστεύω` (G4100, 31× in BCV) and `πιστός` (G4103, 27×), while `H0530` (`אֱמוּנָה`, "faithfulness/steadfastness" in Hab 2:4) is split evenly in BCV LXX between `ἀλήθεια` (G0225, 16×) and `πίστις` (G4102, 16×).
   - This lexical bridge enabled Paul in **Rom 9:32–33** to connect `πίστις` (`ἐκ πίστεως`) with Isa 28:16 (`ὁ πιστεύων`), and in **Rom 1:17** / **Gal 3:11** to read Hab 2:4's `ἐκ πίστεως` without the 3ms/1cs possessive pronoun (`בֶּאֱמוּנָתוֹ` / `μου`), allowing `ἐκ πίστεως` to modify either `ὁ δίκαιος` or `ζήσεται` in the Pauline contrast with `ἐκ νόμου` / `ἐξ ἔργων`.
4. **`עָפַל` (H6075) → `ὑποστέλλω` (G5288):**
   - `H6075` is a rare root (2 verses: `עָפַל` Pual `עֻפְּלָה` in Hab 2:4, "swollen/puffed up," and Hiphil `וַיַּעְפִּלוּ` in Num 14:44, rendered in LXX Num 14:44 by `διαβιασάμενοι`, "forcing their way presumptuously"). LXX Hab 2:4's `ἐὰν ὑποστείληται` ("if he shrinks back/draws back") is lexically distant from MT `עֻפְּלָה` (possibly reading a different Vorlage such as `עֻלְּפָה` "faint/collapse" or interpreting arrogance as cowardly withdrawal). Hebrews 10:38–39 (`ὑποστείληται` / `ὑποστολῆς`) builds its entire pastoral warning against apostasy on this LXX-specific lexical choice (`G5288` occurs only 4× in the NT: Acts 20:20, 27; Gal 2:12; Heb 10:38).
5. **Grammar & Case Shifts:**
   - **Ps 118:22 (`λίθον` → `λίθος`):** LXX Ps 117:22 exhibits inverse relative-clause attraction (`λίθον ὃν ἀπεδοκίμασαν...`, retained in Matt 21:42, Mark 12:10, Luke 20:17). 1 Peter 2:7 breaks this attraction (`λίθος ὃν ἀπεδοκίμασαν...`) so that `λίθος` stands in nominative parallel with `λίθος προσκόμματος` (v. 8) and contrasts with `ἡ τιμή` (v. 7a).
   - **Hab 2:3 (`ἐρχόμενος` → `ὁ ἐρχόμενος`):** LXX renders Hebrew infinitive absolute `בֹּא יָבֹא` ("it will surely come") with an anarthrous circumstantial participle (`ἐρχόμενος ἥξει`). Hebrews 10:37 inserts the definite article `ὁ` (`ὁ ἐρχόμενος`), transforming an emphatic Hebrew verbal construction about the *vision* into a personal/messianic title ("the Coming One").
   - **Isa 28:16 (`פִּנָּה` H6438 → `ἀκρογωνιαῖος` G0204):** `G0204` (`ἀκρογωνιαῖος`) occurs only twice in the NT—**1 Pet 2:6** (quoting LXX Isa 28:16) and **Eph 2:20** (`ὄντος ἀκρογωνιαίου αὐτοῦ Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ`, echoing `θεμέλιος` + `ἀκρογωνιαῖος` from LXX Isa 28:16).

   - query module directly surfaced the lexical hooks binding the three OT stone texts:
     - `אֶבֶן` (H0068) / `λίθος` (G3037) links **Isa 28:16**, **Isa 8:14**, and **Ps 118:22**.
     - `פִּנָּה` (H6438, 31v) links **Isa 28:16** (`פִּנַּת יִקְרַת`) and **Ps 118:22** (`לְרֹאשׁ פִּנָּה`).
2. **Conflation (Splicing) in Romans 9:33:**
   - Paul uses Isa 28:16 as an outer syntactic envelope (`ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον ... καὶ ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ᾽ αὐτῷ οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται`) and splices Isa 8:14 (`προσκόμματος καὶ πέτραν σκανδάλου`) into the middle `λίθον` slot—transforming the "precious cornerstone" of Isa 28:16 into a "stone of stumbling" in a single composite `καθὼς γέγραπται` citation.
   - Notably, in Rom 9:32 (`προσέκοψαν τῷ λίθῳ τοῦ προσκόμματος`), Paul prepares the catchword `προσέκοψαν` (`G4350`) ↔ `προσκόμματος` (`G4348`) right before the quotation (detected by query module literary analysis).
3. **Expansive Tripartite Catena in 1 Peter 2:4–8:**
   - Rather than splicing Isa 28:16 and Isa 8:14 into one sentence as Paul does, 1 Peter 2:4–8 introduces **all three** stone texts sequentially after a programmatic thematic synthesis in v. 4 (`λίθον ζῶντα, ὑπὸ ἀνθρώπων μὲν ἀποδεδοκιμασμένον [Ps 118:22], παρὰ δὲ θεῷ ἐκλεκτὸν ἔντιμον [Isa 28:16]`):
     1. **v. 6:** Isa 28:16 (`διότι περιέχει ἐν γραφῇ...`)
     2. **v. 7:** Ps 118:22 (`ὑμῖν οὖν ἡ τιμὴ τοῖς πιστεύουσιν· ἀπιστοῦσιν δὲ...`)
     3. **v. 8:** Isa 8:14 (`καὶ λίθος προσκόμματος καὶ πέτρα σκανδάλου`), followed by `οἳ προσκόπτουσιν` (`G4350`, mirroring Paul's v. 32 wordplay) and `εἰς ὃ καὶ ἐτέθησαν` (`G5087`, echoing `τίθημι` in v. 6!).
   - Furthermore, `ἔντιμον` (v. 6) generates `ἡ τιμὴ` (v. 7), and `πιστεύων` (v. 6) generates `τοῖς πιστεύουσιν· ἀπιστοῦσιν δέ` (v. 7), weaving the three citations into a tight antithetical diptych (believers vs. unbelievers).
4. **Splicing and Clause-Inversion in Hebrews 10:37–38:**
   - Hebrews 10:37 splices **LXX Isaiah 26:20** (`μικρὸν ὅσον ὅσον`, linked by `ἔτι γάρ` to LXX Hab 2:3a `διότι ἔτι`) onto **LXX Habakkuk 2:3b** (`ὁ ἐρχόμενος ἥξει καὶ οὐ χρονίσει`).
   - Hebrews 10:38 then **inverts the clause order** of Hab 2:4 (putting `2:4b` before `2:4a`) so that the positive promise (`ὁ δὲ δίκαιός μου ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται`) follows immediately after `ὁ ἐρχόμενος ἥξει`, and the warning (`καὶ ἐὰν ὑποστείληται, οὐκ εὐδοκεῖ ἡ ψυχή μου ἐν αὐτῷ`) stands at the end—creating an immediate bridge into v. 39's rhetorical antithesis (`ὑποστολῆς εἰς ἀπώλειαν ἀλλὰ πίστεως εἰς περιποίησιν ψυχῆς`).
5. **Contextual Echoes in Isaiah 28:14–18 and Isaiah 8:13–15:**
   - query module highlights that MT Isa 28:15–18 contrasts the Jerusalem rulers' "covenant with death / shelter of falsehood" (`שֶׁקֶר`, `כָּזָב`, `שׁוֹט שׁוֹטֵף`) with YHWH's laid stone (`יִסַּד ... מוּסָד`).
   - In Isa 8:13–15, MT's `וְכָשְׁלוּ בָם רַבִּים וְנָפְלוּ וְנִשְׁבָּרוּ` ("and many will stumble on them and fall and be broken", root `כשל` linking `מִכְשׁוֹל` in 8:14 to `וְכָשְׁלוּ` in 8:15) stands behind the judgment motif in Luke 20:18 / Matt 21:44 (`ὁ πεσὼν ἐπὶ τὸν λίθον τοῦτον συνθλασθήσεται`) appended right after Ps 118:22.

### D. Interpretive Hypotheses (Explicitly Separated from Observed Textual Facts)

1. **Shared Pre-Pauline / Early Christian Stone *Testimonium* or Hebraizing Greek Revision (Rom 9:33 & 1 Pet 2:6–8):**
   - Both Paul (Rom 9:33) and 1 Peter (2:6–8) agree against the Swete LXX in three independent details: (i) `ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν` (instead of LXX `ἐγὼ ἐμβαλῶ εἰς τὰ θεμέλια Σιὼν`), (ii) `πέτρα(ν) σκανδάλου` (instead of LXX `πέτρας πτώματι`), and (iii) stripping LXX Isa 8:14's negation (`οὐχ ὡς ... οὐδὲ ὡς`). Because 1 Peter retains LXX elements Paul omits (`ἀκρογωνιαῖον ἐκλεκτὸν ἔντιμον`, `οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ`) and does not splice Isa 8:14 inside Isa 28:16, direct literary borrowing by 1 Peter from Romans alone is unlikely to explain the full pattern; a shared early Christian stone *testimonium* tradition and/or a Hebraizing Greek text-type circulating in the first century is a strong explanatory hypothesis.
2. **Reason for the LXX Shift in Habakkuk 2:4 and Hebrews' Pronoun Placement:**
   - LXX's switch from 3ms (`נַפְשׁוֹ`, `בֶּאֱמוּנָתוֹ`) to 1cs (`ἡ ψυχή μου`, `ἐκ πίστεώς μου`) may stem from graphic confusion (`ו`/`י`) or theological reading of God as speaker of both halves. Hebrews 10:38 (`ὁ δὲ δίκαιός μου ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται`) likely reflects either a pre-existing Greek variant (also attested in some LXX witnesses such as Codex Alexandrinus) or an intentional transposition by the author of Hebrews so that `ἐκ πίστεως` remains absolute (as in Paul) while `μου` binds the faithful believer directly to God ("my righteous one").
3. **Why Paul Omitted `μου` in Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11:**
   - Whether Paul followed a Greek Vorlage lacking `μου` or deliberately omitted it, leaving `ἐκ πίστεως` unqualified enables his thesis that righteousness is "from faith to faith" (Rom 1:17) and universally open to Gentiles (Gal 3:8–11), rather than restricted to "my [God's] faithfulness" or "his [own] faithfulness."

## 3. Detailed Passage-by-Passage Analysis

### 3.1 Isaiah 28:16

#### (a) Putative Textual / Original-Language Evidence `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **MT (Isa 28:16):** Commonly attested as opening with *lākēn kōh 'āmar 'ădōnāy YHWH hinnənî yissad bəṣiyyôn 'āben 'eben bōḥan pinnat yiqrat mûsād mûssād hamma'ămîn lō' yāḥîš* (note: whether the verb is vocalized as a Qal/Piel perfect *yissad* or participle *yōsēd*—the latter echoed in some ancient versions and Qumran discussions—requires MT/1QIsa^a verification). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **LXX (Isa 28:16):** Commonly attested as *dia touto houtōs legei kyrios [kyrios]: idou egō emballō eis ta themelia Siōn lithon polytelē eklekton akrogōniaion entimon eis ta themelia autēs, kai ho pisteuōn [ep' autō] ou mē kataischynthē*. Note that the presence or absence of *ep' autō* ("in him/it") in pre-Christian LXX manuscripts (versus Hexaplaric/Christianized witnesses) is a known textual-critical question requiring Göttingen LXX apparatus verification. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Romans 9:33:** *Idou tithēmi en Siōn lithon proskommatos kai petran skandalou, kai ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou kataischynthēsetai* (cf. Rom 10:11, which adds *pas*: *pas ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou kataischynthēsetai*). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **1 Peter 2:6:** *Idou tithēmi en Siōn lithon akrogōniaion eklekton entimon, kai ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou mē kataischynthē*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (b) Linguistic & Grammatical Relationships
1. **Verb of placement:** MT's *yissad* / *yōsēd* ("laid / founded") is rendered by LXX *emballō* ("I cast/throw into," paired with *eis ta themelia*, "into the foundations"). Both **Rom 9:33** and **1 Pet 2:6** replace *emballō* with ***tithēmi*** ("I place/set") and omit *eis ta themelia*, leaving *en Siōn* ("in Zion")—structurally closer to MT's *bəṣiyyôn* than to LXX's doubled *eis ta themelia*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
2. **Stone descriptors:** MT's *'eben bōḥan pinnat yiqrat mûsād mûssād* ("stone of testing, precious cornerstone of a founded foundation") becomes in LXX a four-adjective sequence (*polytelē eklekton akrogōniaion entimon*). **1 Pet 2:6** drops *polytelē* and *eis ta themelia autēs* and fronts *akrogōniaion* (*lithon akrogōniaion eklekton entimon*), whereas **Rom 9:33** replaces all the positive descriptors with the negative descriptors of Isa 8:14. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
3. **Final verb shift (MT *yāḥîš* → LXX/NT *kataischyn-*):** MT *lō' yāḥîš* derives from *ḥwš* ("to hasten, hurry, panic/flee in alarm"). LXX translates this as *ou mē kataischynthē* ("will by no means be put to shame/disgraced"). Both **Rom 9:33** (future indicative *ou kataischynthēsetai*) and **1 Pet 2:6** (aorist subjunctive *ou mē kataischynthē*, matching LXX mood) follow the **LXX against the MT**. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
4. **Object of belief (*ep' autō*):** MT has an absolute participle *hamma'ămîn* ("the one trusting/believing") with no prepositional object. Both Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:6 include *ep' autō* ("in/on him"), matching one stream of the LXX tradition (or introducing a personal christological antecedent). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (c) Structural & Rhetorical Parallels
- **Rom 9:33** uses Isa 28:16 as an outer frame (*Idou tithēmi en Siōn... kai ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou kataischynthēsetai*) into which Isa 8:14 is inserted.
- **1 Pet 2:6** keeps Isa 28:16 intact as the first of three distinct *lithos* quotations (2:6 = Isa 28:16; 2:7 = Ps 118:22; 2:8 = Isa 8:14), though already in 2:4 (*eklekton entimon*) the author anticipates Isa 28:16's vocabulary.

#### (d) Physical Analogy
- The physical image in MT Isa 28:16 is architectural stability under siege/storm (cf. Isa 28:15, 17): a heavy, tested (*bōḥan*) corner-anchor (*pinnāh*) deeply bedded (*mûsād mûssād*) so that inhabitants relying on the structure need not flee in panic (*lō' yāḥîš*). LXX shifts the physical/behavioral consequence from "fleeing in haste" to social/covenantal "being put to shame" (*kataischynthē*), generalizing architectural security into vindicated trust.

#### (e) Interpretive Hypotheses
- **Why did LXX render *yāḥîš* as *kataischynthē*?** Two standard hypotheses exist: (1) a translational/semantic extension ("not panic/flee" = "not be disappointed/shamed" by a failing refuge), or (2) graphic/root assimilation to *bwš* ("be ashamed," *yēbôš*).
- **Why do both Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:6 share *tithēmi en Siōn* and *ep' autō* against standard LXX?** Hypotheses include: (1) a shared pre-Pauline/early Christian Greek *testimonia* compilation; (2) 1 Peter's literary dependence on Romans; or (3) a Greek recension of Isaiah no longer preserved independently.

### 3.2 Isaiah 8:14

#### (a) Putative Textual / Original-Language Evidence `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **MT (Isa 8:14):** Commonly attested as *wəhāyāh ləmiqdāš ûlə'eben negep ûləṣûr mikšôl lišnê bāttê yiśrā'ēl ləpaḥ ûləmôqēš ləyôšēb yərûšālāim* ("and he shall become a sanctuary, yet/and a stone of striking and a rock of stumbling to both houses of Israel, a trap and a snare to the inhabitant[s] of Jerusalem"). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **LXX (Isa 8:14):** Commonly attested as *kai ean ep' autō pepoithōs ēs, estai soi eis hagiasma, kai ouch hōs lithou proskommati synantēsesthe autō oude hōs petras ptōmati; ho de oikos Iakōb en pagidi kai en koilasmati enkathemenoi en Ierousalēm* ("and if you trust in him, he will be to you for a sanctuary, and **not** as a stone-stumbling will you meet him, nor as a rock-fall..."). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Rom 9:33 & 1 Pet 2:8:** Both contain *lithon proskommatos kai petran skandalou* (accusative in Rom 9:33; nominative *lithos proskommatos kai petra skandalou* in 1 Pet 2:8). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (b) Linguistic & Grammatical Relationships
1. **Polarity reversal (MT positive predication vs. LXX negation):** MT states directly that YHWH will be both a sanctuary and a stone of striking / rock of stumbling. LXX inserts a conditional protasis (*ean ep' autō pepoithōs ēs*) and **negates** the stumbling-stone clauses (*ouch hōs... oude hōs...*). **Both Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:8 follow the MT against the LXX** by removing the negation and presenting the stone as an actual stone of stumbling. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
2. **Noun phrase alignment (*proskommatos* + *skandalou*):**
   - For MT *'eben negep* ("stone of striking/stumbling"), LXX has dative *lithou proskommati* ("stumbling of a stone"); NT has genitive *lithos proskommatos* ("stone of stumbling"), preserving LXX's *proskomma* root.
   - For MT *ṣûr mikšôl* ("rock of stumbling"), LXX has *petras ptōmati* ("falling/ruin of a rock"). Both Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:8 replace *ptōmati* with ***skandalou*** ("of offense / trap / stumbling-block"). Note that *skandalon* is a standard Greek rendering of *mikšôl* (and *môqēš* in v. 14b) in Jewish-Greek revisionist/recensional traditions (later associated with Aquila/Symmachus/Theodotion, or early Hebraizing revisions). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
3. **1 Pet 2:8b expansion:** *hoi proskoptousin tō logō apeithountes* ("who stumble, disobeying the word") echoes the cognate verb *proskoptō* from Isa 8:15 LXX (*kai poloi en autois proskopsousin*, "and many among them shall stumble"). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (c) Structural & Rhetorical Parallels
- In **Rom 9:33**, Isa 8:14 is fused with Isa 28:16 via the shared catchword *'eben* / *lithos* ("stone"). Notably, LXX Isa 8:14 begins *kai ean ep' autō pepoithōs ēs* ("if you trust in him"), providing a conceptual bridge to Isa 28:16's *ho pisteuōn ep' autō*.
- In **1 Pet 2:7–8**, the stone motif splits Israel/humanity into two responses: belief (v. 7a, *hymin oun hē timē tois pisteuousin*, echoing *entimon* from 2:6) vs. disobedience/stumbling (vv. 7b–8, linking Ps 118:22's rejected stone with Isa 8:14's stumbling stone).

#### (d) Physical Analogy
- **Isa 8:14** shifts from architectural construction (28:16) to hazards along a path and hunting traps: an unexpected rock jutting into a walker's path (*'eben negep* / *ṣûr mikšôl*) causing collision/tripping (*skandalon* originally denotes the trigger-stick of a trap, bridging *mikšôl* with *paḥ*/*môqēš* "trap/snare" in 8:14b).

#### (e) Interpretive Hypotheses
- **Why did LXX Isa 8:14 insert the negation?** Likely theological sensitivity: the translator softened the jarring idea that God Himself becomes a stumbling block to Israel, turning it into a pious promise for those who trust Him ("if you trust in him... you shall *not* encounter him as a stumbling stone").
- **Why do Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:8 agree verbatim on *lithos proskommatos kai petra skandalou* against standard LXX?** Either (1) Paul and/or a pre-Pauline Christian *testimonium* revised LXX Isa 8:14 toward the MT (*mikšôl* → *skandalou*, dropping the LXX negation), or (2) they drew on an existing Hebraizing Greek text (*kaige*-like revision) of Isaiah.

### 3.3 Psalm 118:22 (LXX Psalm 117:22)

#### (a) Putative Textual / Original-Language Evidence `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **MT (Ps 118:22):** Commonly attested as *'eben mā'ăsû habbônîm hāyətāh lərō'š pinnāh*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **LXX (Ps 117:22):** Commonly attested as *lithon hon apedokimasan hoi oikodomountes, houtos egenēthē eis kephalēn gōnias*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **1 Pet 2:7:** *Lithon hon apedokimasan hoi oikodomountes, houtos egenēthē eis kephalēn gōnias*. (Verbatim match with **Matt 21:42**, **Mark 12:10**, **Luke 20:17**, and LXX Ps 117:22). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **1 Pet 2:4 (allusion/splice):** *lithon zōnta, hypo anthrōpōn men apodedokimasmenon, para de theō eklekton entimon*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Acts 4:11 (adapted citation):** *houtos estin ho lithos ho exouthenētheis hyph' hymōn tōn oikodomōn, ho genomenos eis kephalēn gōnias*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (b) Linguistic & Grammatical Relationships
1. **MT–LXX isomorphism:** Unlike Isa 28:16 and Isa 8:14, LXX Ps 117:22 exhibits no major semantic divergence from MT Ps 118:22. LXX preserves the Hebrew casus pendens syntax (*'eben* → accusative *lithon* attracted/governed by *apedokimasan*, resumed by *houtos*—though MT uses a feminine verb *hāyətāh* agreeing with fem. *'eben*, whereas Greek *lithos* is masculine, hence *houtos*). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
2. **NT verbal alignment:**
   - **1 Pet 2:7** and the **Synoptics** adopt LXX Ps 117:22 verbatim, including the Hebraistic *ginomai eis* (*egenēthē eis* = *hāyətāh lə-*) and *kephalēn gōnias* (*rō'š pinnāh*).
   - **1 Pet 2:4** transforms the finite verb *apedokimasan* ("they rejected") into a perfect passive participle *apodedokimasmenon* ("having been rejected") paired with *hypo anthrōpōn* ("by humans") and contrasted with Isa 28:16's *eklekton entimon* ("chosen, precious").
   - **Acts 4:11** substitutes *exouthenētheis* ("despised / treated as nothing") for *apedokimasan* and converts *hoi oikodomountes* ("the builders," participle) into *hyph' hymōn tōn oikodomōn* ("by you, the builders," 2nd-person noun phrase).

#### (c) Structural & Rhetorical Parallels
- **1 Pet 2:4–8** frames the entire *lithos* catena by first introducing a synthetic *composite* thesis in 2:4 (combining Ps 118:22's "rejected stone" with Isa 28:16's "chosen, precious stone"), then citing Isa 28:16 (v. 6), Ps 118:22 (v. 7), and Isa 8:14 (v. 8) in sequence, bound together by the catchword *lithos* and the *timē*/*entimon*/*apisteousin*/*proskoptousin* wordplay.
- **Luke 20:17–18** (and the textually disputed **Matt 21:44**) immediately follows the Ps 118:22 citation with a judgment saying about falling on the stone and being crushed (*ho pesōn ep' ekeinon ton lithon synthlasthēsetai*), rhetorically bridging Ps 118:22 with Isa 8:14–15 (and Dan 2:34–35, 44–45). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (d) Physical Analogy
- The concrete analogy is masonry selection on a construction site: skilled masons (*habbônîm* / *hoi oikodomountes*) inspect cut blocks, discard an irregular or seemingly unsuitable stone (*mā'ăsû* / *apedokimasan*), yet that exact discarded block is elevated to the structural apex or primary corner-binding position (*rō'š pinnāh* / *kephalēn gōnias*). (Architectural debate continues on whether *rō'š pinnāh* denotes a foundational cornerstone or a crowning keystone/capstone.)

#### (e) Interpretive Hypotheses
- **Why does Acts 4:11 use *exouthenētheis* instead of *apedokimasan*?** Either (1) Luke/Peter paraphrases freely in oral sermonic style to heighten the indictment (*hyph' hymōn*), or (2) it reflects an independent Greek rendering of *mā'ăsû* (or *bāzû*) prior to LXX standardization in the book of Acts—though Luke 20:17 already uses *apedokimasan*, favoring rhetorical/sermonic adaptation in Acts 4:11.

### 3.4 Habakkuk 2:4 (with 2:3b)

#### (a) Putative Textual / Original-Language Evidence `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **MT (Hab 2:3b–4):** Commonly attested as:
  - v. 3b: *'im yitmahmāh ḥakkēh lô kî bō' yābō' lō' yə'aḥēr* ("if it tarries, wait for it; for it will surely come, it will not delay").
  - v. 4a: *hinnēh 'uppəlāh lō' yāšərāh napšô bô* ("behold, puffed up / swollen—his soul is not upright in him").
  - v. 4b: *wəṣaddîq be'ĕmûnātô yihyeh* ("and/but the righteous one by his faithfulness/faith shall live"). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **LXX (Hab 2:3b–4):** Commonly attested as:
  - v. 3b: *ean hysterēsē, hypomeinon auton, hoti erchomenos hēxei kai ou mē chronisē*.
  - v. 4a: *ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou en autō*.
  - v. 4b: *ho de dikaios ek pisteōs mou zēsetai* (with a major internal LXX variant placing *mou* after *dikaios*: *ho de dikaios mou ek pisteōs zēsetai*, e.g. in Alexandrinus/certain witnesses vs. Vaticanus *ek pisteōs mou*—exact MS distribution requires Göttingen XII Minor Prophets verification). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Hebrews 10:37–38:** Commonly attested as:
  - v. 37: *eti gar mikron hoson hoson, ho erchomenos hēxei kai ou chronisei* (opening phrase *mikron hoson hoson* drawn from **LXX Isa 26:20**).
  - v. 38: *ho de dikaios mou ek pisteōs zēsetai, kai ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou en autō* (note: some NT witnesses omit *mou* after *dikaios*, likely under Pauline influence, requiring NA28 verification). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Romans 1:17 & Galatians 3:11:** Both read *ho [de] dikaios ek pisteōs zēsetai* (omitting both *mou* and *autou*). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (b) Linguistic & Grammatical Relationships
1. **Personification of the "vision" (v. 3b):** In MT, *bō' yābō'* refers back to *ḥāzôn* ("vision," grammatical masc., "it will surely come"). LXX renders *erchomenos hēxei* ("coming, he/it will come") and *hypomeinon auton* ("wait for him/it"). **Hebrews 10:37** adds the definite article ***ho*** (*ho erchomenos*, "**the** Coming One"), turning an ambiguous Hebrew/Greek participle into an explicit messianic title (cf. Matt 11:3 / Luke 7:19), and shifts LXX's *ou mē chronisē* (subjunctive) to *ou chronisei* (future indicative). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
2. **Complete divergence of v. 4a (MT vs. LXX/Hebrews):** MT's obscure *'uppəlāh* ("puffed up / swollen" or "presumptuous") and *lō' yāšərāh napšô bô* ("his soul is not upright in him") become in LXX *ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou en autō* ("if he shrinks back, **my** soul has no pleasure in him"). **Hebrews 10:38b follows LXX against MT verbatim** in this entire clause. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
3. **Pronoun shift on *'ĕmûnāh* / *pistis* (v. 4b):**
   - **MT:** *be'ĕmûnātô* — "by **his** faithfulness/steadfastness" (3ms suffix referring to *ṣaddîq*).
   - **LXX:** Shifts 3ms suffix to 1cs ***mou*** ("my"): either *ek pisteōs **mou*** ("by **my** faithfulness"—God's faithfulness) or *ho de dikaios **mou** ek pisteōs* ("**my** righteous one by faith").
   - **Hebrews 10:38a:** Follows the *ho de dikaios **mou** ek pisteōs zēsetai* LXX variant (against MT's *'ô* and against the B-text *ek pisteōs mou*).
   - **Romans 1:17 & Galatians 3:11:** Omit the possessive pronoun altogether (*ek pisteōs*, neither "his" nor "my"). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (c) Structural & Rhetorical Parallels
- **Splicing in Heb 10:37:** The author of Hebrews prefaces Hab 2:3b with *eti gar mikron hoson hoson* from **LXX Isa 26:20** (*mikron hoson hoson* — "a very little while," originally urging God's people to hide briefly until divine wrath passes). This creates an eschatological urgency framing the Habakkuk citation.
- **Clause inversion in Heb 10:38:** Both MT and LXX place the negative warning first (2:4a) and the promise of life to the righteous second (2:4b). **Hebrews inverts them** (2:4b first, 2:4a second), so that the warning against shrinking back (*hyposteilētai*) stands immediately adjacent to v. 39's pastoral contrast (*hypostolēs* vs. *pisteōs*), bridging directly into the faith-roll of Hebrews 11.

#### (d) Physical Analogy
- **Hab 2:3–4** draws on watchtower/courier and military/traveler imagery (cf. Hab 2:1, standing on the watchpost): waiting patiently for an expected arrival that seems delayed (*yitmahmāh* / *hysterēsē*), contrasted with someone who either puffs up/falters (MT) or recoils/retreats under pressure (*hyposteilētai*, LXX/Hebrews—literally pulling back sails or drawing back in cowardice).

#### (e) Interpretive Hypotheses
- **Why did LXX Hab 2:4a read *ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou*?** Hypotheses include: (1) a different Hebrew *Vorlage* or vocalization (e.g., reading *'ulləpāh* / *'lp* "faint/draw back" instead of *'uppəlāh*, and *napšî* "my soul" instead of *napšô* "his soul", possibly due to *waw*/*yod* confusion); (2) deliberate theological re-reading in Hellenistic Judaism turning a cryptic line about a puffed-up enemy into a warning against apostasy/retreat under persecution.
- **Why does Paul omit *mou* in Rom 1:17 and Gal 3:11?** Either Paul follows a pre-existing Greek text lacking *mou*, or he deliberately drops the possessive pronoun to make *ek pisteōs* ("by faith") syntactically open to his theological contrast between *ek pisteōs* and *ex ergōn nomou* (Gal 3:11–12), whereas *mou* in LXX B (*ek pisteōs mou*, "by my [God's] faithfulness") would have obscured human faith as the contrasting principle.

## 4. Synthesis: Modes of NT Textual Engagement

1. **Following LXX against MT:**
   - **Isa 28:16 in Rom 9:33 & 1 Pet 2:6:** Adopts LXX's *kataischyn-* ("be put to shame") rather than MT's *lō' yāḥîš* ("not make haste/panic"), plus *ep' autō*.
   - **Hab 2:3b–4 in Heb 10:37–38:** Adopts LXX's masculine *erchomenos*, LXX's complete divergence in 2:4a (*ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou en autō*), and LXX's 1st-person *mou* (*ho de dikaios mou*) against MT's *'uppəlāh... napšô* and *be'ĕmûnātô*.
2. **Following MT (or Hebraizing Greek) against LXX:**
   - **Isa 8:14 in Rom 9:33 & 1 Pet 2:8:** Rejects LXX's conditional negation (*ouch hōs... oude hōs...*) and restores MT's affirmative claim that the stone is a stumbling stone; replaces LXX's *ptōmati* ("falling") with *skandalou* (closer to MT *mikšôl*).
   - **Isa 28:16 verb/location in Rom 9:33 & 1 Pet 2:6:** Both read *tithēmi en Siōn* rather than LXX's *emballō eis ta themelia Siōn*, aligning structurally closer to MT *bəṣiyyôn*.
3. **Splicing / Conflating Multiple Passages:**
   - **Rom 9:33:** Inserts Isa 8:14 (*lithon proskommatos kai petran skandalou*) directly inside Isa 28:16 (*Idou tithēmi en Siōn... kai ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou kataischynthēsetai*).
   - **1 Pet 2:4–8:** Fuses Ps 118:22 + Isa 28:16 in an introductory composite allusion (v. 4), then quotes Isa 28:16 (v. 6), Ps 118:22 (v. 7), and Isa 8:14 (v. 8) as a catchword-linked catena.
   - **Heb 10:37–38:** Prefaces Hab 2:3b–4 with LXX Isa 26:20 (*mikron hoson hoson*).
4. **Deliberate Rhetorical & Theological Adaptation:**
   - **Heb 10:37–38:** Adds definite article *ho* to *erchomenos* and inverts Hab 2:4b before 2:4a to set up v. 39 (*hypostolē* vs. *pistis*).
   - **Rom 1:17 / Gal 3:11:** Drops the possessive pronoun (*'ô* / *mou*) from Hab 2:4b.
   - **Acts 4:11:** Adapts Ps 118:22 from 3rd-person narrative into 2nd-person accusation (*hyph' hymōn tōn oikodomōn*) with *exouthenētheis*.

## Detailed Textual Analysis by Passage

### 1. Isaiah 28:16 → Romans 9:33; Romans 10:11; 1 Peter 2:6

#### (a) Attested Textual Evidence (MT vs. LXX)
* **Hebrew MT (Isaiah 28:16)**:
  `לָכֵ֗ן כֹּ֤ה אָמַר֙ אֲדֹנָ֣י יְהוִ֔ה הִנְנִ֛י יִסַּ֥ד בְּצִיּ֖וֹן אָ֑בֶן אֶ֣בֶן בֹּ֜חַן פִּנַּ֤ת יִקְרַת֙ מוּסָ֣ד מוּסָּ֔ד הַֽמַּאֲמִ֖ין לֹ֥א יָחִֽישׁ׃`
  * Key tokens: `הִנְנִי` (pos 6, `H2005`), `יִסַּד` (pos 7, `H3245`, "has laid/founded" — Piel perf. or archaic/defective form), `בְּצִיּוֹן` (pos 8, `H6726`), `אָבֶן` (pos 9, `H0068`), `אֶבֶן` (pos 10, `H0068`), `בֹּחַן` (pos 11, `H0976`, "testing", hapax legomenon in OT), `פִּנַּת` (pos 12, `H6438`, "corner of"), `יִקְרַת` (pos 13, `H3368`, "preciousness of"), `מוּסָד` (pos 14, `H4143`, "foundation"), `מוּסָּד` (pos 15, `H3245`, "founded"), `הַמַּאֲמִין` (pos 16, `H0539`, "the one who trusts/believes"), `לֹא` (pos 17, `H3808`), `יָחִישׁ` (pos 18, `H2363`, "he will make haste").
* **Greek LXX (Isaias 28:16, Rahlfs)**:
  `διὰ τοῦτο οὕτως λέγει Κύριος, Κύριος, ἐγὼ ἐμβαλῶ εἰς τὰ θεμέλια Σιὼν λίθον πολυτελῆ ἐκλεκτὸν ἀκρογωνιαῖον ἔντιμον, εἰς τὰ θεμέλια αὐτῆς, καὶ ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ’ αὐτῷ οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ.` *(Note: corpus text prints `εἷς` / `τὸ,` due to orthographic accents in the Rahlfs morph witness).*

#### (b) Linguistic & Concordance Shifts (MT → LXX)
1. **Verb & Prepositional Expansion (`הִנְנִי יִסַּד` → `ἐγὼ ἐμβαλῶ εἰς τὰ θεμέλια`)**:
   * `H3245` (`יָסַד`) is normally rendered by `θεμελιόω` in the LXX (27 times, mean stability `0.965`). In Isaiah 28:16 alone, the LXX introduces an `lxx_plus_expansion` (`ἐμβαλῶ εἰς`, "I will cast/lay into") and renders the founding root as plural noun `θεμέλια` (`θεμέλιον`, `p_g_given_h = 0.0256`, `g_idf = 12.32`), omitting `הִנְנִי` ("Behold!") in favor of emphatic `ἐγὼ` + future `ἐμβαλῶ`.
2. **Architectural Stone Qualifiers (`אֶבֶן בֹּחַן פִּנַּת יִקְרַת מוּסָד מוּסָּד` → `λίθον πολυτελῆ ἐκλεκτὸν ἀκρογωνιαῖον ἔντιμον, εἰς τὰ θεμέλια αὐτῆς`)**:
   * **MT minus**: `בֹּחַן` (`H0976`, "testing") occurs only once in the Hebrew Bible (Isa 28:16) and has zero direct LXX equivalents in query module (`mt_minus`). Instead, LXX substitutes `ἐκλεκτὸν` ("chosen") and adds `ἔντιμον` ("honored/precious", `lxx_plus_expansion`, `sum_idf = 10.04`).
   * **Unique rendering of `פִּנַּת` (`H6438`)**: Across 31 LXX verses, `H6438` is normally `γωνία` (20x, mean stability `0.996`). Only in Isaiah 28:16 is `פִּנַּת` rendered `ἀκρογωνιαῖον` (`G0204`, `p_g_given_h = 0.037`, `g_idf = 14.9`), a rare compound adjective meaning "at the extreme corner."
   * `יִקְרַת` (`H3368`) is rendered `πολυτελῆ` ("costly/precious"), and `מוּסָד מוּסָּד` is restructured into `εἰς τὰ θεμέλια αὐτῆς`.
3. **Theological Shift at the Climax (`הַמַּאֲמִין לֹא יָחִישׁ` → `καὶ ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ’ αὐτῷ οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ`)**:
   * **LXX plus (`ἐπ’ αὐτῷ`)**: MT has only the absolute participle `הַמַּאֲמִין` ("the one who trusts/believes") without an explicit object; LXX supplies `ἐπ’ αὐτῷ` ("on it / on him"), personalizing or specifying the stone as the object of trust.
   * **Unique rendering of `יָחִישׁ` (`H2363`)**: `H2363` (`חוּשׁ`, "to hasten") is rendered across the LXX by `προσέχω` (5x), `σπεύδω`, `ὁρμάω`, `σῴζω`, etc. **Only in Isaiah 28:16** is `H2363` rendered by `καταισχύνω` ("to be put to shame", `p_g_given_h = 0.0526`, `g_idf = 8.69`). Conversely, `καταισχύνω` (`G2617`) normally translates `בּוֹשׁ` (`H0954`, 6x), `קָלוֹן` (`H7036`, 3x), or `קָלָה` (`H7034`, 1x).

#### (c) New Testament Citations & Divergences
* **1 Peter 2:6**: `Ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον ἐκλεκτὸν ἀκρογωνιαῖον ἔντιμον καὶ ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ᾿ αὐτῷ οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ`
  * **Follows LXX against MT**: Preserves `ἐκλεκτὸν`, `ἀκρογωνιαῖον`, `ἔντιμον`, `ἐπ᾿ αὐτῷ`, and the exact LXX negative subjunctive `οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ`.
  * **Omits LXX elements / adapts**: Drops `πολυτελῆ`, drops both occurrences of `εἰς τὰ θεμέλια [αὐτῆς]`, swaps order of `ἐκλεκτὸν` and `ἀκρογωνιαῖον` (LXX has `πολυτελῆ ἐκλεκτὸν ἀκρογωνιαῖον ἔντιμον`; 1 Pet 2:6 has `ἐκλεκτὸν ἀκρογωνιαῖον ἔντιμον`, matching the `ἐκλεκτὸν ἔντιμον` pair already introduced in 1 Pet 2:4).
  * **Opening formula shared with Romans 9:33**: Both 1 Peter 2:6 and Romans 9:33 open with `Ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον` instead of LXX `ἐγὼ ἐμβαλῶ εἰς τὰ θεμέλια Σιὼν λίθον`. This restores MT's `הִנְנִי` (`Ἰδοὺ`) and uses present indicative `τίθημι` (`G5087`, "I lay/place") rather than LXX's future `ἐμβαλῶ`.
* **Romans 9:33**: `Ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον προσκόμματος καὶ πέτραν σκανδάλου καὶ ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ᾿ αὐτῷ οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται`
  * Shares `Ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον` and `καὶ ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ᾿ αὐτῷ` with 1 Peter 2:6 / LXX, replaces the architectural modifiers (`ἐκλεκτὸν ἀκρογωνιαῖον ἔντιμον`) with **Isaiah 8:14** (`λίθον προσκόμματος καὶ πέτραν σκανδάλου`), and shifts LXX's aorist subjunctive `οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ` to future indicative `οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται`.
* **Romans 10:11**: `Πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ᾿ αὐτῷ οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται`
  * Quotes only the final clause of Isaiah 28:16 in the exact form of Romans 9:33 (`οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται`, following LXX `καταισχυν-` + `ἐπ᾿ αὐτῷ` against MT `יָחִישׁ`), adding universalizing `Πᾶς` ("everyone") to support the Jew/Greek inclusion argument in Rom 10:12.

*(Interpretive Hypothesis)*: The identical opening `Ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον` in both Romans 9:33 and 1 Peter 2:6—diverging identically from Rahlfs LXX `ἐγὼ ἐμβαλῶ εἰς τὰ θεμέλια Σιὼν λίθον` while retaining LXX's `ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ᾿ αὐτῷ` and `καταισχυν-`—suggests either (i) a shared early Christian Greek *testimonia* stone tradition/recension already combining Hebraizing corrections (`הִנְנִי` → `Ἰδοὺ`; `יִסַּד` → `τίθημι`) with LXX endings, or (ii) literary acquaintance between Romans 9:33 and 1 Peter 2:6–8. Similarly, LXX's `καταισχυνθῇ` for MT `יָחִישׁ` may reflect either an interpretive rendering of "not hurrying/panicking" as "not being shamed" or a Hebrew Vorlage/reading confusing/exchanging `יחיש` with `יבוש` (`בּוֹשׁ`, `H0954`).

### 2. Isaiah 8:14 → Romans 9:32–33; 1 Peter 2:8

#### (a) Attested Textual Evidence (MT vs. LXX)
* **Hebrew MT (Isaiah 8:14)**:
  `וְהָיָ֖ה לְמִקְדָּ֑שׁ וּלְאֶ֣בֶן נֶ֠גֶף וּלְצ֨וּר מִכְשׁ֜וֹל לִשְׁנֵ֨י בָתֵּ֤י יִשְׂרָאֵל֙ לְפַ֣ח וּלְמוֹקֵ֔שׁ לְיוֹשֵׁ֖ב יְרוּשָׁלִָֽם׃`
  * Key tokens: `וְהָיָה` (`H1961`, "and he will be"), `לְמִקְדָּשׁ` (`H4720`, "a sanctuary"), `וּלְאֶבֶן` (`H0068`, "and a stone of"), `נֶגֶף` (`H5063`, "striking"), `וּלְצוּר` (`H6697`, "and a rock of"), `מִכְשׁוֹל` (`H4383`, "stumbling"), `לִשְׁנֵי בָתֵּי יִשְׂרָאֵל` ("for the two houses of Israel"), `לְפַח` (`H6341`, "trap") `וּלְמוֹקֵשׁ` (`H4170`, "and snare").
* **Greek LXX (Isaias 8:14, Rahlfs)**:
  `καὶ ἐὰν ἐπ᾿ αὐτῷ πεποιθὼς ᾖς, ἔσται σοι εἰς ἁγίασμα, καὶ οὐχ ὡς λίθου προσκόμματι συναντήσεσθε αὐτῷ, οὐδὲ ὡς πέτρας πτώματι. ὁ δὲ οἶκος Ἰακὼβ ἐν παγίδι, καὶ ἐν κοιλάσματι ἐγκαθήμενοι ἐν Ἰερουσαλήμ·`

#### (b) Linguistic & Theological Shifts (MT → LXX)
1. **Syntactic & Theological Reversal (`lxx_plus_expansion`)**:
   * **MT** presents YHWH (from v. 13) unconditionally as *both* a sanctuary (`לְמִקְדָּשׁ`) *and* a stone of striking (`לְאֶבֶן נֶגֶף`) and rock of stumbling (`לְצוּר מִכְשׁוֹל`) to both houses of Israel (`לִשְׁנֵי בָתֵּי יִשְׂרָאֵל`).
   * **LXX** inserts a conditional clause (`καὶ ἐὰν ἐπ᾿ αὐτῷ πεποιθὼς ᾖς`, "and if you trust in him") and **negates** the stumbling clause (`καὶ οὐχ ὡς λίθου προσκόμματι συναντήσεσθε αὐτῷ, οὐδὲ ὡς πέτρας πτώματι`, "and you shall *not* encounter him as a stumbling of a stone, nor as a falling of a rock"), shifting the trap/snare onto `ὁ δὲ οἶκος Ἰακὼβ` ("the house of Jacob") rather than "both houses of Israel."
2. **Lexical Shifts**:
   * `נֶגֶף` (`H5063`) → `προσκόμματι` (`πρόσκομμα`, `G4348`): attested in LXX Isa 8:14 (this is the *only* place `H5063` is rendered `πρόσκομμα` in LXX; elsewhere `πληγή`, `θραῦσις`, etc., though verbal `נָגַף` `H5062` is rendered `προσκόπτω` `G4350` 15x).
   * `מִכְשׁוֹל` (`H4383`) → `πτώματι` (`πτῶμα`, "fall"): a `lexical_theological_shift` unique to Isaiah 8:14 (`p_g_given_h = 0.0714`, `g_idf = 10.44`). Normally `H4383` is rendered `κόλασις` (5x) or `σκάνδαλον` (3x: Lev 19:14; 1 Sam 25:31; Ps 118:165 LXX).
   * `וּלְמוֹקֵשׁ` (`H4170`) → `κοιλάσματι` ("hollow/pit", `p_g_given_h = 0.0333`, `g_idf = 14.9`), whereas `H4170` is normally `σκάνδαλον` (9x) or `παγίς` (9x).
   * `לְיוֹשֵׁב` (`H3427`) → `ἐγκαθήμενοι` (`ἐγκάθημαι`, "sitting in wait/lying in ambush", `p_g_given_h = 0.0171`).

#### (c) New Testament Citations & Divergences (Rom 9:32–33; 1 Pet 2:8)
Both **Romans 9:33** (`λίθον προσκόμματος καὶ πέτραν σκανδάλου`) and **1 Peter 2:8** (`λίθος προσκόμματος καὶ πέτρα σκανδάλου`, echoed in **Rom 9:32** `προσέκοψαν τῷ λίθῳ τοῦ προσκόμματος`) show a striking **hybrid MT/LXX profile**:
1. **Follows MT against LXX syntax**: Both NT passages **reject** the LXX's added negation (`καὶ οὐχ ὡς ... συναντήσεσθε`) and restore the MT's positive statement that the stone/rock *is* a stumbling obstacle for the unbelieving/disobedient.
2. **Follows MT against LXX lexicon (`σκάνδαλον` vs. `πτῶμα`)**: Instead of LXX Isa 8:14's `πέτρας πτώματι`, both Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:8 use `πέτρα(ν) σκανδάλου`. In query module, `σκανδάλου` is explicitly flagged `agrees_mt_against_lxx` (`Isaiah 8:14#MT_vs_LXX`), because `σκάνδαλον` (`G4625`) is a documented LXX equivalent of MT `מִכְשׁוֹל` (`H4383`, 3x) and `מוֹקֵשׁ` (`H4170`, 9x), whereas `πτῶμα` is an anomalous LXX shift.
3. **Follows LXX `πρόσκομμα` + structural parallel**: Both NT passages retain `λίθον/λίθος προσκόμματος` (matching LXX Isa 8:14 `λίθου προσκόμματι`), and **1 Peter 2:8** adds an exegetical gloss (`οἳ προσκόπτουσιν τῷ λόγῳ ἀπειθοῦντες εἰς ὃ καὶ ἐτέθησαν`), exploiting the cognate verb `προσκόπτω` (`G4350`, echoed also in **Rom 9:32** `προσέκοψαν`) and connecting back to `τίθημι` (`ἐτέθησαν`, v. 8; `τίθημι`, v. 6).

### 3. Psalm 118:22 (LXX 117:22) → Synoptics, Acts 4:11, and 1 Peter 2:4, 7

#### (a) Attested Textual Evidence (MT vs. LXX)
* **Hebrew MT (Psalm 118:22)**:
  `אֶ֭בֶן מָאֲס֣וּ הַבּוֹנִ֑ים הָ֝יְתָ֗ה לְרֹ֣אשׁ פִּנָּֽה׃`
  * Tokens: `אֶבֶן` (pos 1, `H0068`), `מָאֲסוּ` (pos 2, `H3988`, "[which] they rejected"), `הַבּוֹנִים` (pos 3, `H1129`, "the builders"), `הָיְתָה` (pos 4, `H1961`, "it has become"), `לְרֹאשׁ` (pos 5, `H7218`, "head of"), `פִּנָּה` (pos 6, `H6438`, "corner").
* **Greek LXX (Psalmi 117:22, Rahlfs)**:
  `λίθον ὃν ἀπεδοκίμασαν οἱ οἰκοδομοῦντες, οὗτος ἐγενήθη εἰς κεφαλὴν γωνίας·`
  * Notably, query module records **zero `lxx_shifts_in_sources`** for Psalm 118:22–23: LXX renders MT literally word-for-word, supplying relative pronoun `ὃν` (absent in Hebrew asyndetic relative `אֶבֶן מָאֲסוּ`), rendering `מָאַס` (`H3988`) with `ἀποδοκιμάζω` (`G0593`, 7x in LXX, mean stability `0.904`), `הַבּוֹנִים` with present participle `οἱ οἰκοδομοῦντες`, and `לְרֹאשׁ פִּנָּה` with `εἰς κεφαλὴν γωνίας` (`γωνία` for `H6438` `פִּנָּה` = standard 20x equivalence).

#### (b) New Testament Citations & Divergences
1. **Matthew 21:42, Mark 12:10–11, and Luke 20:17 (Verbatim LXX)**:
   * **Matt 21:42** and **Mark 12:10–11** reproduce **LXX Ps 117:22–23** verbatim (`Λίθον ὃν ἀπεδοκίμασαν οἱ οἰκοδομοῦντες, οὗτος ἐγενήθη εἰς κεφαλὴν γωνίας· παρὰ Κυρίου ἐγένετο αὕτη καὶ ἔστιν θαυμαστὴ ἐν ὀφθαλμοῖς ἡμῶν`), keeping the accusative attraction `Λίθον ὃν` (matching LXX's `λίθον`, even though grammatically `οὗτος` is the nominative subject of `ἐγενήθη`!). **Luke 20:17** quotes Ps 117:22 verbatim (`Λίθον ὃν...`) and omits v. 23, transitioning directly to v. 18 (stone-crushing imagery echoing Isa 8:14–15 / Dan 2:34–35).
2. **1 Peter 2:4 and 2:7 (Nominative Adaptation + Allusion)**:
   * **1 Peter 2:4** already previews Ps 118:22 by describing Christ as `λίθον ζῶντα ὑπὸ ἀνθρώπων μὲν ἀποδεδοκιμασμένον` (perfect passive participle of `ἀποδοκιμάζω` `G0593`, shared with LXX Ps 117:22, Synoptic Passion predictions [Mark 8:31; Luke 9:22; 17:25], and 1 Pet 2:7) contrasted with `παρὰ δὲ Θεῷ ἐκλεκτὸν ἔντιμον` (from Isa 28:16 LXX).
   * **1 Peter 2:7** introduces Ps 118:22 with an antithetical dative bridge (`ὑμῖν οὖν ἡ τιμὴ τοῖς πιστεύουσιν, ἀπιστοῦσιν δὲ`) linking `ἔντιμον` (v. 6) → `τιμή` (v. 7) and `πιστεύων` → `πιστεύουσιν`/`ἀπιστοῦσιν`, and **adapts** LXX `λίθον` (accusative) to **nominative `λίθος`** (`λίθος ὃν ἀπεδοκίμασαν οἱ οἰκοδομοῦντες οὗτος ἐγενήθη εἰς κεφαλὴν γωνίας`), matching the nominative `καὶ λίθος προσκόμματος καὶ πέτρα σκανδάλου` in v. 8.
3. **Acts 4:11 (Free Adaptation / MT-Closer Nominal Form)**:
   * `οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ λίθος ὁ ἐξουθενηθεὶς ὑφ᾿ ὑμῶν τῶν οἰκοδόμων, ὁ γενόμενος εἰς κεφαλὴν γωνίας`
   * Differs from LXX Ps 117:22 in three ways:
     * **Verb of rejection**: Uses `ἐξουθενηθεὶς` (`ἐξουθενέω`, "despised/treated with contempt") instead of LXX `ἀπεδοκίμασαν`. Notably, query module for `H3988` (`מָאַס`) shows that LXX elsewhere renders `מָאַס` as `ἐξουθενέω` (2x: 1 Sam 8:7; 10:19) and `ἐξουδενόω` (16x)!
     * **Builders (`τῶν οἰκοδόμων` vs. LXX `οἱ οἰκοδομοῦντες`)**: Flagged by query module as `agrees_mt_against_lxx` (`Psalms 118:22#MT_vs_LXX`) because `οἰκοδόμων` (noun `οἰκοδόμος`, "builders") matches the nominal/substantive force of MT `הַבּוֹנִים` without adopting the LXX's verbal participle `οἱ οἰκοδομοῦντες`.
     * **Rhetorical recasting**: Converts the relative clause into a direct predicate (`οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ λίθος ὁ ... ὑφ᾿ ὑμῶν τῶν οἰκοδόμων`), identifying the Sanhedrin leaders as "you builders."

*(Catchword / Structural Linkage)*: query module verifies a direct lexical bridge between **Isaiah 28:16** and **Psalm 118:22** via the shared rare Hebrew noun **`פִּנָּה` (`H6438`, "corner", 29 OT verses)** alongside `אֶבֶן` (`H0068`, "stone"). In Hebrew, Isa 28:16 (`פִּנַּת יִקְרַת`) and Ps 118:22 (`רֹאשׁ פִּנָּה`) share the exact catchword `פִּנָּה`; in the LXX, however, Isa 28:16 translates `פִּנַּת` as `ἀκρογωνιαῖον` while Ps 117:22 translates `פִּנָּה` as `γωνίας`, preserving the Greek root `γων-` (`γωνία` / `ἀκρογωνιαῖος`) across both texts.

### 4. Habakkuk 2:4 (+ Habakkuk 2:3 & Isaiah 26:20) → Romans 1:17; Galatians 3:11; Hebrews 10:37–39

#### (a) Attested Textual Evidence (MT vs. LXX)
* **Hebrew MT (Habakkuk 2:4)**:
  `הִנֵּ֣ה עֻפְּלָ֔ה לֹא־ יָשְׁרָ֥ה נַפְשׁ֖וֹ בּ֑וֹ וְצַדִּ֖יק בֶּאֱמוּנָת֥וֹ יִחְיֶֽה׃`
  * Tokens: `הִנֵּה` (pos 1, `H2009`), `עֻפְּלָה` (pos 2, `H6075`, "is swollen/puffed up" — Pual perf. 3fs), `לֹא־` (pos 3, `H3808`), `יָשְׁרָה` (pos 4, `H3474`, "is upright"), `נַפְשׁוֹ` (pos 5, `H5315`, "his soul" — 3ms suffix), `בּוֹ` (pos 6, `H9033`, "in him" — 3ms suffix), `וְצַדִּיק` (pos 7, `H6662`, "and [the] righteous"), `בֶּאֱמוּנָתוֹ` (pos 8, `H0530`, "by his faithfulness/steadfastness" — 3ms suffix), `יִחְיֶה` (pos 9, `H2421`, "he will live").
* **Greek LXX (Habacuc 2:4, Rahlfs)**:
  `ἐὰν ὑποστείληται, οὐκ εὐδοκεῖ ἡ ψυχή μου ἐν αὐτῷ· ὁ δὲ δίκαιος ἐκ πίστεώς μου ζήσεται.`

#### (b) Linguistic & Theological Shifts (MT → LXX)
Every clause of Habakkuk 2:4 undergoes a major shift in the Septuagint:
1. **`עֻפְּלָה` (`H6075`) → `ἐὰν ὑποστείληται` ("if he shrinks back")**:
   * `H6075` (`עָפַל`, "to swell/presume") occurs only twice in the OT: Num 14:44 (`διαβιάζομαι`, "acted presumptuously") and Hab 2:4 (`ὑποστέλλω`, "draw back/shrink back", stability `0.956`). The LXX converts MT's declarative accusation of arrogance (`הִנֵּה עֻפְּלָה`, "behold, it is puffed up") into a conditional warning against cowardice/retreat (`ἐὰν ὑποστείληται`). *(Interpretive hypothesis: possibly reading `עֻפְּלָה` via a different root/Vorlage such as `עָלַף` "faint" or `עָפֵל` "sluggish/timid", or contextualizing to Hab 2:3's wait for the vision).*
2. **`לֹא־יָשְׁרָה נַפְשׁוֹ בּוֹ` → `οὐκ εὐδοκεῖ ἡ ψυχή μου ἐν αὐτῷ`**:
   * **Lexical shift (`יָשְׁרָה` `H3474` → `εὐδοκεῖ`)**: `H3474` ("to be straight/upright") is normally rendered `ἀρέσκω` (6x), `εὐθύς` (5x), or `κατευθύνω` (5x); **only in Habakkuk 2:4** is it rendered `εὐδοκέω` (`p_g_given_h = 0.037`, `g_idf = 9.02`), transforming "his soul is not upright in him" into "my soul takes no pleasure in him."
   * **Pronoun Person Shifts (`3ms` → `1sg`)**: query module documents three `pronoun_person_shift` transformations in Hab 2:4:
     * `נַפְשׁוֹ` ("his soul", 3ms) → `ἡ ψυχή μου` ("my soul", 1sg — transferring the `ψυχή` from the arrogant person to God!).
     * `בּוֹ` ("in him/it", 3ms) combined with `נַפְשׁוֹ` realignment → `ἐν αὐτῷ` ("in him", referring to the one who shrinks back).
     * `בֶּאֱמוּנָתוֹ` ("by his faithfulness", 3ms) → `ἐκ πίστεώς μου` ("by my faithfulness / faith in me", 1sg).
   * In `H0530` (`אֱמוּנָה`), query module shows `ἀλήθεια` (22x) and `πίστις` (19x, stability `0.995`) as top equivalents; Hab 2:4 uses `πίστις`, shifting the grammatical person of the pronominal suffix from 3ms (the righteous person's steadfastness) to 1sg (God's faithfulness, or faith directed toward God).
3. **Antecedent Context: Habakkuk 2:3 (`MT → LXX`)**:
   * **MT**: `כִּי־בֹא יָבֹא לֹא יְאַחֵר` ("for it [the vision, `חָזוֹן`] will surely come; it will not delay").
   * **LXX**: `ὅτι ἐρχόμενος ἥξει καὶ οὐ μὴ χρονίσῃ` — `וְיָפֵחַ` (`H6315`) → `ἀνατελεῖ` (`p_g_given_h = 0.0714`), `יְכַזֵּב` (`H3576`) → `κενόν` (`p_g_given_h = 0.0588`), and `בֹא יָבֹא` (`H0935`) → `ἐρχόμενος ἥξει` (`p_g_given_h = 0.0749`). Critically, the masculine participle `ἐρχόμενος` (agreeing grammatically in Hebrew with masc. `חָזוֹן` "vision", or impersonalized in Greek as "the coming one" — contrasted with feminine `ὅρασις` in LXX v. 3a!) enables a **personal messianic reading** ("the Coming One will come").

#### (c) New Testament Citations & Divergences
1. **Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11**:
   * Both quote only the second clause: `ὁ δὲ δίκαιος ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται` (Rom 1:17 has `Ὁ δὲ δίκαιος ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται`; Gal 3:11 omits `δέ` inside `ὅτι Ὁ δίκαιος ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται`).
   * **Divergence from both MT and Rahlfs LXX**: Neither Paul's Romans 1:17 nor Galatians 3:11 contains `μου` (LXX `ἐκ πίστεώς μου`) or `αὐτοῦ` (rendering MT 3ms `בֶּאֱמוּנָתוֹ`). Both read `ἐκ πίστεως` without any possessive pronoun.
   * **Framing adaptation in Rom 1:17**: Paul introduces the quote with `δικαιοσύνη γὰρ Θεοῦ ἐν αὐτῷ ἀποκαλύπτεται ἐκ πίστεως εἰς πίστιν`, hooking directly into `δίκαιος` (`δικαιοσύνη`) and `ἐκ πίστεως` from Hab 2:4.
2. **Hebrews 10:37–39**:
   * **Hebrews 10:37 (`Composite Splice`: Isaiah 26:20 LXX + Habakkuk 2:3 LXX)**:
     * `ἔτι γὰρ μικρὸν ὅσον ὅσον, ὁ ἐρχόμενος ἥξει καὶ οὐ χρονίσει`
     * **Isaiah 26:20**: MT has `חֲבִי כִמְעַט־רֶגַע` ("hide for a little moment"); LXX Isaias 26:20 has `ἀποκρύβηθι μικρὸν ὅσον ὅσον` ("hide for a very little while"). Hebrews 10:37 splices `μικρὸν ὅσον ὅσον` from LXX Isa 26:20 directly after `ἔτι [γὰρ]` from LXX Hab 2:3 (`διότι ἔτι ὅρασις...`).
     * **Habakkuk 2:3 LXX**: Hebrews adopts LXX's `ἐρχόμενος ἥξει καὶ οὐ ... χρονίσει` (against MT's impersonal `חָזוֹן` "vision"), **adding the definite article `ὁ`** (`ὁ ἐρχόμενος`, "the Coming One") to make the messianic/parousia subject explicit, and changing LXX's aorist subjunctive `οὐ μὴ χρονίσῃ` to simple future indicative `οὐ χρονίσει` (mirroring the mood shift in Rom 9:33 `οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται`).
   * **Hebrews 10:38 (Follows LXX against MT + Clause Inversion + Pronoun Transposition)**:
     * `ὁ δὲ δίκαιός μου ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται, καὶ ἐὰν ὑποστείληται, οὐκ εὐδοκεῖ ἡ ψυχή μου ἐν αὐτῷ.`
     * **Follows LXX against MT**: Retains all three distinctive LXX Hab 2:4 shifts—`ὑποστείληται` (for MT `עֻפְּלָה`), `οὐκ εὐδοκεῖ ἡ ψυχή μου` (for MT `לֹא־יָשְׁרָה נַפְשׁוֹ`), and the 1sg pronoun `μου`.
     * **Clause Inversion**: In both MT and Rahlfs LXX Habakkuk 2:4, the negative/arrogant/shrinking-back clause comes **first**, and `וְצַדִּיק` / `ὁ δὲ δίκαιος` comes **second**. Hebrews 10:38 **reverses the two halves**, placing `ὁ δὲ δίκαιός μου ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται` first and `καὶ ἐὰν ὑποστείληται...` second.
     * **Pronoun Transposition**: Rahlfs LXX reads `ὁ δὲ δίκαιος ἐκ πίστεώς μου ζήσεται` (`μου` modifying `πίστεως`, "my faithfulness"); Hebrews 10:38 reads `ὁ δὲ δίκαιός μου ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται` (`μου` modifying `δίκαιος`, "my righteous one"). *(Note: textual witnesses such as Codex Alexandrinus of LXX Hab 2:4 also place `μου` after `δίκαιος`, so this may reflect an existing Greek textual variant rather than an independent alteration by Hebrews).*
   * **Hebrews 10:39 (Rhetorical Echo / Adaptation)**:
     * `ἡμεῖς δὲ οὐκ ἐσμὲν ὑποστολῆς εἰς ἀπώλειαν ἀλλὰ πίστεως εἰς περιποίησιν ψυχῆς`
     * Reuses the nominal cognate `ὑποστολή` (`G5289`, NT hapax legomenon, echoing LXX Hab 2:4 `ὑποστείληται`, `G5288`—whose only other NT occurrences are Acts 20:20, 27 and Gal 2:12), `πίστις` (`G4102`), and `ψυχή` (`G5590`), flipping the clause order back so that shrinking back comes first (negated) and faith/soul-preservation ends the pericope.

## Physical & Architectural Analogies vs. Theological Claims

* **Masonry / Temple Building Domain (`Isa 28:16`, `Ps 118:22`, `1 Pet 2:4–8`)**: In the physical domain, `אֶבֶן` / `λίθος` denotes a cut building stone; `מוּסָד` / `θεμέλιον` denotes a structural foundation; `פִּנָּה` / `ἀκρογωνιαῖος` / `κεφαλὴ γωνίας` denotes a primary corner/bonding stone or capstone evaluated by builders (`הַבּוֹנִים` / `οἱ οἰκοδομοῦντες`). `1 Peter 2:4–5` explicitly signals metaphorical/analogical transfer (`λίθον ζῶντα`, `ὡς λίθοι ζῶντες οἰκοδομεῖσθε οἶκος πνευματικός`).
* **Obstacle / Hunting Domain (`Isa 8:14`, `Rom 9:32–33`, `1 Pet 2:8`)**: `אֶבֶן נֶגֶף` / `λίθος προσκόμματος` and `צוּר מִכְשׁוֹל` / `πέτρα σκανδάλου` (`פַּח` / `מוֹקֵשׁ`) belong to physical tripping hazards and bird/animal traps. In `Isa 8:14`, `Rom 9:32–33`, and `1 Pet 2:8`, these physical hazards serve as analogies for encountering divine action or "stumbling at the word" (`προσκόπτουσιν τῷ λόγῳ`).

## Interpretive Hypotheses (Explicitly Separated from Attested Evidence)

1. **Shared Christian Stone *Testimonia* vs. Literary Dependence (`Rom 9:33` & `1 Pet 2:6–8`)**: Because *both* Romans 9:33 and 1 Peter 2:6 open Isaiah 28:16 with the identical non-LXX phrase `Ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον` and *both* render Isaiah 8:14 as `λίθος/ν προσκόμματος καὶ πέτρα/ν σκανδάλου` (following MT `מִכְשׁוֹל` with `σκάνδαλον` instead of LXX `πτώματι`, and dropping LXX's negative `οὐχ`), it is a plausible hypothesis that either (a) a pre-Pauline/early-Christian Greek stone *testimonium* already harmonized Isa 28:16 and Isa 8:14 toward the Hebrew, or (b) 1 Peter drew directly on Romans 9:33 while expanding the chain with 1 Pet 2:6's fuller LXX Isa 28:16 and 1 Pet 2:7's Ps 118:22.
2. **Paul's Omission of `μου` in Habakkuk 2:4 (`Rom 1:17`; `Gal 3:11`)**: Paul may have omitted `μου` either because his Greek Vorlage lacked it, or intentionally to leave `ἐκ πίστεως` open to his Christ-faith / faith-righteousness antithesis (`ἐκ πίστεως` vs. `ἐξ ἔργων νόμου`).
3. **Hebrews 10:37–38 Clause Inversion & Messianic Re-reading**: By splicing `μικρὸν ὅσον ὅσον` (Isa 26:20 LXX) with `ὁ ἐρχόμενος` (Hab 2:3 LXX + definite article) and reversing the two clauses of Hab 2:4 LXX, the author of Hebrews restructures an exhortation about a coming *vision* into a two-stage eschatological choice: faithful endurance leading to life (transitioning into Heb 11's `πίστις` catalog) vs. apostate shrinking back (`ὑποστολή`).
