### 2.2 Participant Roles

1. **The Travelers / Guests:**
   - *Genesis 18–19:* Yahweh (Gen 18) and two divine messengers (*mal'akim*, Gen 19) who come to inspect Sodom's outcry, enforce moral judgment, and rescue the host's household.

2. **The Hosts:**
   - *Genesis 18 (Abraham):* Ideal covenantal host—eager, attentive, self-effacing, creating life-giving blessing (promise of Isaac).
   - *Genesis 19 (Lot):* Compromised yet courageous host in a wicked city—insists on sheltering strangers and risks his own person to bar the door, though morally compromised in offering his daughters.
   - *Judges 19 (Bethlehemite Father-in-Law & Ephraimite Old Man):* The father-in-law displays suffocating/delaying hospitality (Judg 19:4–9) that indirectly precipitates the nighttime crisis; the Ephraimite old man mirrors Lot's status as an outsider-host (*ger*) and repeats Lot's tragic counter-offer, yet lacks Lot's divine protection.
3. **The Aggressors:**
   - *Genesis 19:* The entire male populace of Sodom, a Canaanite/Gentile city paradigmatic for wickedness (Gen 13:13; 18:20).
   - *Judges 19:* The "sons of Belial" (*bene beliyya'al*) of Gibeah—members of the covenant tribe of Benjamin, inside Israel's promised land.
4. **The Women:**
   - *Genesis 19:* Lot's two virgin daughters are shielded from the mob by angelic intervention (though they later enact an inverted, desperate preservation of line via incest in the cave, Gen 19:30–38).
   - *Judges 19:* Two women are offered (the host's virgin daughter and the Levite's concubine); the concubine alone is thrust out, brutalized, left mute, and dismembered—making her both victim and silent summons to civil war.

### 2.3 Hospitality and Moral Inversions

- **(e) Interpretive Hypothesis — "Sodomization" of Israel:** Judges 19 does not merely imitate Genesis 19; it *inverts* its ethnic and moral geography. In Judg 19:11–12, the Levite refuses to lodge in Jebus because it is "a city of foreigners, who are not of the people of Israel," trusting instead in Israelite Gibeah. Yet Gibeah behaves *worse* than Sodom:
  1. **Refusal of Native Hospitality:** In Sodom, Lot at least intercepts the visitors at the gate before nightfall; in Gibeah, *no native Benjaminite* offers shelter, leaving the travelers stranded in the public square (*rechov*).
  2. **Loss of Guest-Protection:** In Gen 19, the guests rescue the host and his family; in Judg 19, the guest (the Levite) betrays his own dependent (his concubine) to preserve himself, and the host's moral failure is compounded by the guest's callousness (19:25–28).
  3. **Absence of Divine Rescue & Redemptive Intercession:** In Gen 18:16–33, Abraham intercedes for Sodom's righteous; in Gen 19:29, God remembers Abraham and rescues Lot. In Judges 19–21, Yahweh is largely silent during the atrocity, and Israel's subsequent vengeance escalates into near-annihilation of Benjamin and further sexual abductions (Judg 21).
  4. **Over-Hospitality as Hazard:** Even the father-in-law's lavish "strengthen your heart" hospitality (Judg 19:5–9) is structurally inverted: instead of blessing the journey like Abraham's hospitality in Gen 18, it traps the travelers on the road at dusk.

### 3. Participant Role Alignments & Testing Inversions

1. **Israel as Son (`Deut 8:5`) ↔ Jesus as Son of God (`Matt 4:3, 6`)**:
   - In `Deut 8:5`, YHWH's wilderness testing is explicitly framed as parental discipline: "as a man disciplines his son (`אֶת־בְּנוֹ` / `τὸν υἱὸν αὐτοῦ`), so YHWH your God disciplines you." Jesus enters the wilderness immediately after the baptismal declaration ("This is my beloved Son," `Matt 3:17`), and both conditional challenges begin `εἰ υἱὸς εἶ τοῦ θεοῦ` ("If you are the Son of God").
2. **Double Inversion of "Testing" (`נָסָה` `H5254` / `πειράζω` `G3985` / `ἐκπειράζω` `G1598`)**:
   - Lexicon and syntactic-object analysis of `H5254` (`נָסָה`) documents two sharply opposed grammatical frames:
     - **God tests Israel** (`Gen 22:1; Exod 15:25; 16:4; Deut 8:2, 16`) to uncover what is in the heart (`לֵבָב` / `καρδία`) and whether they will keep his commandments.
     - **Israel tests YHWH/El** (`נָסָה` with direct object `יְהוָה` `H3068`, $27.35\text{ bits}$, $p_{\text{null}} = 5.86 \times 10^{-9}$, e.g., `Exod 17:2; Deut 6:16`; and direct object `אֵל` `H0410`, $23.55\text{ bits}$, $p_{\text{null}} = 8.14 \times 10^{-8}$, e.g., `Ps 78:18; 106:14`)—wordplay-linked to `מַסָּה` (`H4532`, $26.42\text{ bits}$, $p_{\text{null}} = 1.12 \times 10^{-8}$).
   - **Inversion in Matthew 4**: The Spirit leads Jesus to be tested (`Matt 4:1`) in continuity with `Deut 8:2` (divine probation of the Son), yet the *agent* executing the test is the devil (`ὁ πειράζων`), who tries to coax Jesus into committing the *opposite* sin—putting God to the test (`ἐκπειράζω`, `Matt 4:7`) like Israel at Massah.
3. **Inversion of Provision, Rock/Stone, & Pisgah**:
   - **Bread & Stone**: In `Deut 8:9, 15–16`, the wilderness lacks bread (`לֶחֶם`), God feeds with manna from heaven and water from flint rock (`צוּר`), and the promised land's stones (`אֲבָנִים`) are iron. In `Matt 4:3`, Satan proposes turning wilderness stones (`λίθοι`) into bread (`ἄρτοι`) by fiat—bypassing God's mouth (`στόμα θεοῦ`).
   - **Pisgah (`Deut 34:1–4`) vs. High Mountain (`Matt 4:8–9`)**: On Mount Nebo/Pisgah (`Deut 34:1`), **YHWH shows** (`וַיַּרְאֵהוּ` / LXX `ἔδειξεν` `G1166`) Moses *all the land* (`כָּל־הָאָרֶץ` / `πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν`) promised by oath; on the exceedingly high mountain (`Matt 4:8`), **the devil shows** (`δείκνυσιν` `G1166`) Jesus *all the kingdoms of the world* (`πάσας τὰς βασιλείας τοῦ κόσμου`), inverting divine inheritance-viewing into idolatrous usurpation.

### 4. Intermediate Bridge Passages (Even Where Surface Vocabulary Differs)

1. **Exodus 16:4, 8 & Exodus 17:1–7 (Historical Wilderness Origin Bridges)**:
   - `Exod 16:4` ("that I may test them [`נָסָה`], whether they will walk in my law or not") and `Exod 17:2, 7` (quarreling over thirst and asking, "Is YHWH among us or not?", naming the place `מַסָּה` `H4532`) are the concrete historical narratives behind `Deut 8:3` and `Deut 6:16`. Notably, `Exod 17:7`'s question *"Is YHWH in our midst, or not?"* structurally anticipates Satan's *"If you are the Son of God..."* (`Matt 4:3, 6`).
2. **Exodus 34:28; Deuteronomy 9:9; 1 Kings 19:8 (The 40-Day Fasting & Mountain Bridges)**:
   - `Deuteronomy 6–8` mentions *40 years* (`Deut 8:2, 4`), whereas `Matthew 4:2` specifies *40 days and 40 nights* of fasting. Tri-textual analysis surfaces **Deuteronomy 9:9**, **Exodus 34:28**, and **1 Kings 19:8** as the exact bridging texts:
     - `Exod 34:28` and `Deut 9:9`: Moses stays on the mountain `אַרְבָּעִים יוֹם וְאַרְבָּעִים לַיְלָה` (LXX `τεσσαράκοντα ἡμέρας καὶ τεσσαράκοντα νύκτας`, exact match to `Matt 4:2`), eating no bread and drinking no water (`לֶחֶם לֹא אָכַל וּמַיִם לֹא שָׁתָה`), receiving the words written on stone tablets (`לֻחֹת הָאֲבָנִים` / `πλάκας λιθίνας`).
     - `1 Kings 19:8`: Elijah is ministered to by an angel (`1 Kgs 19:5–7`, cf. `Matt 4:11`) and walks `אַרְבָּעִים יוֹם וְאַרְבָּעִים לַיְלָה` to Horeb.
3. **Psalms 95:6–9 (LXX 94:6–9), Psalms 78:17–18, Psalms 106:14, & Numbers 14:22 (Poetic & Liturgical Bridges)**:
   - Where `Deut 6:13` lacks the verb `חוה` (`H7812`, "bow down / worship", LXX `προσκυνέω` `G4352`), **Psalm 95:6–9** unites all three temptation motifs in a single liturgical sequence:
     - `Ps 95:6`: `נִשְׁתַּחֲוֶה וְנִכְרָעָה` → LXX `προσκυνήσωμεν καὶ προσπέσωμεν` ("let us worship [`προσκυνέω`] and fall down [`προσπίπτω`]", matching `πεσὼν προσκυνήσῃς` in `Matt 4:9` and `προσκυνήσεις` in `Matt 4:10`).
     - `Ps 95:8–9`: Recalls the day of `מַסָּה` (`LXX Πειρασμοῦ`) in the wilderness (`בַּמִּדְבָּר` / `ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ`) where the fathers tested (`נָסָה` / `ἐπείρασαν`) God.
   - `Ps 78:18` and `Ps 106:14` similarly bridge `Exod 16–17` and `Deut 6–8` by linking craving/food in the wilderness with `נָסָה` ("testing God").
4. **Psalms 91:11–12; Nehemiah 11:1; Esther 1:4; Daniel 3:6; Deuteronomy 10:20; 1 Samuel 7:3 (Composite Splice & Worship Bridges)**:
   - `Nehemiah 11:1` provides the exact LXX/MT collocation `τὴν ἁγίαν πόλιν` ("the holy city", `Matt 4:5`); `Psalms 91:11–12` supplies the angelic protection text misapplied by Satan (`Matt 4:6`, omitting `τοῦ διαφυλάξαι σε ἐν πάσαις ταῖς ὁδοῖς σου`); `Esther 1:4` (LXX `δείκνυσιν ... βασιλείας`) and `Daniel 3:6` ("fall down and worship") furnish the imperial-display and coerced-prostration idiom of `Matt 4:8–9`; and `1 Samuel 7:3` / `Deuteronomy 10:20` bridge `Deut 6:13` to exclusive service (`αὐτῷ μόνῳ λατρεύσεις` in `Matt 4:10`).
5. **Genesis 3:1–6 (Interpretive Typological Bridge)**:
   - Surfaced as a candidate background source in `Matt 4:1–4` (query module), `Genesis 3:1–6` offers a thematic/typological parallel (interpretive hypothesis rather than direct quotation): an adversary (`נָחָשׁ` / `διάβολος`) tests obedience through food (`אָכַל` / bread) and the desire of the eyes, contrasting Adam's fall with Christ's wilderness victory.
