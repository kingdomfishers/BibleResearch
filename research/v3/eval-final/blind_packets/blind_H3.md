## 2. Textual & MT–LXX Witness Profile of Ezekiel 47:1–12

### 2.1 Narrative Architecture of the Pericope
* **Verses 1–2 (Origin & Exit):** The guide returns Ezekiel to the temple entrance (`פֶּתַח הַבַּיִת`, Ezek 47:1 pos. 3–4; `H6607`, `H1004`), where waters issue eastward from beneath the temple threshold (`מִתַּחַת מִפְתַּן הַבַּיִת קָדִימָה`, v. 1 pos. 8–11; `H8478`, `H4670`, `H1004`, `H6921`)—specifically descending from under the right/south flank of the house, south of the altar (`מִכֶּתֶף הַבַּיִת הַיְמָנִית מִנֶּגֶב לַמִּזְבֵּחַ`, v. 1 pos. 19–23). Because the outer east gate is shut (cf. Ezek 44:1–2), the guide leads Ezekiel out via the north gate (`שַׁעַר צָפוֹנָה`, v. 2 pos. 3–4) around to the outer east gate, where waters are trickling (`מְפַכִּים`, v. 2 pos. 16; `H6379`) on the south/right shoulder (`מִן־הַכָּתֵף הַיְמָנִית`, v. 2 pos. 17–19).
* **Verses 3–5 (Four Measurements & Exponential Depth):** A man with a measuring line (`וְקָו בְּיָדוֹ`, v. 3 pos. 4–5; `H6957`, `H3027`) measures four successive increments of **1,000 cubits** (`אֶלֶף בָּאַמָּה`, `H0505`, `H0520`; 4,000 cubits total, ~2 km), leading the prophet across (`וַיַּעֲבִרֵנִי`, `H5674`) at each stage:
  * 1,000 cubits: ankle-deep (`מֵי אָפְסָיִם`, v. 3 pos. 11–12; `H0657`)
  * 2,000 cubits: knee-deep (`מַיִם בִּרְכָּיִם`, v. 4 pos. 5–6; `H1290`)
  * 3,000 cubits: waist/loin-deep (`מֵי מָתְנָיִם`, v. 4 pos. 10–11; `H4975`)
  * 4,000 cubits: risen (`גָאוּ`, v. 5 pos. 9; `H1342`) into waters of swimming (`מֵי שָׂחוּ`, v. 5 pos. 11–12; `H7813`), a wadi/river (`נַחַל`, `H5158`) that cannot be crossed on foot (`אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יֵעָבֵר`, v. 5 pos. 14–16).
* **Verses 6–12 (Ecological & Healing Transformation):** Returned to the river bank (`שְׂפַת הַנַּחַל`, v. 6 pos. 8–9; `H8193`, `H5158`), Ezekiel sees very many trees on both banks (`מִזֶּה וּמִזֶּה`, v. 7 pos. 9–10). The waters flow toward the eastern circuit (`הַגְּלִילָה הַקַּדְמוֹנָה`, v. 8 pos. 7–8; `H1552`, `H6930`), descend into the Arabah (`הָעֲרָבָה`, v. 8 pos. 11; `H6160`), and enter the sea (`הַיָּמָּה הַמּוּצָאִים`, v. 8 pos. 15–16), healing its waters (`וְנִרְפּוּ הַמָּיִם`, v. 8 pos. 17–18; `H7495`). Swarming living creatures and abundant fish fill the waters from En-gedi to En-eglaim (vv. 9–10), while swamps and marshes (`בִּצֹּאתָיו וּגְבָאָיו`, v. 11 pos. 1–2; `H1207`, `H1360`) remain unhealed, given over to salt (`לְמֶלַח נִתָּנוּ`, v. 11 pos. 5–6; `H4417`). Along both banks grow every fruit-bearing food tree (`כָּל־עֵץ־מַאֲכָל`, v. 12 pos. 8–10) bearing monthly first-fruits (`לָחֳדָשָׁיו יְבַכֵּר`, v. 12 pos. 17–18; `H2320`, `H1069`), with non-withering leaves (`לֹא־יִבּוֹל עָלֵהוּ`, v. 12 pos. 11–13; `H5034`, `H5929`) for healing (`לִתְרוּפָה`, v. 12 pos. 29; `H8644`).

### 2.2 Unexpected MT vs. LXX Divergences
Direct comparison between the Hebrew MT and the Greek Septuagint (`Ezechiel 47`) exposes four significant textual variants:

1. **Ezekiel 47:8 (`הַגְּלִילָה הַקַּדְמוֹנָה` vs. `τὴν Γαλειλαίαν τὴν πρὸς ἀνατολάς`):** Whereas the Hebrew uses `גְּלִילָה` (`H1552`, "region/circuit") for the eastern Judean-Jordan district, the LXX renders it as a proper toponym, *"Galilee toward the east"* (`Γαλειλαίαν`). Similarly, MT's passive/Hophal participle `הַיָּמָּה הַמּוּצָאִים` ("into the sea, the brought-out [waters]") becomes in LXX `ἐπὶ τὸ ὕδωρ τῆς διεκβολῆς` ("to the water of the outlet").
2. **Ezekiel 47:9 (Dual `נַחֲלַיִם` vs. Singular `ὁ ποταμός`):** MT pos. 12 attests the **dual** form `נַחֲלַיִם` (*naḥălayim*, "two rivers/double stream") in the first clause before reverting to singular `הַנָּחַל` at pos. 29. The LXX harmonizes both occurrences to singular `ὁ ποταμός` (pos. 17 and 41). Whether MT's dual reflects a split stream (cf. Zech 14:8), a double-banked torrent, or an early scribal orthographic reading (`-ayim` vs. `-ām`), the dual form is firmly attested in the MT.
3. **Ezekiel 47:11 (Two Marshes vs. Three Prepositional Nouns):** MT has two plural nouns with 3ms suffixes—`בִּצֹּאתָיו וּגְבָאָיו` ("its swamps and its marshes")—whereas LXX expands into three distinct nouns: `καὶ ἐν τῇ διεκβολῇ αὐτοῦ καὶ ἐν τῇ ἐπιστροφῇ αὐτοῦ καὶ ἐν τῇ ὑπεράρσει αὐτοῦ` ("at its outlet, and at its turning/return, and at its overflowing/elevation").
4. **Ezekiel 47:12 (`וְעָלֵהוּ לִתְרוּפָה` vs. `καὶ ἀνάβασις αὐτῶν εἰς ὑγίειαν`):** MT reads *"and its leaf (`עָלֵהוּ`, pos. 28) for healing (`לִתְרוּפָה`, pos. 29)."* The LXX instead translates `ἀνάβασις αὐτῶν` ("their ascending/growth")—likely reading a form of `עלה` ("to go up/grow") rather than `עָלֶה` ("leaf")—and renders the *hapax legomenon* `תְּרוּפָה` with `ὑγίειαν` ("health"). Crucially, **Revelation 22:2** (`καὶ τὰ φύλλα τοῦ ξύλου εἰς θεραπείαν τῶν ἐθνῶν`) agrees with the **MT** ("the leaves," `τὰ φύλλα` = `עָלֵהוּ`) against LXX's `ἀνάβασις`, while translating `תְּרוּפָה` with `θεραπείαν` rather than LXX's `ὑγίειαν`.

## 3. Architectural Connections: The Missing Bronze Sea & The Glory Threshold

### 3.1 The Exact Coordinate of Solomon's Molten Sea (1 Kgs 7:39; 2 Chr 4:10)
* **Attested Textual & Architectural Evidence:** Cross-book lemma alignment links **Ezekiel 47:1** directly with **1 Kings 7:39** and **2 Chronicles 4:10** through a rare spatial-architectural formula:
  * **Ezekiel 47:1 (pos. 19–22):** `מִכֶּתֶף הַבַּיִת הַיְמָנִית מִנֶּגֶב` (`H3802` shoulder/side + `H1004` house + `H3233` right/south + `H5045` south).
  * **1 Kings 7:39 (pos. 15–22):** `וְאֶת־הַיָּם נָתַן מִכֶּתֶף הַבַּיִת הַיְמָנִית קֵדְמָה מִמּוּל נֶגֶב` ("And the Sea he put on the right shoulder of the house, eastward, facing south"; `H3220`, `H3802`, `H1004`, `H3225`, `H6924`, `H5045`).
  * **2 Chronicles 4:10 (pos. 2–8):** `וְאֶת־הַיָּם נָתַן מִכֶּתֶף הַיְמָנִית קֵדְמָה מִמּוּל נֶגְבָּה` (`H3220`, `H3802`, `H3233`, `H6924`, `H5045`).
* **Architectural Contrast (`sacred_architecture`):** In Solomon's First Temple, the cast-bronze **Molten Sea** (`הַיָּם הַמּוּצָק`, 10 × 5 × 30 cubits, holding 2,000 baths atop 12 bronze oxen) stood at the exact southeast corner of the house (`מִכֶּתֶף הַבַּיִת הַיְמָנִית`, 1 Kgs 7:39). In Ezekiel's visionary temple (Ezek 40–48), **no Bronze Sea (and no Ark) is present**. Instead, at that identical southeast coordinate (`מִכֶּתֶף הַבַּיִת הַיְמָנִית מִנֶּגֶב לַמִּזְבֵּחַ`, Ezek 47:1), living waters issue *from under the temple itself* and flow out to heal the actual **Salt Sea** (`הַיָּם`, Ezek 47:8).
* **Interpretive Hypothesis:** Ezekiel's temple replaces the static bronze reservoir of Solomon's court with a dynamic, self-multiplying source that flows outward—shifting temple water from priestly containment inside the court to cosmic renewal outside the sanctuary.

### 3.2 The Intra-Ezekiel Threshold Itinerary (`מִפְתָּן`, `H4670`) & The Voice of Many Waters
* **Attested Occurrences (`H4670`):** The noun `מִפְתָּן` (*miphtān*, "threshold") occurs in only **8 verses** across the entire Hebrew Bible, **5 of which are in Ezekiel**:
  1. **1 Samuel 5:4–5** (2×): Dagon falls headless and handless on the *miphtān* before the Ark of YHWH.
  2. **Ezekiel 9:3** (`וּכְבוֹד אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל נַעֲלָה מֵעַל הַכְּרוּב ... אֶל מִפְתַּן הַבָּיִת`) and **Ezekiel 10:4, 18** (3×): YHWH's glory rises from the cherub to the **threshold of the house** (`מִפְתַּן הַבָּיִת`) before departing out the east gate (10:19).
  3. **Ezekiel 46:2**: The prince worships at the *miphtān* of the outer east gate.
  4. **Ezekiel 47:1**: Living water flows out *from beneath* `מִפְתַּן הַבַּיִת` eastward.
  5. **Zephaniah 1:9**: Judgment on those who leap over the *miphtān* (echoing 1 Sam 5:5).
* **Return of the Glory (`Ezekiel 43:2–4`):** When YHWH's glory returns from the east (`מִדֶּרֶךְ הַקָּדִים`, 43:2) through the east gate (`דֶּרֶךְ שַׁעַר אֲשֶׁר פָּנָיו דֶּרֶךְ הַקָּדִים`, 43:4), *"his voice was like the sound of many waters"* (`וְקוֹלוֹ כְּקוֹל מַיִם רַבִּים`, 43:2 pos. 8–11).
* **Structural & Rhetorical Synthesis:** The exact architectural threshold (`מִפְתַּן הַבַּיִת`) over which YHWH's glory hovered as it abandoned the defiled First Temple eastward (Ezek 9:3; 10:4, 18) becomes, following the glory's acoustic "many waters" return from the east (Ezek 43:2–4), the very threshold from beneath which real water pours eastward (Ezek 47:1). In effect, the auditory metaphor of 43:2 (*"sound of many waters"*) materializes as the physical river of 47:1–12.

### 4.2 The Tyre–Dead Sea Reversal (`מִשְׁטוֹחַ לַחֲרָמִים`, Ezek 26:5, 14 ↔ 47:10)
* **Attested Textual & Rhetorical Parallel:** The phrase *"place for the spreading of nets"* (`H4894` + `H2764`) appears nowhere in Scripture outside Ezekiel, linking three verses:
  * **Ezekiel 26:5, 14:** Maritime **Tyre**, thriving in the midst of the Mediterranean ("Great Sea"), is sentenced to ruin: *"She will be a place for the spreading of nets (`מִשְׁטַח חֲרָמִים`) in the middle of the sea"* (26:5; cf. 26:14).
  * **Ezekiel 47:10:** The formerly lifeless **Salt Sea** becomes *"a place for the spreading of nets (`מִשְׁטוֹחַ לַחֲרָמִים`)"* with fish *"like the fish of the Great Sea (`כִּדְגַת הַיָּם הַגָּדוֹל`), exceedingly many."*
* **Lexical Distinction on `H2764` (`חֵרֶם`):** Occurrences of `H2764` across 31 OT verses span two distinct homographic senses under the same Strong's code: (a) *fishing net* (`Ezek 26:5, 14; 32:3; 47:10; Eccl 7:26; Hab 1:15–17`) and (b) *devoted thing / ban of total destruction* (`Lev 27:21, 28–29; Josh 6–7; Ezek 44:29; Zech 14:11`). Ezekiel 47:10 strictly uses sense (a) to invert Tyre's marine curse into Dead Sea abundance.

Ezekiel 47:9–10 thus exhibits a **unique lexical lock** to the Fifth Day of Creation.

(Lexical root relation does not prove contextual punning, though the juxtaposition at the chapter-seam 47:12/13 is structurally striking.)

## 5. Geographical, Physical & Hydrological Connections

### 5.1 Topographical Gradient: Temple Mount → Kidron/Wadi en-Nar → Arabah → Salt Sea
* **Attested Coordinates & Elevations:**
  * **Jerusalem Temple Mount & Kidron (`sacred_architecture`):** The Temple Mount platform (~743 m) sits directly west of the Kidron Valley (~640–670 m) and Mount of Olives ridge (~815–826 m).
  * **Joel 3:18's Endpoint (`Valley of Shittim`):** Gazetteer data maps Joel 3:18's `נַחַל הַשִּׁטִּים` ("Valley/Wadi of Shittim") to **Wadi en-Nar** (`31.66715, 35.44856`), the lower Kidron drainage course descending southeast from Jerusalem through the Judean desert directly toward the Dead Sea.
  * **The Arabah & Salt Sea (`H6160`, `H4417`):** Ezekiel 47:8 specifies `הָעֲרָבָה` (`H6160`; Jordan-Dead Sea rift depression, with OpenBible/Theographic general Arabah marker at `30.416944, 35.151389` and Beth-arabah near the northern Dead Sea at `31.845782, 35.501718`). The Dead Sea sits at ~430 m below sea level—an over 1,100 m vertical drop from Jerusalem.
  * **En-gedi (`H5872`) to En-eglaim (`H5882`):** En-gedi (modern Ein Gedi, `31.45, 35.383333`) sits on the western Dead Sea shore; in **Joshua 15:62** it is grouped directly with **the City of Salt** (`עִיר־הַמֶּלַח`), and in **2 Chronicles 20:2** it is identified with **Hazazon Tamar** ("pruning of the palm"). En-eglaim (`H5882`) is a *hapax* whose exact site is **disputed** (OpenBible's #1 of 15 proposals places it at Mutraba, `31.28064, 35.533581`, on the eastern/Lisan sector of the Dead Sea—if so, spanning from En-gedi to En-eglaim sweeps across the breadth of the Dead Sea basin).

### 5.2 Physical & Chemical Inversion: Elisha's Salt Miracle (2 Kgs 2:19–22) vs. Ezekiel 47:8–11
* **Attested Textual Link (`intersect_words` `H4325` + `H7495`):** Searching all occurrences of `מַיִם` (water) + `רָפָא` (heal) within 2 verses yields only 14 verses, spotlighting a remarkable **physical and rhetorical inversion** between **2 Kings 2:21–22** and **Ezekiel 47:8–11**:
  * **2 Kings 2:21–22 (Jericho, lower Jordan/Arabah):** Elisha goes to the spring (`מוֹצָא הַמַּיִם`), throws **salt** (`מֶלַח`, `H4417`) into the bad water, and declares: *"I have healed these waters (`רִפִּאתִי לַמַּיִם הָאֵלֶּה`)... So the waters were healed (`וַיֵּרָפוּ הַמַּיִם`)."*
  * **Ezekiel 47:8–11 (Dead Sea, Arabah):** Instead of salt healing fresh spring water, fresh temple water (`הַמּוּצָאִים`, v. 8) heals the **Salt Sea** (`וְנִרְפּוּ הַמָּיִם` / `וְיֵרָפְאוּ`, vv. 8–9), while the shallow side-basins (`בִּצֹּאתָיו וּגְבָאָיו`, v. 11) are **left unhealed and given up to salt** (`לְמֶלַח נִתָּנוּ`).
* **Physical & Ecological Analogies:**
  1. **Hydrological Self-Multiplication:** Natural wadis (`נַחַל`) in the Judean desert are ephemeral (*achzav* brooks, cf. Jer 15:18, which also pairs `מַיִם` and `הֵרָפֵא`) and grow only by catching tributaries. Ezekiel's stream expands from a threshold trickle (`מְפַכִּים`) to an uncrossable river within 4,000 cubits (~2 km) *with zero tributaries*—a physically anomalous discharge sustained entirely by its sanctuary source (v. 12).
  2. **Salinity & Evaporative Salt Pans:** Hypersaline water (~30%+ salinity in the Dead Sea vs. ~3.5% in the Mediterranean, `הַיָּם הַגָּדוֹל`) suppresses macroscopic aquatic life. Turning the main basin freshwater/marine while isolating stagnant marshes (`בִּצֹּאתָיו וּגְבָאָיו`) *"for salt"* preserves solar evaporation salt flats—essential both economically and cultically, since Ezekiel's own altar liturgy requires **salt** on offerings (`Ezek 43:24`, cf. `Lev 2:13` "salt of the covenant"; external concept bridge `oewn:07825344-n`).
  3. **Anomalous Depth (`47:3–5`) vs. Ankle-Extremity (`אָפְסַיִם`, `H0657`):** Note that `H0657` (*ʾephes*) normally denotes *"end / extremity / nothingness"* (43 OT verses, e.g., `אַפְסֵי־אָרֶץ`, "ends of the earth" in Ps 2:8; 72:8), appearing in dual `אָפְסַיִם` only in Ezek 47:3 for the "extremities" (ankles/soles).

# System Y

# The Temple River of Ezekiel 47:1–12: Textual, Architectural, Lexical, Geographical, Physical, and Apocalyptic Connections Across Scripture

## 1. Scope and Epistemic Framework

This study examines **Ezekiel 47:1–12**—the visionary account of the stream emerging from the restored Jerusalem sanctuary and flowing eastward into the Dead Sea—across six analytical dimensions: textual structure, architectural-cultic placement, Hebrew and Greek lexical networks, historical geography, physical/hydrological analogies, and canonical-apocalyptic trajectory.

To preserve scholarly rigor and avoid conflating primary textual data with theological synthesis, every major claim in this report is classified under one of five evidentiary categories:

1. **(a) Attested Textual / Original-Language Evidence:** Direct readings in the Masoretic Text (MT), Septuagint (LXX), or canonical parallels as transmitted.
2. **(b) Linguistic / Grammatical Relationships:** Lexical roots, rare morphology, hapax legomena, and syntactic parallels across Biblical Hebrew and Septuagint/New Testament Greek.
3. **(c) Structural / Rhetorical Parallels:** Compositional framing, spatial symmetry, progression schemes, and intertextual patterning.
4. **(d) Physical / Hydrological & Ecological Analogies:** Comparisons with limestone karst hydrology, topographic gradients, Dead Sea evaporitic limnology, and riparian ecology—offered strictly as physical realia illuminating the text rather than naturalistic reductions of a prophetic vision.
5. **(e) Interpretive Hypotheses:** Theological, liturgical, or canonical-eschatological syntheses inferred from the intersection of (a)–(d).

## 2. Textual Architecture and Literary Placement

### 2.1 Compositional Seam in Ezekiel 40–48
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (c) Structural Parallel:** Ezekiel 47:1–12 occupies a pivotal hinge within the closing Temple Vision (Ezek 40–48). Immediately prior, Ezek 46:19–24 details the sacrificial boiling and baking kitchens at the corners of the courts; immediately after, Ezek 47:13–48:35 delineates the tribal boundaries and the new city (*YHWH šāmmāh*, "YHWH Is There," 48:35). Life-giving water must issue from the sanctuary to heal the eastern wasteland *before* the land is partitioned among the twelve tribes.

Moreover, the eastward trajectory of the river in 47:1–8 inversely mirrors the departure and return of the *kābôd* (glory of YHWH):
- **Departure (Ezek 9:3; 10:4, 18–19; 11:22–23):** The glory moves from the cherubim to the **threshold** (*mip̄tān*) of the house, then out through the **east gate**, pausing on the mountain east of the city (Mount of Olives).
- **Return (Ezek 43:1–5):** The glory returns from the east through the east gate and fills the house.
- **Overflow (Ezek 47:1–12):** Water now emerges from beneath that same **threshold** (*mip̄tān*, 47:1) and flows eastward toward the Jordan Rift and Dead Sea. **(e) Interpretive Hypothesis:** Where the *mip̄tān* previously marked the threshold of divine withdrawal and judicial slaughter (9:3–7), in 47:1 it becomes the fountainhead of cosmic renewal.

### 2.2 Textual Tensions and Versional Phenomena
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence:**
- **The Closed-Gate Detour (47:1–2 vs. 44:1–3):** In 47:1, the water flows eastward (*qādîmāh*) from beneath the threshold, yet in 47:2 the angelic guide leads Ezekiel out through the **north gate** (*dereḵ šaʿar ṣāp̄ôn*) and around the exterior to the outer east gate. Why does the prophet not walk straight out the east gate behind the water? Because Ezek 44:1–3 stipulates that the outer east gate must remain permanently shut (*sāḡûr yihyeh*) after YHWH entered by it. The vision maintains strict internal architectural consistency: the river passes *underneath* the threshold/gate complex, while human transit must respect the closed gate.
- **Singular vs. Dual River (47:9):** In MT 47:9, the text reads *kōl ʾăšer-yāḇôʾ šām hannaḥălāyim* ("wherever the *two* torrents/streams go"—a dual form, *naḥălāyim*). The LXX translates with the singular *ho potamos* ("the river"), and many modern translations render it as an intensive or collective singular ("wherever the river goes"). **(e) Interpretive Hypothesis:** Whether the MT dual represents an intensive/broad double-stream, a grammatical anomaly, or a textual resonance with a bifurcated flow (as later made explicit in Zech 14:8, flowing both east and west), it stands as an unexpected morphological seam within a chapter that otherwise uses the singular *hannaḥal* (vv. 5, 6, 7, 12).
- **The Unhealed Salt Marshes (47:11):** Despite the sweeping promise of healing in vv. 8–9, v. 11 inserts a striking exception: "its swamps and its marshes (*biṣṣōṯāw ûḡᵉḇāʾāw*) will not be healed; they are given over to salt (*lᵉmelaḥ nittānû*)." **(e) Interpretive Hypothesis:** Unlike apocalyptic totalism that eradicates all salt or barrenness (cf. Rev 22:3), Ezekiel preserves salt production—an economic necessity in the Levant and a liturgical requirement in Ezekiel's own altar law (Ezek 43:24; cf. Lev 2:13).

## 3. Unexpected Lexical and Grammatical Connections

### 3.1 *Mᵉp̄akkîm* (מְפַכִּים, "Trickling") and *Paḵ* (פַּךְ, "Anointing Flask")
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (b) Linguistic Relationship:** In 47:2, the initial outflow south of the east gate is described with the rare Piel participle *mᵉp̄akkîm* ("trickling, oozing, issuing drop by drop" from *p-k-h*)—a **hapax legomenon** in the Hebrew Bible. Its closest nominal cognate in Biblical Hebrew is *paḵ* ("flask, cruse, vial"), which appears exclusively in the context of royal anointing oil: Samuel's flask anointing Saul (1 Sam 10:1) and Elisha's prophet's flask anointing Jehu (2 Kgs 9:1, 3). **(e) Interpretive Hypothesis:** While one should not over-press etymological identity into direct theological allegory, the lexical resonance links the humble initial trickle from the temple wall to the modest vessel of prophetic/royal consecration, underscoring the radical disproportion between the temple's silent drop and the uncrossable flood 4,000 cubits downstream.

### 3.2 *Mê ʾĀp̄sāyim* (מֵי אָפְסָיִם, "Ankle-Deep Waters")
**(b) Linguistic Relationship:** In 47:3, the first 1,000-cubit measurement reaches the *ʾāp̄sāyim* ("ankles"), a dual form of *ʾep̄es*. Everywhere else in Biblical Hebrew, *ʾep̄es* carries the abstract meaning "end, extremity, cessation, nothingness" (e.g., *ʾap̄sê-ʾāreṣ*, "ends of the earth," Ps 2:8; Isa 45:22; or *ʾep̄es* "there is none," Isa 45:6). Only here (reflecting a sense attested in later Aramaic/Mishnaic usage) does the dual denote the anatomical "extremities" of the legs (ankles).

### 3.3 *Mê Śāḥû* (מֵי שָׂחוּ, "Waters for Swimming") and Moab's Humiliation (Isa 25:11)
**(b) Linguistic Relationship / (c) Structural Parallel:** In 47:5, the fourth measurement yields *mê śāḥû* ("waters of swimming"), a nominal *qāṭûl*-type hapax from the root *ś-ḥ-h* ("to swim"). Outside Ezek 47:5 and Ps 6:7 (where the psalmist "floods/swims" his bed with tears, Hiphil), the root *ś-ḥ-h* appears in **Isaiah 25:11**: YHWH brings down Moab's pride "as a swimmer spreads his hands to swim" (*kaʾăšer yᵉp̄ārēś haśśōḥeh liśḥôṯ*). Geographically, Moab sits immediately across the Dead Sea—the exact terminus of Ezekiel's river. Whereas Isa 25:11 uses swimming imagery ironically for Moab sinking in a pit of dung/waters of judgment, Ezek 47:5 uses *śāḥû* for the overwhelming abundance of sanctuary grace reaching the border of Moab.

### 3.4 *Rāp̄āʾ* ("Heal"), *Šāraṣ* ("Swarm"), *Nep̄eš Ḥayyāh* ("Living Creature"), and Genesis 1
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (b) Linguistic Relationship:** Ezekiel 47:8–10 deploys a dense cluster of verbs and nouns unusual in hydrological descriptions:
- **Healing of Waters (*rāp̄āʾ*, vv. 8, 9, 11):** Applied not to human bodies, as is typical, but to *waters* (*wᵉnirpᵉʾû hammāyim*). The exact precedent is **2 Kings 2:19–22**, where Elisha heals Jericho's bad water near the lower Jordan/Dead Sea basin using salt in a new bowl: *"I have healed these waters"* (*rippîṯî lammayim hāʾēlleh*, 2 Kgs 2:21). Paradoxically, Elisha uses *salt* to heal freshwater, whereas Ezekiel's temple water heals *salt* water—while leaving designated salt marshes intact (v. 11).
- **Day 5 New Creation Echo (47:9–10 vs. Gen 1:20–21):** Ezek 47:9–10 combines *kol-nep̄eš ḥayyāh ʾăšer-yišrōṣ* ("every living creature that swarms"), *bammayim*, and *lᵉmînāh* ("according to their kinds," v. 10). In **Genesis 1:20–21**, God commands: *"Let the waters swarm with swarms of living creatures"* (*yišrᵉṣû hammayim šereṣ nep̄eš ḥayyāh*), creating sea monsters and every living thing *lᵉmînēhem* ("according to their kinds"). The healing of the Dead Sea is thus lexically framed as a **re-enactment of the fifth day of creation** in a basin associated with Genesis's judgment narrative (Sodom/Salt Sea, Gen 14:3; 19:24–28).

### 3.5 *Tᵉrûp̄āh* ("Healing"), *Yᵉḇakkēr* ("Bear Monthly Firstfruits"), and Psalm 1:3 / Jeremiah 17:8
**(b) Linguistic Relationship / (c) Structural Parallel:**
- **Psalm 1:3 & Jeremiah 17:8:** Ezek 47:12 states of the riverbank trees: *"and its leaf will not wither"* (*wᵉlōʾ-yibbôl ʿālēhû*). Psalm 1:3 uses the identical clause in reverse word order for the righteous person planted by streams of water (*palḡê māyim*): *wᵉʿālēhû lōʾ-yibbôl* (cf. Jer 17:8, where the tree by water retains green leaves and never ceases bearing fruit). Ezekiel transplants the wisdom metaphor of the righteous tree into the architectural reality of the eschatological sanctuary.
- ***B-k-r* (*yᵉḇakkēr*, "bear new fruit," 47:12):** The Piel verb *yᵉḇakkēr* derives from the root of *bᵉḵôr* ("firstborn") and *bikkûrîm* ("firstfruits"). Bearing *lᵉḥŏdāšāyw yᵉḇakkēr* means each month produces a fresh *firstfruits* harvest—collapsing the annual agrarian-cultic calendar into an unbroken monthly cycle of *bikkûrîm*.
- ***Tᵉrûp̄āh* (תְּרוּפָה, 47:12):** Another hapax legomenon, rendered "healing" (LXX *hygieia*; Rev 22:2 *therapeia*), traditionally linked to *r-p-ʾ* ("heal," matching vv. 8–9, 11) or occasionally to *r-p-h* ("soothe/refresh"). The leaves function as a botanical extension of the river's *rāp̄āʾ* power.

## 4. Architectural and Cultic Connections

### 4.1 The "Right Shoulder" (*Keṯep̄ Hayyᵉmānîṯ*) and Solomon's Bronze Sea
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (c) Structural Parallel:** In Ezek 47:1, the river issues *mittaḥat keṯep̄ habbayit hayyᵉmānîṯ... minneḡeḇ lammizbēaḥ* ("from beneath the right/south shoulder of the house... south of the altar"). Because ancient Israelite orientation faced east (*qādîm*), "right" (*yāmîn*) equals south (*neḡeḇ*).

Unexpectedly, **1 Kings 7:39** (paralleled in **2 Chronicles 4:10**) uses virtually the same rare anatomical-architectural coordinate to locate Solomon's molten bronze Sea (*hayyām*):
> *...mikkāṯēp̄ habbayit hayyᵉmānîṯ qēḏmāh mûl neḡeḇ* ("on the right/south shoulder of the house, eastward, toward the south").

In Ezekiel's painstakingly measured temple (Ezek 40–46), **there is no bronze Sea** and no mention of the ten wheeled lavers of Solomon's court. **(e) Interpretive Hypothesis:** In Solomon's temple, the cosmic waters were symbolically contained in a static cast-bronze basin at the southeast shoulder near the altar. In Ezekiel's temple, that static cosmic reservoir is absent because the sanctuary itself becomes an uncontained, living spring bursting out from the exact same southeast *keṯep̄* coordinate past the altar.

### 4.2 The Temple-City Without a Natural River (Ps 46:4; Isa 8:6; 33:20–21)
**(c) Structural Parallel / (d) Physical Context:** Historical Jerusalem possessed no river—only the intermittent karstic Gihon spring in the Kidron Valley, supplemented by cisterns and aqueducts. Yet **Psalm 46:5 (ET 46:4)** declares: *"There is a river (*nāhār*) whose streams (*pᵉlāḡāyw*) make glad the city of God, the holy habitation of the Most High."* **Isaiah 8:6** contrasts Judah's rejection of *"the waters of Shiloah that flow gently"* with the overwhelming Euphrates flood of Assyria, while **Isaiah 33:20–21** promises that YHWH himself will be for Zion *"a place of broad rivers and streams (*nᵉhārîm yᵉʾōrîm raḥăḇê yāḏāyim*), where no galley with oars can go."* Ezekiel 47 materializes this paradoxical temple-hydrology tradition into measured spatial narrative.

### 4.3 New Testament Temple-Body Appropriation (John 7:37–39; 19:34)
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (e) Interpretive Hypothesis:** Within Second Temple Sukkot liturgy, water drawn from the Pool of Siloam was poured at the altar while Ezekiel 47 and Zechariah 14 were associated with eschatological rain and temple waters. Against this backdrop, **John 7:37–38** presents Jesus at the Feast of Tabernacles declaring that *"rivers of living water (*potamoi hydatos zōntos*) will flow from within him/his belly (*ek tēs koilias autou*)"*—a composite echo of temple-river texts (Ezek 47; Zech 14:8; Isa 44:3; Prov 18:4) applied to Christ's body as the true sanctuary (cf. John 2:19–21). Even more striking structurally is **John 19:34**: after Jesus's death, a soldier pierces his **side** (*pleura*), and blood and water flow out. Just as Ezekiel 47:1 specifies water issuing from beneath the *side/shoulder* (*keṯep̄*) of the temple *south of the altar* (the place of blood sacrifice), John pairs sacrificial blood and living water issuing from the side of the crucified temple-body.

## 5. Geographical and Topographical Connections

### 5.1 *Haggᵉlîlāh Haqqadmônāh*, the Arabah, and the Kidron Drainage
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (d) Physical Geography:** Ezekiel 47:8 traces three geographical zones:
1. ***Haggᵉlîlāh haqqadmônāh* ("the eastern region/circuit"):** *Gᵉlîlāh* here does not refer to northern Galilee, but to its root meaning "circuit/district" (cf. Josh 13:2; Joel 4:4 [ET 3:4]), specifically the eastern hill-country slope and Jordan circuit east of Jerusalem.
2. ***Hāʿărāḇāh* ("the Arabah"):** The deep Jordan Rift Valley descending to the Dead Sea (cf. Deut 1:1; 2 Sam 2:29).
3. ***Hayyāmmāh* ("into the Sea"):** Unambiguously the Dead Sea / Salt Sea (*yam hammelaḥ*, Gen 14:3).

An unexpected topographical irony underlies this route. Physically, any watercourse flowing straight east from the Temple Mount descends into the **Wadi Kidron** (*naḥal qiḏrôn*) and eastward through the Judean Wilderness toward the northwest Dead Sea. Throughout the Deuteronomistic and prophetic corpus, the Kidron is a dry ravine associated with **grief** (2 Sam 15:23), **corpse-impurity** (Jer 31:40), and the **dumping/burning of idolatrous ashes** from temple purges (2 Kgs 23:4, 6, 12; 2 Chr 29:16; 30:14). Although Ezek 47 does not name the Kidron, its eastward path turns Jerusalem's historical ash-dump gully into an uncrossable river of Edenic regeneration.

### 5.2 En-Gedi to En-Eglaim and the Reversal of Sodom
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (c) Structural Parallel:** Ezekiel 47:10 anchors the new fishery between **En-Gedi** (*ʿên geḏî*, "Spring of the Kid") on the western shore of the Dead Sea—a freshwater oasis famed for David's cave refuge (1 Sam 24:1–2), vineyards (Song 1:14), and Moabite/Ammonite battle mobilization (2 Chr 20:2)—and **En-Eglaim** (*ʿên ʿeḡlayim*, "Spring of Two Calves"), a place-name occurring **nowhere else in Scripture**. While En-Eglaim's precise identification remains debated (proposals include Ain Feshkha at the northwestern shore or a site near the Jordan delta/Moabite border), the pair establishes an extensive shoreline zone where nets are spread as at the Mediterranean ("the Great Sea," *hayyām haggāḏôl*).

Topographically, this reverses the Genesis arc of the Jordan *kikkār*:
- **Genesis 13:10:** Before destruction, the Jordan plain was *"well-watered everywhere... like the garden of YHWH."*
- **Genesis 14:3; 19:24–28; Deut 29:22 (ET 23); Zeph 2:9:** Judgment turns the basin into the Salt Sea, brimstone, and salt wasteland.
- **Ezekiel 47:8–12:** Temple waters restore the Sodom/Salt Sea basin back to garden-of-YHWH fertility—while retaining only controlled salt marshes (47:11) rather than total wasteland.

## 6. Physical, Hydrological, Mathematical, and Ecological Analogies

*(Note: All physical considerations in this section are offered as real-world analogies and spatial checks, not as naturalistic explanations of a prophetic vision.)*

### 6.1 Non-Conservative Downstream Escalation (4 × 1,000 Cubits)
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (d) Physical Analogy:** Ezekiel 47:3–5 records four sequential measurements of 1,000 cubits each (*ʾelep̄ bāʾammāh*). Taking a standard cubit (~0.45 m) to a royal cubit (~0.525 m, cf. Ezek 40:5), each leg spans ~450–525 m, totaling **~1.8–2.1 km** from the outer east gate. Over that short reach, depth increases monotonically without any mention of tributaries:
1. **1,000 cubits:** ankle-deep (*mê ʾāp̄sāyim*)
2. **2,000 cubits:** knee-deep (*mê birkāyim*)
3. **3,000 cubits:** waist-deep (*mê moṯnāyim*)
4. **4,000 cubits:** uncrossable swimming *naḥal* (*mê śāḥû*).

In open-channel hydraulics, volumetric discharge ($Q = A \cdot v$, cross-sectional area $\times$ velocity) is conserved unless water enters along the reach via tributaries, precipitation runoff, or distributed groundwater upwelling (baseflow). Conversely, channel depth can increase without extra discharge only if channel width narrows dramatically or slope flattens (causing backwater ponding and slower velocity). Here, however, the river becomes both *deep* and a *rushing, uncrossable torrent* (*naḥal*) over less than two miles, with no tributaries named. Physically, this behaves like continuous **artesian/karstic discharge or supernatural mass-addition**—fitting the limestone-dolomite karst geology of the Jerusalem hills only as a visual motif, while vastly exceeding natural Judean spring yields.

### 6.2 Relief Gradient vs. Floodplain Ecology
**(d) Physical Analogy:** The actual elevation drop between the Temple Mount (~740 m above sea level) and the Dead Sea (~400+ m below sea level) exceeds 1,100 m over roughly 25–30 km of east-southeast terrain—an average slope near 4%. A perennial river descending that escarpment would be a high-energy, steep-gradient canyon stream (consistent with an impassable *naḥal*), whereas the lush orchard banks (v. 7, 12) and stagnant salt-evaporating marshes (v. 11) presuppose the sudden transition to the flat Arabah/Dead Sea floor at the base of the fault scarp.

### 6.3 Dead Sea Limnology and Evaporitic Salt Marshes (47:11)
**(d) Physical Analogy:** The Dead Sea's hypersaline brine (~30–34% dissolved salts, dominated by magnesium, calcium, sodium, and chloride ions) prevents fish survival. Yet in natural limnology, where freshwater springs (such as En-Gedi or Ain Feshkha) meet the Dead Sea shore, localized brackish ecosystems support specialized killifish (*Aphanius*) and reeds, while isolated shallow lagoons cut off from main current circulation undergo intense solar evaporation in the rift heat, precipitating thick halite crusts (**sabkhas / salt pans**). Strikingly, Ezek 47:11 (*biṣṣōṯāw ûḡᵉḇāʾāw*, "its swamps and marshes... given over to salt") mirrors the exact physical behavior of peripheral stagnant basins in an arid rift valley: main-channel flow flushes and freshens the sea, whereas off-channel marshes remain evaporitic salt traps.

## 7. Apocalyptic and Canonical Trajectory

### 7.1 From Eden (Gen 2:10–14; Ezek 28:13–14) to the Temple Mountain
**(c) Structural Parallel / (e) Interpretive Hypothesis:** In Genesis 2:10, a single river (*nāhār*) waters Eden and divides outward into four headwaters (*rāʾšîm*) nourishing the earth. In Ezek 28:13–14, Eden is explicitly situated on *"the holy mountain of God."* Ezekiel 40:2 places the visionary temple on *"a very high mountain."* Thus Ezek 47 functions as **re-opened Eden**: the sanctuary mountain restores the outward-flowing life-stream forfeited in Genesis 3.

### 7.2 Joel 3:18 (Heb 4:18) and Zechariah 14:8 — Divergent Hydrology
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (c) Structural Parallel:** Two post-exilic/prophetic texts adapt the temple-river motif with unexpected geographical shifts:
- **Joel 4:18 (ET 3:18):** *"A fountain shall come forth from the house of YHWH and water the Wadi of Shittim (*naḥal haššiṭṭîm*)."* Shittim (Num 25:1; Josh 2:1; 3:1) lies on the Moabite plain east of the Jordan—the scene of Israel's Baal-peor apostasy and its final staging ground before crossing the Jordan. Joel's river crosses/reaches *trans-Jordan* Moabite territory, healing a site of covenantal failure.
- **Zechariah 14:8:** *"Living waters (*mayim ḥayyîm*) shall flow out from Jerusalem, half of them to the eastern sea (*hayyām haqqadmônî*) and half of them to the western sea (*hayyām hāʾaḥărôn*); it shall continue in summer as in winter."* Unlike Ezek 47's strictly eastward flow (apart from the ambiguous dual *naḥălāyim* in 47:9), Zechariah explicitizes a **bi-directional watershed**, sending living waters both to the Dead Sea and the Mediterranean, and explicitly cancels the seasonal dry-wadi cycle (*baccayiṣ ûḇāḥōrep̄*, "in summer and in winter").

### 7.3 Daniel 7:10 — The Contrasting Apocalyptic River of Fire
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (c) Structural Parallel:** While prophetic temple texts feature a river of *water* issuing from the sanctuary, **Daniel 7:10** depicts a **river of fire** (*nᵉhar dî-nûr*) streaming forth from before the throne of the Ancient of Days for cosmic judgment. Both traditions share the motif of an unstoppable river emanating from the divine presence—one judicial and consuming (Dan 7:10), the other restorative and life-giving (Ezek 47:1–12)—a polarity that Revelation later separates into the river of life from the throne (Rev 22:1) and the lake of fire (Rev 20:10, 14–15).

### 7.4 Revelation 22:1–5 — Architectural Culmination and Universalization
**(a) Attested Textual Evidence / (c) Structural Parallel / (e) Interpretive Hypothesis:** Revelation 22:1–5 reworks Ezekiel 47:1–12 (primarily via the LXX) with four decisive apocalyptic transformations:

| Feature | Ezekiel 47:1–12 | Revelation 22:1–5 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Source of River** | Beneath the **temple threshold** (47:1), south of the altar | Directly from **the throne of God and of the Lamb** (Rev 22:1; no temple in the city, 21:22) |
| **Course** | Eastward out of the sanctuary down to the Arabah/Dead Sea | Down the middle of the **city street** (*plateia*) |
| **Riparian Flora** | *"Very many trees"* (*ʿēṣ raḇ mᵉʾōḏ*) on both banks (*mizzeh ûmizzeh*, 47:7, 12) | Collective/singular **tree of life** (*xylon zōēs*) on both sides (*enteuthen kai ekeithen*, 22:2), fusing Ezek 47 with Gen 2:9 / 3:22 |
| **Scope of Healing** | Dead Sea waters healed; leaves for healing (47:8–12), **salt marshes excepted** (47:11) | Leaves *"for the healing of the nations"* (*eis therapeian tōn ethnōn*, 22:2); **no longer any accursed thing** (22:3; cf. Zech 14:11) |

# System Z

# The Sanctuary River of Ezekiel 47:1–12: Textual, Architectural, Lexical, Geographical, Physical, and Apocalyptic Connections Across Scripture

## Executive Summary

Ezekiel 47:1–12 stands at the intersection of Israel’s temple architecture, creation theology, desert hydrology, mineral-salt covenant practice, and apocalyptic restoration. While commonly read alongside Genesis 2:9–10, Joel 3:18, Zechariah 14:8, and Revelation 22:1–2, close original-language, Septuagintal (LXX), and structural analysis reveals several less obvious but tightly attested connections:

1. **Architectural Substitution for Solomon’s Molten Sea:** Ezekiel 47:1 shares a unique tri-lemma spatial formula—`כָּתֵף` (*katheph*, H3802, "shoulder/side") + `יְמָנִי` (*yemani*, H3233, "right/south") + `נֶגֶב` (*negev*, H5045, "south")—exclusively with 2 Chronicles 4:10 (and closely with 1 Kings 7:39, where `יְמָנִית` is tagged under H3225), which locates Solomon’s bronze **Molten Sea** at the southeast flank of the temple. Ezekiel’s visionary temple omits the bronze Sea altogether; in its exact spatial slot, living water issues directly from beneath the sanctuary threshold and altar.
2. **Threshold Theology of Departure and Return (`מִפְתָּן`, H4670):** Of the eight occurrences of `מִפְתָּן` (*miftan*, "threshold") in the Hebrew Bible, five appear in Ezekiel (9:3; 10:4; 10:18; 46:2; 47:1). The very threshold over which YHWH’s glory hovered when departing eastward (Ezek 9–11) becomes, after the glory’s eastern return (Ezek 43:1–5), the subterranean fountainhead of the eastward river (47:1).
3. **High-Density Hapax and Septuagintal Shifts:** Ezekiel 47:1–12 concentrates four biblical *hapax legomena* (`פָּכָה` H6379, `קַדְמוֹן` H6930, `עֵין עֶגְלַיִם` H5882, `תְּרוּפָה` H8644) and exhibits striking LXX divergences—most notably `ὕδωρ ἀφέσεως` ("water of release/remission") for `מֵי אָפְסָיִם` ("waters of ankles," 47:3), `τὴν Γαλειλαίαν` ("Galilee") for `הַגְּלִילָה` ("eastern circuit," 47:8), `ὑγιάζω`/`ὑγίεια` ("make wholesome/health") for `רָפָא`/`תְּרוּפָה` (47:8, 9, 11, 12), and `ἀνάβασις` ("shooting-up/ascent") for `עָלֵהוּ` ("its leaf," 47:12), which Revelation 22:2 corrects back to `φύλλα` ("leaves") while shifting `ὑγίεια` to `θεραπεία`.
4. **Ecological and Covenantal Salt Paradox:** By leaving the marshes (`בִּצֹּאתָיו וּגְבָאָיו`, H1207, H1360) unhealed and "given to salt" (`לְמֶלַח נִתָּנוּ`, 47:11), the vision preserves mineral salt alongside healed freshwater fisheries—reversing both Elisha’s salt-healing of Jericho’s spring (2 Kgs 2:19–22, linked with Ezek 43:24 as the only two verses where `מֶלַח` is the direct object of `שָׁלַךְ`, "throw") and the judgment language of Tyre (`מִשְׁטַח חֲרָמִים`, Ezek 26:5, 14 ↔ 47:10).
5. **Macro-Structural Hinge (`נַחַל` ↔ `נָחַל`):** Ezekiel 47 pivots from the river (`נַחַל`, H5158; dual `נַחֲלַיִם` in 47:9) in vv. 1–12 to the tribal land inheritance (`נָחַל` H5157 / `נַחֲלָה` H5159) in vv. 13–23 via root paronomasia (`נ-ח-ל`), extending the healed East Sea boundary to resident aliens (47:22–23) in a manner echoed by Revelation 22:2’s "healing of the nations."

## Methodological Distinction of Evidence Registers

To maintain strict epistemic clarity, the connections documented below are categorized across five registers:
- **Attested textual evidence:** Exact Masoretic Hebrew lemmas, vocalized forms, morphology, and Septuagint (Rahlfs) / New Testament Greek readings.
- **Linguistic and grammatical relationships:** Documented root families, voice inversions, rare multi-term intersections, and LXX translation equivalences or lexical shifts.
- **Structural and rhetorical parallels:** Intratextual reversals, chapter-hinge paronomasia, and shared scene sequences across books.
- **Physical and geographical analogies:** Judean Rift topography, hydrological gradients, hypersaline brine dynamics, and mineral salt extraction.
- **Interpretive hypotheses:** Theological or typological syntheses inferred from the above evidence, explicitly marked as interpretive.

## 1. Architectural Connections: Temple Orientation, the Threshold, and the Missing Molten Sea

### 1.1 Replacement of Solomon’s Bronze Sea (`כָּתֵף` + `יְמָנִי` + `נֶגֶב`)
**Attested textual & linguistic evidence:**  
In Ezekiel 47:1, the waters descend from beneath the right/south flank of the temple, south of the altar:  
> `וְהַמַּיִם יֹרְדִים מִתַּחַת מִכֶּתֶף הַבַּיִת הַיְמָנִית מִנֶּגֶב לַמִּזְבֵּחַ`  
> ("and the waters were going down from underneath, from the right side [`כָּתֵף` H3802 + `יְמָנִי` H3233] of the house, on the south [`נֶגֶב` H5045] of the altar.")

An exact intersection search across the Hebrew Bible for the three lemmas `כָּתֵף` (H3802, "shoulder/side"), `יְמָנִי` (H3233, "right/south"), and `נֶגֶב` (H5045, "south") returns **only two verses in Scripture**:
- **Ezekiel 47:1**
- **2 Chronicles 4:10**: `וְאֶת־הַיָּם נָתַן מִכֶּתֶף הַיְמָנִית קֵדְמָה מִמּוּל נֶגְבָּה` ("And he set the sea on the right side eastward, toward the south.")

Its synoptic parallel in **1 Kings 7:39** uses the identical phrase `מִכֶּתֶף הַבַּיִת הַיְמָנִית קֵדְמָה מִמּוּל נֶגֶב` (differing only in the lexical tagging of `הַיְמָנִית` under H3225 `יָמִין`), while **1 Kings 6:8** uses `אֶל־כֶּתֶף הַבַּיִת הַיְמָנִית` (H6607 + H3802 + H1004 + H3233) for the entrance to the middle side-chamber and its winding stairs. Moreover, **Ezekiel 47:6, 7, 12** shares with **1 Kings 7:23–26** the noun `שָׂפָה` (H8193, "lip/brim/bank"), denoting both the molded rim of Solomon’s bronze Sea and the fertile banks of Ezekiel’s river.

**Structural parallel & interpretive hypothesis:**  
In Solomon’s First Temple, the bronze Molten Sea (`הַיָּם הַמּוּצָק`, 10 cubits wide, 2,000 baths capacity, resting on twelve oxen; 1 Kgs 7:23–26; 2 Chr 4:2–5) stood in the southeast quadrant of the inner court (`מִכֶּתֶף הַבַּיִת הַיְמָנִית קֵדְמָה מִמּוּל נֶגֶב`). Strikingly, Ezekiel’s temple blueprint (Ezek 40–46) contains **no Molten Sea and no wheeled lavers**. Instead, at the *exact architectural coordinates* once occupied by Solomon’s static, cast-bronze reservoir, a living, uncontained river emerges directly from beneath the sanctuary and flows east-southeast to heal a real sea (the Dead Sea). On an interpretive level, the static liturgical "sea" of bronze gives way to a dynamic river that transforms the "Salt Sea" itself.

### 1.2 The Threshold (`מִפְתָּן`, H4670) and the East-Gate Detour
**Attested textual evidence:**  
The Hebrew noun `מִפְתָּן` (*miftan*, "threshold," H4670) occurs in only **8 verses** in the entire Hebrew Bible:
- **1 Samuel 5:4–5** (2×): Dagon’s severed head and hands lie on the threshold (`הַמִּפְתָּן`) of his temple before the Ark of YHWH.
- **Zephaniah 1:9** (1×): Judgment on those who leap over the threshold (`הַמִּפְתָּן`).
- **Ezekiel** (5×):
  1. **Ezekiel 9:3**: The glory of the God of Israel ascends from the cherub **to the threshold (`מִפְתַּן הַבָּיִת`)**.
  2. **Ezekiel 10:4**: The glory of YHWH mounts up over **the threshold (`מִפְתַּן הַבָּיִת`)**, filling the house with cloud.
  3. **Ezekiel 10:18**: The glory of YHWH **goes out from over the threshold (`מֵעַל מִפְתַּן הַבָּיִת`)** and departs by the east gate (10:19; 11:23).
  4. **Ezekiel 46:2**: The prince worships **at the threshold of the [inner east] gate (`מִפְתַּן הַשַּׁעַר`)**.
  5. **Ezekiel 47:1**: Waters flow out **from under the threshold of the house (`מִתַּחַת מִפְתַּן הַבַּיִת`)** eastward.

**Structural parallel:**  
In **Ezekiel 43:1–5**, YHWH’s glory returns from the east (`מִדֶּרֶךְ הַקָּדִים`) with a voice "like the sound of many waters" (`כְּקוֹל מַיִם רַבִּים`, 43:2) and fills the house. Because YHWH entered through the outer East Gate, that gate is permanently shut to human traffic (`סָגוּר יִהְיֶה`, Ezek 44:1–2; cf. 46:1). Consequently, when the river flows eastward past the shut East Gate in **Ezekiel 47:2**, the guide cannot lead Ezekiel straight out the east gate; he must bring him out **northward** (`דֶּרֶךְ שַׁעַר צָפוֹנָה`) and circle around the exterior wall to the outside of the outer East Gate, where the water is seen trickling out from the south/right flank (`מִן־הַכָּתֵף הַיְמָנִית`). Furthermore, inside the sanctuary walls from which the river originates, alternating **cherubim and palm trees** (`כְּרוּבִים וְתִמֹרִים`, Ezek 41:18–20) are carved from floor to lintel—architecturally anticipating the Edenic shoreline trees outside (47:7, 12).

### 2.2 Voice Inversions, Syntactic Talion, and the Dual `נַחֲלַיִם`
**Linguistic & grammatical evidence:**
- **Active-to-Passive Voice Inversion ("Syntactic Talion"):**
  - In **47:5**, `עָבַר` (H5674, "pass through") brackets the verse in active and passive stems: `אֲשֶׁר לֹא־אוּכַל לַעֲבֹר` (Qal active infinitive: "which I could not cross") → `נַחַל אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יֵעָבֵר` (Niphal passive imperfect: "a river that cannot be crossed"). The subjective inability of the prophet becomes an objective property of the river.
  - In **47:8**, `יָצָא` (H3318, "go out") shifts from Qal active participle `יוֹצְאִים` ("going out" from the sanctuary) to Hophal passive participle `הַמּוּצָאִים` ("the [waters] brought out" / "made to issue" into the sea).
- **The Dual `נַחֲלַיִם` in 47:9:**  
  Throughout 47:5, 6, 7, 9b, and 12, the Hebrew uses the singular `נַחַל` (*nachal*, H5158). In the first clause of **47:9**, however, the MT preserves the **dual** `נַחֲלַיִם` (*nachalayim*, "two rivers/streams": `אֶל כָּל־אֲשֶׁר יָבוֹא שָׁם נַחֲלַיִם יִחְיֶה`), followed immediately by singular `הַנָּחַל` at the end of the same verse. The LXX levels this to singular `ὁ ποταμός`. While some grammatically treat `נַחֲלַיִם` as an intensive plural/dual ("double-stream" or "merged channels"), it structurally prefigures **Zechariah 14:8**, where the Jerusalem fountain literally divides into two streams (eastern and western), and mirrors the dual-sided symmetry (`מִזֶּה וּמִזֶּה`, 47:7, 12) of the riverbanks.

### 2.3 Significant Septuagint (LXX) Translation Shifts
Comparing MT Ezekiel 47:1–12 with Rahlfs’ LXX reveals systematic theological, hydraulic, and geographical re-readings:

1. **47:1 — Sanctuary Threshold Architecture:**  
   MT `פֶּתַח` ("entrance," H6607) becomes `πρόθυρα` ("vestibule/porch"), while `מִפְתַּן` ("threshold," H4670) becomes `τοῦ αἰθρίου` ("of the open court/atrium," matching LXX Ezek 9:3 and 10:4, whereas Ezek 46:2 and 1 Sam 5:4 render `מִפְתָּן` as `πρόθυρον`). Further, `כָּתֵף` ("shoulder," H3802) shifts from its usual LXX rendering `ὦμος` (shoulder) to the architectural-topographical term `κλίτος` ("slope/flank").
2. **47:2 — From Trickle to Downpour (Plus Textual Minus):**  
   MT’s *hapax* `מְפַכִּים` ("trickling," H6379) is rendered `κατεφέρετο` (`καταφέρω`, "was carried/poured down"—used in LXX Isa 17:13; 28:2; Mic 1:4 of rushing torrents). The LXX also expands with `περιήγαγέν με` ("led me around") and `τῆς αὐλῆς` ("of the court") while lacking MT’s `ח֔וּץ דֶּ֖רֶךְ`.
3. **47:3 — `מֵי אָפְסָיִם` → `ὕδωρ ἀφέσεως` ("Water of Release/Remission"):**  
   Rather than translating `אָפְסָיִם` (H0657) anatomically ("ankles"), the LXX renders `ὕδωρ ἀφέσεως` (`ἄφεσις`, "release, discharge, sluice," and in NT/LXX "remission/forgiveness"). Whether driven by phonetic association (*ʾephes* ↔ *aphesis*) or hydraulic interpretation ("water of the sluice/outflow"), the shift introduces an unmistakable soteriological resonance into the Greek text.
4. **47:4–5 — Measurement and Surge:**  
   In 47:4, MT `בִּרְכָּיִם` ("knees," H1290) shifts upward to `ἕως τῶν μηρῶν` ("up to the thighs"). In 47:5, MT `גָאוּ הַמַּיִם מֵי שָׂחוּ` ("the waters had risen, waters for swimming") is condensed to `ἐξύβριζεν` (`ἐξυβρίζω`, "it surged/overflowed violently").
5. **47:8 — Geographical Shift (`Γαλειλαία` and `Ἀραβία`) and `διεκβολή`:**  
   MT `הַגְּלִילָה הַקַּדְמוֹנָה` ("the eastern circuit/district," H1552 + H6930) is rendered **`τὴν Γαλειλαίαν τὴν πρὸς ἀνατολάς`** ("Galilee toward the east"—the same Greek toponym `Γαλιλαία` used for `גְּלִיל` in Isa 8:23 [9:1] and Joel 4:4 [3:4]), while `הָעֲרָבָה` ("the Arabah") becomes `τὴν Ἀραβίαν` ("Arabia"). Meanwhile, `הַיָּמָּה הַמּוּצָאִים` becomes `τὴν θάλασσαν ἐπὶ τὸ ὕδωρ τῆς διεκβολῆς` ("the sea, upon the water of the outlet [`διεκβολή`]"). Remarkably, the rare noun `διεκβολή` also appears in LXX **Zechariah 9:10** translating `אַפְסֵי־[אָרֶץ]`!
6. **47:8, 9, 11, 12 — Medical Shift from `ἰάομαι` to `ὑγιάζω` / `ὑγίεια`:**  
   Across 67 LXX occurrences of `רָפָא` (H7495), the standard Greek equivalent is `ἰάομαι` (46×), including Elisha’s water-healing in 2 Kings 2:21–22. Yet in **Ezekiel 47:8, 9, 11**, the LXX consistently shifts `רָפָא` to **`ὑγιάζω`** ("make wholesome/sound"—otherwise found for `רָפָא` only in Leviticus 13:18, 37 regarding ritual cleansing of skin disease), pairing it in **47:12** with **`εἰς ὑγίειαν`** ("for health/wholesomeness") for the *hapax* `לִתְרוּפָה`.
7. **47:11 — Re-reading the Salt Marshes:**  
   Confronted with the rare `בִּצֹּאתָיו וּגְבָאָיו` ("its swamps and its marshes"), LXX translates `ἐν τῇ διεκβολῇ αὐτοῦ καὶ ἐν τῇ ἐπιστροφῇ αὐτοῦ καὶ ἐν τῇ ὑπεράρσει αὐτοῦ` ("in its outlet and in its turning-back and in its overflowing").
8. **47:12 — `עָלֵהוּ` ("Its Leaf") → `ἀνάβασις` ("Shooting-Up"):**  
   In MT, `וְעָלֵהוּ לִתְרוּפָה` means "and its leaf (`עָלֶה`, H5929) for healing." The LXX read the consonants `עלהו` as derived from the verb `עָלָה` ("to go up/sprout," H5927, matching `יַעֲלֶה` at the start of v. 12) and translated `καὶ ἀνάβασις αὐτῶν εἰς ὑγίειαν` ("and their shooting-up/growth for health"). As shown in Section 5, Revelation 22:2 independently restores the MT sense `τὰ φύλλα` ("the leaves").

## 3. Geographical and Topographical Connections

### 3.1 The Kidron Hydrological Gradient and Jeremiah 31:38–40
**Physical & geographical evidence:**  
Jerusalem’s Temple Mount plateau (~743 m elevation) sits directly west of the Kidron Valley (`נַחַל קִדְרוֹן`, ~640–670 m), which drops steeply southeastward through the Judean Wilderness into the Dead Sea / Arabah Rift (~400+ m below sea level). In the Hebrew Bible, `נַחַל` (H5158) repeatedly collocates with `קִדְרוֹן` (H6939, 9 verses), including **Jeremiah 31:38–40**:
> `וְיָצָא עוֹד קָו הַמִּדָּה נֶגְדּוֹ ... עַד נַחַל קִדְרוֹן עַד פִּנַּת שַׁעַר הַסּוּסִים מִזְרָחָה קֹדֶשׁ לַיהוָה`

Ezekiel 47:1–8 traces this exact physical drainage path: eastward from the temple plateau (`קָדִימָה`), through the eastern district (`הַגְּלִילָה הַקַּדְמוֹנָה`), down into the Arabah rift (`וְיָרְד֖וּ עַל־הָעֲרָבָה`), and into the Salt Sea (`הַיָּמָּה`). Notice also the parallel with **Jeremiah 31:39–40**: just as Jeremiah’s **measuring line** (`קָו הַמִּדָּה`, cf. Ezek 47:3 `קָו` + `מָדַד` + `אַמָּה`, unique in the OT) extends eastward to the Kidron Valley to sanctify a valley of corpses and ashes as "holy to YHWH," Ezekiel’s measured river descends eastward to resurrect a dead sea.

### 3.2 En-gedi (`עֵין גֶּדִי`), Hazazon-tamar, and En-eglaim (`עֵין עֶגְלַיִם`)
**Attested textual & geographical evidence:**  
Ezekiel 47:10 defines the restored Dead Sea fishery: "From En-gedi even to En-eglaim (`מֵעֵין גֶּדִי וְעַד־עֵין עֶגְלַיִם`)."
- **Joshua 15:62** places **En-gedi** directly beside the **City of Salt** (`עִיר־הַמֶּלַח וְעֵין גֶּדִי`) in Judah’s wilderness district along the western Dead Sea shore.
- **2 Chronicles 20:2** explicitly identifies En-gedi as **Hazazon-tamar** (`בְּחַצְצוֹן תָּמָר הִיא עֵין גֶּדִי`, "pruning/division of the palm"), linking it back to **Genesis 14:3, 7**, where the kings gather in the Valley of Siddim—explicitly glossed as the **Salt Sea** (`הוּא יָם הַמֶּלַח`)—and strike Hazazon-tamar.
- **Song of Solomon 1:14** celebrates "the vineyards of En-gedi," attesting that En-gedi was already a famed freshwater oasis along an otherwise lifeless brine shore.
- **En-eglaim** (H5882, "Spring of Two Calves") is a biblical *hapax legomenon*, generally placed near the northern shore/Jordan mouth of the Dead Sea (facing the southern/mid-western shore oasis of En-gedi), thereby enclosing the northern half or full navigable basin of the healed sea.

## 4. Physical, Ecological, and Liturgical Analogies: Salt, Swarms, and Healing

### 4.1 The Salt Paradox: Elisha’s Healing (2 Kgs 2:19–22), Ezekiel’s Altar (43:24), and Preserved Brine Marshes (47:11)
**Attested textual & physical evidence:**  
Salt (`מֶלַח`, H4417; halite mineral) plays a striking dual role across Ezekiel 43–47 and 2 Kings 2:
- **Lexical exclusivity (`שָׁלַךְ` + `מֶלַח`):** Across the entire Hebrew Bible, `מֶלַח` (H4417) appears as the direct object of `שָׁלַךְ` (H7993, "to throw/cast") in **only two verses**:
  1. **2 Kings 2:21**: Just northwest of the Dead Sea at Jericho, Elisha goes to the spring outlet (`מוֹצָא הַמַּיִם`), **throws salt** (`וַיַּשְׁלֶךְ שָׁם מֶלַח`) from a new jar into the bad waters (`הַמַּיִם רָעִים`), and declares: `רִפִּאתִי לַמַּיִם הָאֵלֶּה` ("I have healed [H7495] these waters; there shall no longer be death or barrenness from there").
  2. **Ezekiel 43:24**: At the consecration of Ezekiel’s visionary altar—the very altar south of which the river of 47:1 flows—the priests **throw salt** (`וְהִשְׁלִיכוּ הַכֹּהֲנִים עֲלֵיהֶם מֶלַח`) upon the burnt offerings, fulfilling the Torah mandate of the **"salt of the covenant"** (`מֶלַח בְּרִית אֱלֹהֶיךָ`, Lev 2:13; cf. Num 18:19; 2 Chr 13:5).
- **Physical process & ecological contrast (47:11):** In the Dead Sea basin, hypersaline brine prevents aquatic life (`אֶרֶץ מְלֵחָה`, Jer 17:6; Deut 29:23; Ps 107:34). Yet when Ezekiel’s sanctuary river neutralizes the sea’s salinity so that fish swarm (`וְנִרְפּוּ הַמָּיִם`, 47:8–9), **47:11** deliberately exempts the shallow evaporation pools and marshes: `בִּצֹּאתָיו וּגְבָאָיו וְלֹא יֵרָפְאוּ לְמֶלַח נִתָּנוּ` ("its swamps and its marshes will not be healed; they are given to salt"). Physically, coastal evaporation pans (*biṣṣāh* and *geveʾ*) remain essential for harvesting mineral salt; liturgically, the restored temple’s altar sacrifices (Ezek 43:24; Lev 2:13) still require a perpetual supply of covenant salt!

### 4.2 Reversal of Tyre’s Judgment (`מִשְׁטוֹחַ לַחֲרָמִים`) and the `חֵרֶם` Homonym
**Structural parallel & attested textual evidence:**  
In **Ezekiel 47:10**, the healed Dead Sea shore becomes `מִשְׁטוֹחַ לַחֲרָמִים` ("a spreading-place for nets"; LXX `ψυγμὸς σαγηνῶν`). The noun `מִשְׁטָח` / `מִשְׁטוֹחַ` (H4894) and its LXX equivalent `ψυγμός` appear in **only four verses** in the biblical corpus:
- **Ezekiel 26:5 & 26:14**: Tyre is stripped to a bare rock and made `מִשְׁטַח חֲרָמִים` (`ψυγμὸς σαγηνῶν`)—a scene of utter desolation and ruin.
- **Ezekiel 47:10**: The same rare phrase (`מִשְׁטוֹחַ לַחֲרָמִים` / `ψυγμὸς σαγηνῶν`) recurs in reverse polarity—not a ruined city reduced to fishing rocks, but a dead sea resurrected into an abundant fishery! (The fourth occurrence of `ψυγμός` in LXX Num 11:32 renders the spreading out of quails in the camp.)

Additionally, the Hebrew word `חֵרֶם` (H2764) encompasses two homonymous senses: "dragnet" (Ezek 26:5, 14; 47:10; Hab 1:15–17) and "devoted thing / ban / curse" (LXX `ἀνάθεμα`; Ezek 44:29 `ἀφόρισμα`). While Ezekiel 47:10 celebrates the *fishing nets* (`חֲרָמִים`) of abundance, the later prophetic trajectory in Zechariah 14:11 (`וְחֵרֶם לֹא יִהְיֶה עוֹד`) and Revelation 22:3 (`πᾶν κατάθεμα οὐκ ἔσται ἔτι`) announces the abolition of the *curse* (`חֵרֶם`).

### 4.3 Creation Day 5 and Leviticus 11 Taxonomy
**Attested textual evidence:**  
Ezekiel 47:9–10 systematically echoes **Genesis 1:20–22** and **Leviticus 11:10, 46**:
- **Genesis 1:20–21**: `יִשְׁרְצוּ הַמַּיִם שֶׁרֶץ נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה ... אֲשֶׁר שָׁרְצוּ הַמַּיִם לְמִינֵהֶם` ("let the waters swarm with swarming living creatures ... after their kinds").
- **Leviticus 11:10**: `בַּיַּמִּים וּבַנְּחָלִים מִכֹּל שֶׁרֶץ הַמַּיִם וּמִכֹּל נֶפֶשׁ הַחַיָּה אֲשֶׁר בַּמָּיִם` ("in the seas and in the rivers/wadis [`נְחָלִים`], of all swarming things of the water and every living creature in the waters").
- **Ezekiel 47:9–10**: `כָל־נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה אֲשֶׁר יִשְׁרֹץ אֶל כָּל־אֲשֶׁר יָבוֹא שָׁם נַחֲלַיִם יִחְיֶה ... לְמִינָהּ תִּהְיֶה דְגָתָם כִּדְגַת הַיָּם הַגָּדוֹל` ("every living creature that swarms wherever the two rivers come shall live ... their fish shall be according to its kind, like the fish of the Great Sea").

By importing Day 5 creation language (`נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה`, `שָׁרַץ`, `לְמִינָהּ`) into a sea previously barren of aquatic life, Ezekiel depicts the sanctuary river as an act of **new creation**, matching the biodiversity of the Mediterranean ("the Great Sea," `הַיָּם הַגָּדוֹל`) inside the Dead Sea basin.

## 5. Wisdom, Prophetic, and Apocalyptic Trajectory

### 5.1 The Evergreen Tree Matrix (Gen 2:9–10; Ps 1:3; Jer 17:6–8; Ezek 47:12)
**Attested textual & intersection evidence:**  
An exact lexical intersection of `עֵץ` (H6086, "tree") + `עָלֶה` (H5929, "leaf") + `נָבֵל` (H5034, "wither") occurs in **only two verses in the Hebrew Bible**:
- **Psalms 1:3**: `וְהָיָה כְּעֵץ שָׁתוּל עַל־פַּלְגֵי מָיִם אֲשֶׁר פִּרְיוֹ יִתֵּן בְּעִתּוֹ וְעָלֵהוּ לֹא־יִבּוֹל` ("like a tree planted by streams of water, which yields its fruit in its season, and its leaf does not wither").
- **Ezekiel 47:12**: `כָּל־עֵץ־מַאֲכָל לֹא־יִבּוֹל עָלֵהוּ וְלֹא־יִתֹּם פִּרְיוֹ לָחֳדָשָׁיו יְבַכֵּר` ("every tree for food: its leaf will not wither, nor will its fruit fail; every month it will bear firstfruits").

**Jeremiah 17:6–8** bridges Ezekiel 47:8–12 conceptually by juxtaposing the two landscapes within three verses: the cursed shrub `בָּעֲרָבָה ... אֶרֶץ מְלֵחָה` ("in the Arabah ... a salt land," 17:6) versus the blessed `עֵץ שָׁתוּל עַל־מַיִם ... וְהָיָה עָלֵהוּ רַעֲנָן` ("tree planted by water ... its leaf shall be green," 17:8). Meanwhile, **Exodus 15:27 / Numbers 33:9** (Elim, with **12 springs of water** and **70 palm trees**) provides a wilderness prototype for the twelve-month fruiting oasis at En-gedi/Hazazon-tamar.

### 5.2 Prophetic Bridge Texts: Psalm 46, Isaiah 33 & 41, Joel 3:18, and Zechariah 14:8–11
- **Psalms 46:4–5 & Isaiah 33:20–21:** Jerusalem is already celebrated in Zion hymnody as possessing a hidden river (`נָהָר פְּלָגָיו יְשַׂמְּחוּ עִיר־אֱלֹהִים`, Ps 46:4) and "broad rivers and streams" (`מְקוֹם־נְהָרִים יְאֹרִים רַחֲבֵי יָדָיִם`, Isa 33:21), though Jerusalem lacks any natural navigable river—a theological paradox resolved eschatologically by Ezekiel 47, Joel 3:18, and Zechariah 14:8.
- **Isaiah 41:18 & Psalms 107:34–35:** YHWH reverses `אֶרֶץ פְּרִי לִמְלֵחָה` ("a fruitful land into a salt waste," Ps 107:34) by turning wilderness into `אֲגַם־מַיִם` and `מוֹצָאֵי מָיִם` ("springs of water").
- **Joel 3:18 (MT 4:18):** `וּמַעְיָן מִבֵּית יְהוָה יֵצֵא וְהִשְׁקָה אֶת־נַחַל הַשִּׁטִּים` ("a spring shall go out from the house of YHWH and water the Wadi [`נַחַל` H5158] of Shittim"). Since Shittim lies across the Jordan in the Moabite Arabah opposite Jericho, Joel’s spring crosses the very Jordan-Arabah rift valley targeted by Ezekiel 47:8.
- **Zechariah 14:8–11:** `יֵצְאוּ מַיִם־חַיִּים מִירוּשָׁלִַם חֶצְיָם אֶל־הַיָּם הַקַּדְמוֹנִי וְחֶצְיָם אֶל־הַיָּם הָאַחֲרוֹן` ("living waters shall go out from Jerusalem, half to the eastern sea [Dead Sea] and half to the western sea [Mediterranean]"), flowing perennially in summer and winter, followed by the abolition of `חֵרֶם` (14:11).

### 5.3 New Testament Reception: John 7:37–39, Matthew 13:47–48, and Revelation 22:1–3
1. **John 7:37–39:** Speaking on the great last day of the Feast of Tabernacles (cf. Neh 8:18; 1 Kgs 8:65), Jesus fuses prophetic water-from-the-temple imagery (Ezek 47:1; Zech 14:8; Isa 44:3; 55:1; Gen 26:19) and promises `ποταμοὶ ἐκ τῆς κοιλίας αὐτοῦ ῥεύσουσιν ὕδατος ζῶντος` ("rivers of living water shall flow from within him," interpreted by John 7:39 of the Spirit).
2. **Matthew 13:47–48:** The Parable of the Dragnet (`σαγήνη` thrown into the sea gathering `ἐκ παντὸς γένους`, "of every kind") directly shares its rare dragnet noun `σαγήνη` and taxonomic phrase `ἐκ παντὸς γένους` with LXX **Ezekiel 47:10** (`ψυγμὸς σαγηνῶν` and `לְמִינָהּ`; cf. also John 21:6–11’s abundant catch).
3. **Revelation 22:1–3:** John’s New Jerusalem vision weaves **Ezekiel 47:1, 7, 12**, **Genesis 2:9**, **Psalms 1:3**, and **Zechariah 14:8, 11** into a deliberate culmination:
   - *Source shift:* In Ezekiel 47:1, water flows from beneath the **temple threshold and altar** (`מִתַּחַת מִפְתַּן הַבַּיִת ... לַמִּזְבֵּחַ`); in Revelation 21:22 there is no temple building, so in 22:1 the river proceeds `ἐκπορευόμενον` (matching LXX Ezek 47:1, 12 `ἐκπορεύομαι` = `יָצָא`) directly **from the throne of God and of the Lamb**.
   - *Bank symmetry & monthly fruiting:* `ἐντεῦθεν καὶ ἐκεῖθεν` ("on this side and on that," Rev 22:2) matches LXX Ezek 47:7, 12 (`ἔνθεν καὶ ἔνθεν` = `מִזֶּה וּמִזֶּה`), and `κατὰ μῆνα ἕκαστον ἀποδιδοῦν τὸν καρπὸν αὐτοῦ` echoes `לָחֳדָשָׁיו יְבַכֵּר`.
   - *MT-vs-LXX correction in Rev 22:2:* Whereas LXX Ezek 47:12 translated `וְעָלֵהוּ לִתְרוּפָה` as `καὶ ἀνάβασις αὐτῶν εἰς ὑγίειαν` ("their shooting-up for health"), Revelation 22:2 writes **`καὶ τὰ φύλλα τοῦ ξύλου εἰς θεραπείαν τῶν ἐθνῶν`** ("and the leaves of the tree for the healing of the nations")—restoring the Masoretic sense "leaves" (`φύλλα` = `עָלֶה`, as in Ps 1:3) while adapting `ὑγίεια` to `θεραπεία` and universalizing the healed Israelite inheritance of Ezek 47:13–23 (including the *ger*/alien in 47:22–23) to "the nations."
   - *Abolition of the curse:* `καὶ πᾶν κατάθεμα οὐκ ἔσται ἔτι` (Rev 22:3) echoes Zech 14:11 (`חֵרֶם`), leaving behind even Ezekiel 47:11’s unhealed salt marshes in the final new creation.

## 6. Chapter-Wide Structural Hinge: From River (`נַחַל`) to Inheritance (`נָחַל`)

Finally, Ezekiel 47 is unified internally by a consonantal root hinge (`נ-ח-ל`) rarely noticed when vv. 1–12 and vv. 13–23 are read separately:
- **Ezekiel 47:5–12** repeats **`נַחַל`** (*nachal*, H5158, "river/wadi"; dual `נַחֲלַיִם` in v. 9) six times as the healing water transforms the eastern desert and Salt Sea.
- **Ezekiel 47:13–23** immediately pivots to **`נָחַל`** (*nachal*, H5157, "to inherit/divide for inheritance") and **`נַחֲלָה`** (*nachalah*, H5159, "inheritance") five times—defining the northern, eastern (`עַל הַיָּם הַקַּדְמוֹנִי`, v. 18, the very East Sea just healed in vv. 8–11), southern (including `נַחְלָה` "the Wadi [of Egypt]," v. 19), and western (`הַיָּם הַגָּדוֹל`, v. 20) borders of the twelve tribes, and explicitly granting `נַחֲלָה` to the resident aliens (`הַגֵּרִים`, vv. 22–23).

Thus, the river (`נַחַל`) that brings life out from the sanctuary and makes the Salt Sea like the Great Sea is the narrative and lexical prelude to the land inheritance (`נַחֲלָה`) bounded between those very two seas—culminating in the inclusion of the stranger (`גֵּר`) within Israel’s inheritance and anticipating the healing of the nations in Revelation 22:2.
