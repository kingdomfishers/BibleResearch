# Textual Transmission and Adaptation of Isaiah 28:16, Isaiah 8:14, Psalm 118:22, and Habakkuk 2:4 Across the MT, LXX, and New Testament (No-MCP Baseline Report — B2)

> **Epistemic Status & Verification Notice:** This research report was produced under No-MCP control conditions without access to any live biblical corpus, Masoretic Text (BHS/BHQ), Septuagint (Rahlfs/Göttingen), Greek New Testament (NA28/UBS5), Dead Sea Scrolls transcriptions, or Strong's/lexicon databases. Accordingly, **every verse citation, Hebrew/Greek transcription, morphological claim, and textual-variant claim below is marked `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`** to indicate that it rests on prior scholarly knowledge and requires direct primary-text verification before being treated as verified corpus data. Strong's numbers are intentionally omitted rather than reconstructed from memory.

## 1. Epistemic Framework & Analytical Categories

Throughout this report, findings are explicitly separated into five analytical categories:
- **(a) Putative Textual / Original-Language Evidence `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`:** Recalled Hebrew (MT), Greek (LXX), and Greek (NT) readings, omissions, additions, and manuscript splits.
- **(b) Linguistic / Grammatical Relationships:** Lexical equivalences, translation shifts (e.g., changes in grammatical person, mood, voice, or root identification), and syntactic adjustments.
- **(c) Structural / Rhetorical Parallels:** Splicing/conflation (*haraz* / *testimonia* chaining), catchword linking (*gezerah shawah*), and clause reordering.
- **(d) Physical Analogies:** The underlying concrete imagery (architectural foundation/cornerstone, roadside stumbling stone, bird/animal snare, delayed messenger/retreating traveler) as distinct from its theological application.
- **(e) Interpretive Hypotheses:** Scholarly proposals explaining *why* a given shift occurred (e.g., LXX *Vorlage* differences, homographic/root confusion, Jewish-Christian *testimonia* collections, or deliberate apostolic adaptation).

## 3. Detailed Passage-by-Passage Analysis

### 3.1 Isaiah 28:16

#### (a) Putative Textual / Original-Language Evidence `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **MT (Isa 28:16):** Commonly attested as opening with *lākēn kōh 'āmar 'ădōnāy YHWH hinnənî yissad bəṣiyyôn 'āben 'eben bōḥan pinnat yiqrat mûsād mûssād hamma'ămîn lō' yāḥîš* (note: whether the verb is vocalized as a Qal/Piel perfect *yissad* or participle *yōsēd*—the latter echoed in some ancient versions and Qumran discussions—requires MT/1QIsa^a verification). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **LXX (Isa 28:16):** Commonly attested as *dia touto houtōs legei kyrios [kyrios]: idou egō emballō eis ta themelia Siōn lithon polytelē eklekton akrogōniaion entimon eis ta themelia autēs, kai ho pisteuōn [ep' autō] ou mē kataischynthē*. Note that the presence or absence of *ep' autō* ("in him/it") in pre-Christian LXX manuscripts (versus Hexaplaric/Christianized witnesses) is a known textual-critical question requiring Göttingen LXX apparatus verification. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Romans 9:33:** *Idou tithēmi en Siōn lithon proskommatos kai petran skandalou, kai ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou kataischynthēsetai* (cf. Rom 10:11, which adds *pas*: *pas ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou kataischynthēsetai*). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **1 Peter 2:6:** *Idou tithēmi en Siōn lithon akrogōniaion eklekton entimon, kai ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou mē kataischynthē*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (b) Linguistic & Grammatical Relationships
1. **Verb of placement:** MT's *yissad* / *yōsēd* ("laid / founded") is rendered by LXX *emballō* ("I cast/throw into," paired with *eis ta themelia*, "into the foundations"). Both **Rom 9:33** and **1 Pet 2:6** replace *emballō* with ***tithēmi*** ("I place/set") and omit *eis ta themelia*, leaving *en Siōn* ("in Zion")—structurally closer to MT's *bəṣiyyôn* than to LXX's doubled *eis ta themelia*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
2. **Stone descriptors:** MT's *'eben bōḥan pinnat yiqrat mûsād mûssād* ("stone of testing, precious cornerstone of a founded foundation") becomes in LXX a four-adjective sequence (*polytelē eklekton akrogōniaion entimon*). **1 Pet 2:6** drops *polytelē* and *eis ta themelia autēs* and fronts *akrogōniaion* (*lithon akrogōniaion eklekton entimon*), whereas **Rom 9:33** replaces all the positive descriptors with the negative descriptors of Isa 8:14. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
3. **Final verb shift (MT *yāḥîš* → LXX/NT *kataischyn-*):** MT *lō' yāḥîš* derives from *ḥwš* ("to hasten, hurry, panic/flee in alarm"). LXX translates this as *ou mē kataischynthē* ("will by no means be put to shame/disgraced"). Both **Rom 9:33** (future indicative *ou kataischynthēsetai*) and **1 Pet 2:6** (aorist subjunctive *ou mē kataischynthē*, matching LXX mood) follow the **LXX against the MT**. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
4. **Object of belief (*ep' autō*):** MT has an absolute participle *hamma'ămîn* ("the one trusting/believing") with no prepositional object. Both Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:6 include *ep' autō* ("in/on him"), matching one stream of the LXX tradition (or introducing a personal christological antecedent). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (c) Structural & Rhetorical Parallels
- **Rom 9:33** uses Isa 28:16 as an outer frame (*Idou tithēmi en Siōn... kai ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou kataischynthēsetai*) into which Isa 8:14 is inserted.
- **1 Pet 2:6** keeps Isa 28:16 intact as the first of three distinct *lithos* quotations (2:6 = Isa 28:16; 2:7 = Ps 118:22; 2:8 = Isa 8:14), though already in 2:4 (*eklekton entimon*) the author anticipates Isa 28:16's vocabulary.

#### (d) Physical Analogy
- The physical image in MT Isa 28:16 is architectural stability under siege/storm (cf. Isa 28:15, 17): a heavy, tested (*bōḥan*) corner-anchor (*pinnāh*) deeply bedded (*mûsād mûssād*) so that inhabitants relying on the structure need not flee in panic (*lō' yāḥîš*). LXX shifts the physical/behavioral consequence from "fleeing in haste" to social/covenantal "being put to shame" (*kataischynthē*), generalizing architectural security into vindicated trust.

#### (e) Interpretive Hypotheses
- **Why did LXX render *yāḥîš* as *kataischynthē*?** Two standard hypotheses exist: (1) a translational/semantic extension ("not panic/flee" = "not be disappointed/shamed" by a failing refuge), or (2) graphic/root assimilation to *bwš* ("be ashamed," *yēbôš*).
- **Why do both Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:6 share *tithēmi en Siōn* and *ep' autō* against standard LXX?** Hypotheses include: (1) a shared pre-Pauline/early Christian Greek *testimonia* compilation; (2) 1 Peter's literary dependence on Romans; or (3) a Greek recension of Isaiah no longer preserved independently.

### 3.2 Isaiah 8:14

#### (a) Putative Textual / Original-Language Evidence `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **MT (Isa 8:14):** Commonly attested as *wəhāyāh ləmiqdāš ûlə'eben negep ûləṣûr mikšôl lišnê bāttê yiśrā'ēl ləpaḥ ûləmôqēš ləyôšēb yərûšālāim* ("and he shall become a sanctuary, yet/and a stone of striking and a rock of stumbling to both houses of Israel, a trap and a snare to the inhabitant[s] of Jerusalem"). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **LXX (Isa 8:14):** Commonly attested as *kai ean ep' autō pepoithōs ēs, estai soi eis hagiasma, kai ouch hōs lithou proskommati synantēsesthe autō oude hōs petras ptōmati; ho de oikos Iakōb en pagidi kai en koilasmati enkathemenoi en Ierousalēm* ("and if you trust in him, he will be to you for a sanctuary, and **not** as a stone-stumbling will you meet him, nor as a rock-fall..."). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Rom 9:33 & 1 Pet 2:8:** Both contain *lithon proskommatos kai petran skandalou* (accusative in Rom 9:33; nominative *lithos proskommatos kai petra skandalou* in 1 Pet 2:8). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (b) Linguistic & Grammatical Relationships
1. **Polarity reversal (MT positive predication vs. LXX negation):** MT states directly that YHWH will be both a sanctuary and a stone of striking / rock of stumbling. LXX inserts a conditional protasis (*ean ep' autō pepoithōs ēs*) and **negates** the stumbling-stone clauses (*ouch hōs... oude hōs...*). **Both Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:8 follow the MT against the LXX** by removing the negation and presenting the stone as an actual stone of stumbling. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
2. **Noun phrase alignment (*proskommatos* + *skandalou*):**
   - For MT *'eben negep* ("stone of striking/stumbling"), LXX has dative *lithou proskommati* ("stumbling of a stone"); NT has genitive *lithos proskommatos* ("stone of stumbling"), preserving LXX's *proskomma* root.
   - For MT *ṣûr mikšôl* ("rock of stumbling"), LXX has *petras ptōmati* ("falling/ruin of a rock"). Both Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:8 replace *ptōmati* with ***skandalou*** ("of offense / trap / stumbling-block"). Note that *skandalon* is a standard Greek rendering of *mikšôl* (and *môqēš* in v. 14b) in Jewish-Greek revisionist/recensional traditions (later associated with Aquila/Symmachus/Theodotion, or early Hebraizing revisions). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
3. **1 Pet 2:8b expansion:** *hoi proskoptousin tō logō apeithountes* ("who stumble, disobeying the word") echoes the cognate verb *proskoptō* from Isa 8:15 LXX (*kai poloi en autois proskopsousin*, "and many among them shall stumble"). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (c) Structural & Rhetorical Parallels
- In **Rom 9:33**, Isa 8:14 is fused with Isa 28:16 via the shared catchword *'eben* / *lithos* ("stone"). Notably, LXX Isa 8:14 begins *kai ean ep' autō pepoithōs ēs* ("if you trust in him"), providing a conceptual bridge to Isa 28:16's *ho pisteuōn ep' autō*.
- In **1 Pet 2:7–8**, the stone motif splits Israel/humanity into two responses: belief (v. 7a, *hymin oun hē timē tois pisteuousin*, echoing *entimon* from 2:6) vs. disobedience/stumbling (vv. 7b–8, linking Ps 118:22's rejected stone with Isa 8:14's stumbling stone).

#### (d) Physical Analogy
- **Isa 8:14** shifts from architectural construction (28:16) to hazards along a path and hunting traps: an unexpected rock jutting into a walker's path (*'eben negep* / *ṣûr mikšôl*) causing collision/tripping (*skandalon* originally denotes the trigger-stick of a trap, bridging *mikšôl* with *paḥ*/*môqēš* "trap/snare" in 8:14b).

#### (e) Interpretive Hypotheses
- **Why did LXX Isa 8:14 insert the negation?** Likely theological sensitivity: the translator softened the jarring idea that God Himself becomes a stumbling block to Israel, turning it into a pious promise for those who trust Him ("if you trust in him... you shall *not* encounter him as a stumbling stone").
- **Why do Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:8 agree verbatim on *lithos proskommatos kai petra skandalou* against standard LXX?** Either (1) Paul and/or a pre-Pauline Christian *testimonium* revised LXX Isa 8:14 toward the MT (*mikšôl* → *skandalou*, dropping the LXX negation), or (2) they drew on an existing Hebraizing Greek text (*kaige*-like revision) of Isaiah.

### 3.3 Psalm 118:22 (LXX Psalm 117:22)

#### (a) Putative Textual / Original-Language Evidence `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **MT (Ps 118:22):** Commonly attested as *'eben mā'ăsû habbônîm hāyətāh lərō'š pinnāh*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **LXX (Ps 117:22):** Commonly attested as *lithon hon apedokimasan hoi oikodomountes, houtos egenēthē eis kephalēn gōnias*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **1 Pet 2:7:** *Lithon hon apedokimasan hoi oikodomountes, houtos egenēthē eis kephalēn gōnias*. (Verbatim match with **Matt 21:42**, **Mark 12:10**, **Luke 20:17**, and LXX Ps 117:22). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **1 Pet 2:4 (allusion/splice):** *lithon zōnta, hypo anthrōpōn men apodedokimasmenon, para de theō eklekton entimon*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Acts 4:11 (adapted citation):** *houtos estin ho lithos ho exouthenētheis hyph' hymōn tōn oikodomōn, ho genomenos eis kephalēn gōnias*. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (b) Linguistic & Grammatical Relationships
1. **MT–LXX isomorphism:** Unlike Isa 28:16 and Isa 8:14, LXX Ps 117:22 exhibits no major semantic divergence from MT Ps 118:22. LXX preserves the Hebrew casus pendens syntax (*'eben* → accusative *lithon* attracted/governed by *apedokimasan*, resumed by *houtos*—though MT uses a feminine verb *hāyətāh* agreeing with fem. *'eben*, whereas Greek *lithos* is masculine, hence *houtos*). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
2. **NT verbal alignment:**
   - **1 Pet 2:7** and the **Synoptics** adopt LXX Ps 117:22 verbatim, including the Hebraistic *ginomai eis* (*egenēthē eis* = *hāyətāh lə-*) and *kephalēn gōnias* (*rō'š pinnāh*).
   - **1 Pet 2:4** transforms the finite verb *apedokimasan* ("they rejected") into a perfect passive participle *apodedokimasmenon* ("having been rejected") paired with *hypo anthrōpōn* ("by humans") and contrasted with Isa 28:16's *eklekton entimon* ("chosen, precious").
   - **Acts 4:11** substitutes *exouthenētheis* ("despised / treated as nothing") for *apedokimasan* and converts *hoi oikodomountes* ("the builders," participle) into *hyph' hymōn tōn oikodomōn* ("by you, the builders," 2nd-person noun phrase).

#### (c) Structural & Rhetorical Parallels
- **1 Pet 2:4–8** frames the entire *lithos* catena by first introducing a synthetic *composite* thesis in 2:4 (combining Ps 118:22's "rejected stone" with Isa 28:16's "chosen, precious stone"), then citing Isa 28:16 (v. 6), Ps 118:22 (v. 7), and Isa 8:14 (v. 8) in sequence, bound together by the catchword *lithos* and the *timē*/*entimon*/*apisteousin*/*proskoptousin* wordplay.
- **Luke 20:17–18** (and the textually disputed **Matt 21:44**) immediately follows the Ps 118:22 citation with a judgment saying about falling on the stone and being crushed (*ho pesōn ep' ekeinon ton lithon synthlasthēsetai*), rhetorically bridging Ps 118:22 with Isa 8:14–15 (and Dan 2:34–35, 44–45). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (d) Physical Analogy
- The concrete analogy is masonry selection on a construction site: skilled masons (*habbônîm* / *hoi oikodomountes*) inspect cut blocks, discard an irregular or seemingly unsuitable stone (*mā'ăsû* / *apedokimasan*), yet that exact discarded block is elevated to the structural apex or primary corner-binding position (*rō'š pinnāh* / *kephalēn gōnias*). (Architectural debate continues on whether *rō'š pinnāh* denotes a foundational cornerstone or a crowning keystone/capstone.)

#### (e) Interpretive Hypotheses
- **Why does Acts 4:11 use *exouthenētheis* instead of *apedokimasan*?** Either (1) Luke/Peter paraphrases freely in oral sermonic style to heighten the indictment (*hyph' hymōn*), or (2) it reflects an independent Greek rendering of *mā'ăsû* (or *bāzû*) prior to LXX standardization in the book of Acts—though Luke 20:17 already uses *apedokimasan*, favoring rhetorical/sermonic adaptation in Acts 4:11.

### 3.4 Habakkuk 2:4 (with 2:3b)

#### (a) Putative Textual / Original-Language Evidence `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **MT (Hab 2:3b–4):** Commonly attested as:
  - v. 3b: *'im yitmahmāh ḥakkēh lô kî bō' yābō' lō' yə'aḥēr* ("if it tarries, wait for it; for it will surely come, it will not delay").
  - v. 4a: *hinnēh 'uppəlāh lō' yāšərāh napšô bô* ("behold, puffed up / swollen—his soul is not upright in him").
  - v. 4b: *wəṣaddîq be'ĕmûnātô yihyeh* ("and/but the righteous one by his faithfulness/faith shall live"). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **LXX (Hab 2:3b–4):** Commonly attested as:
  - v. 3b: *ean hysterēsē, hypomeinon auton, hoti erchomenos hēxei kai ou mē chronisē*.
  - v. 4a: *ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou en autō*.
  - v. 4b: *ho de dikaios ek pisteōs mou zēsetai* (with a major internal LXX variant placing *mou* after *dikaios*: *ho de dikaios mou ek pisteōs zēsetai*, e.g. in Alexandrinus/certain witnesses vs. Vaticanus *ek pisteōs mou*—exact MS distribution requires Göttingen XII Minor Prophets verification). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Hebrews 10:37–38:** Commonly attested as:
  - v. 37: *eti gar mikron hoson hoson, ho erchomenos hēxei kai ou chronisei* (opening phrase *mikron hoson hoson* drawn from **LXX Isa 26:20**).
  - v. 38: *ho de dikaios mou ek pisteōs zēsetai, kai ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou en autō* (note: some NT witnesses omit *mou* after *dikaios*, likely under Pauline influence, requiring NA28 verification). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
- **Romans 1:17 & Galatians 3:11:** Both read *ho [de] dikaios ek pisteōs zēsetai* (omitting both *mou* and *autou*). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (b) Linguistic & Grammatical Relationships
1. **Personification of the "vision" (v. 3b):** In MT, *bō' yābō'* refers back to *ḥāzôn* ("vision," grammatical masc., "it will surely come"). LXX renders *erchomenos hēxei* ("coming, he/it will come") and *hypomeinon auton* ("wait for him/it"). **Hebrews 10:37** adds the definite article ***ho*** (*ho erchomenos*, "**the** Coming One"), turning an ambiguous Hebrew/Greek participle into an explicit messianic title (cf. Matt 11:3 / Luke 7:19), and shifts LXX's *ou mē chronisē* (subjunctive) to *ou chronisei* (future indicative). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
2. **Complete divergence of v. 4a (MT vs. LXX/Hebrews):** MT's obscure *'uppəlāh* ("puffed up / swollen" or "presumptuous") and *lō' yāšərāh napšô bô* ("his soul is not upright in him") become in LXX *ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou en autō* ("if he shrinks back, **my** soul has no pleasure in him"). **Hebrews 10:38b follows LXX against MT verbatim** in this entire clause. `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`
3. **Pronoun shift on *'ĕmûnāh* / *pistis* (v. 4b):**
   - **MT:** *be'ĕmûnātô* — "by **his** faithfulness/steadfastness" (3ms suffix referring to *ṣaddîq*).
   - **LXX:** Shifts 3ms suffix to 1cs ***mou*** ("my"): either *ek pisteōs **mou*** ("by **my** faithfulness"—God's faithfulness) or *ho de dikaios **mou** ek pisteōs* ("**my** righteous one by faith").
   - **Hebrews 10:38a:** Follows the *ho de dikaios **mou** ek pisteōs zēsetai* LXX variant (against MT's *'ô* and against the B-text *ek pisteōs mou*).
   - **Romans 1:17 & Galatians 3:11:** Omit the possessive pronoun altogether (*ek pisteōs*, neither "his" nor "my"). `[UNVERIFIED—memory]`

#### (c) Structural & Rhetorical Parallels
- **Splicing in Heb 10:37:** The author of Hebrews prefaces Hab 2:3b with *eti gar mikron hoson hoson* from **LXX Isa 26:20** (*mikron hoson hoson* — "a very little while," originally urging God's people to hide briefly until divine wrath passes). This creates an eschatological urgency framing the Habakkuk citation.
- **Clause inversion in Heb 10:38:** Both MT and LXX place the negative warning first (2:4a) and the promise of life to the righteous second (2:4b). **Hebrews inverts them** (2:4b first, 2:4a second), so that the warning against shrinking back (*hyposteilētai*) stands immediately adjacent to v. 39's pastoral contrast (*hypostolēs* vs. *pisteōs*), bridging directly into the faith-roll of Hebrews 11.

#### (d) Physical Analogy
- **Hab 2:3–4** draws on watchtower/courier and military/traveler imagery (cf. Hab 2:1, standing on the watchpost): waiting patiently for an expected arrival that seems delayed (*yitmahmāh* / *hysterēsē*), contrasted with someone who either puffs up/falters (MT) or recoils/retreats under pressure (*hyposteilētai*, LXX/Hebrews—literally pulling back sails or drawing back in cowardice).

#### (e) Interpretive Hypotheses
- **Why did LXX Hab 2:4a read *ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou*?** Hypotheses include: (1) a different Hebrew *Vorlage* or vocalization (e.g., reading *'ulləpāh* / *'lp* "faint/draw back" instead of *'uppəlāh*, and *napšî* "my soul" instead of *napšô* "his soul", possibly due to *waw*/*yod* confusion); (2) deliberate theological re-reading in Hellenistic Judaism turning a cryptic line about a puffed-up enemy into a warning against apostasy/retreat under persecution.
- **Why does Paul omit *mou* in Rom 1:17 and Gal 3:11?** Either Paul follows a pre-existing Greek text lacking *mou*, or he deliberately drops the possessive pronoun to make *ek pisteōs* ("by faith") syntactically open to his theological contrast between *ek pisteōs* and *ex ergōn nomou* (Gal 3:11–12), whereas *mou* in LXX B (*ek pisteōs mou*, "by my [God's] faithfulness") would have obscured human faith as the contrasting principle.

## 4. Synthesis: Modes of NT Textual Engagement

1. **Following LXX against MT:**
   - **Isa 28:16 in Rom 9:33 & 1 Pet 2:6:** Adopts LXX's *kataischyn-* ("be put to shame") rather than MT's *lō' yāḥîš* ("not make haste/panic"), plus *ep' autō*.
   - **Hab 2:3b–4 in Heb 10:37–38:** Adopts LXX's masculine *erchomenos*, LXX's complete divergence in 2:4a (*ean hyposteilētai, ouk eudokei hē psychē mou en autō*), and LXX's 1st-person *mou* (*ho de dikaios mou*) against MT's *'uppəlāh... napšô* and *be'ĕmûnātô*.
2. **Following MT (or Hebraizing Greek) against LXX:**
