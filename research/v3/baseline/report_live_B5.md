### Register D: Interpretive Hypotheses & Conceptual Analogies

1. **Internalized Sodom (Axis A Hypothesis):** Judges 19 functions literarily as an indictment showing that pre-monarchic Israel ("no king in Israel," Judg 19:1) has become Sodom *without external angels to rescue the righteous or destroy the wicked*. Where Sodom's judgment came cleanly from heaven, Gibeah's crime fractures Israel from within (`Deut 13:13` *ḥērem* logic turned against Benjamin).
2. **Recapitulation of Israel and Moses (Axis B Hypothesis):** By bridging **Deut 6–8** (40-year Israel probation) with **Exod 34:28 / Deut 9:9, 18** (40-day Sinai fast without bread or water), Matthew 4 portrays Jesus simultaneously as **faithful Israel** passing the three wilderness trials Israel failed (manna/hunger, Massah/testing, Canaanite idolatry) and as **new Moses** receiving covenant fidelity on the mountain without the people's golden-calf apostasy (Exod 32–34). *(Note: 1 Kings 19:4–8—Elijah's 40-day wilderness journey sustained by bread and water—provides a classic prophetic parallel, though not returned by MCP's strict lexical intersection; see Section 4.)*
3. **Cross-Axis Conceptual Contrast (`עָנָה` `H6031`):** Across the two assigned research pairs, the single Hebrew root `עָנָה` marks opposite poles of "testing/affliction": in **Judges 19:24**, human depravity says `וְעַנּוּ אוֹתָם` (destructive humiliation of the vulnerable), whereas in **Deuteronomy 8:2–3**, divine pedagogy uses `עַנֹּתְךָ` / `וַיְעַנְּךָ` paired with `נִסָּה` (formative humbling that exposes the heart and produces obedience).
